# Crypto / Stablecoin / Digital Asset Policy Grey Areas for Startups (2025 – Oct 2026)

Research date: 2026-10-09. Method: web search (several primary/law-firm domains such as sec.gov, lw.com, orrick.com, whitecase.com, sullcrom.com and theblock.co were egress-blocked for full-text fetch, so many findings come from search-result summaries of those pages; treat specific numbers as needing primary-source confirmation). ## 1. GENIUS Act (stablecoins): implementing rules, yield/rewards, state vs federal paths, effective dates

### Takeaway
The GENIUS Act became law in July 2025, but as of Oct 2026 its operative regime is **not yet in force**. Agencies missed the statutory July 18, 2026 rulemaking deadline. Only proposals plus one Treasury interim final rule exist, and the effective date floats between "120 days after final rules" and Jan 18, 2027. Startups therefore operate in a limbo: the statute is enacted, the rules are proposed, and the issuer licensing pathway is not yet open.

### Cited Findings
- **Effective date mechanics:** the main operating framework takes effect on the earlier of **Jan 18, 2027** (18 months after enactment) or **120 days after primary federal regulators issue final implementing rules**. Regulators were directed to finalize rules by **July 18, 2026**. — [GovernanceDocs, "GENIUS Act Effective Date: The Complete 2027 Timeline"](https://governancedocs.com/genius-act-effective-date/); [CryptoSlate GENIUS Act timeline](https://cryptoslate.com/crypto-laws/genius-act/)
- **Missed deadline:** the July 18, 2026 deadline passed without an OCC final rule. The OCC has said it is targeting **November 2026** for its final rule. — [PYMNTS, "OCC Promises Final Rule for GENIUS Act by November" (2026)](https://www.pymnts.com/legal/2026/occ-promises-final-rule-for-genius-act-by-november/); [BingX flash news](https://bingx.com/en/flash-news/post/occ-targets-november-for-final-genius-act-payment-stablecoin-rule-after-july-deadline-passed)
- **OCC NPRM:**
  - Released **Feb 25, 2026** (376 pages) and published in the Federal Register **Mar 2, 2026**. Comments were due **May 1, 2026**.
  - Covers licensing, reserves, capital and redemption.
  - BSA/sanctions requirements are deferred to a separate rulemaking with Treasury.
  - Sources: [Federal Register 2026-04089](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/03/02/2026-04089/implementing-the-guiding-and-establishing-national-innovation-for-us-stablecoins-act-for-the); [OCC Bulletin 2026-3](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2026/bulletin-2026-3.html); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/occ-proposes-comprehensive-stablecoin-regulatory-framework-to-implement-the-genius-act/); [MoFo summary (Mar 6, 2026)](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260306-summary-and-analysis-of-the-occ-s-proposed-rule)
- **$5M capital floor (unconfirmed):** one investor guide reports a **$5 million minimum capital floor** for new federally approved issuers. This comes from a secondary source and was not confirmed against the rule text. — [Angel Investors Network (Jul 2026)](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/crypto-digital-assets/genius-act-stablecoin-final-rules-july-2026-accredited-investors)
- **FDIC NPRM** (Federal Register **Apr 10, 2026**):
  - Payment stablecoin issuers (PPSIs) would generally have to redeem within **two business days**.
  - Deposits held as reserves would **not** be pass-through insured to stablecoin holders.
  - Sources: [Federal Register 2026-06974](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/10/2026-06974/genius-act-requirements-and-standards-for-fdic-supervised-permitted-payment-stablecoin-issuers-and); [FDIC FIL](https://www.fdic.gov/news/financial-institution-letters/2026/notice-proposed-rulemaking-establish-genius-act)
- **Treasury NPRM (Apr 2026)** on §4(c) "substantially similar" principles for state regimes. Comments were due **June 2, 2026**. — [Treasury NPRM PDF](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/136/NPRM-GENIUS4c-Principles.pdf); [Consumer Finance Monitor (Apr 14, 2026)](https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2026/04/14/treasury-issues-nprm-on-state-oversight-of-stablecoin-issuers-under-the-genius-act/); [Alston & Bird (May 2026)](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2026/05/genius-act-substantially-similar-state-frameworks); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/kpmg-us/content/dam/kpmg/pdf/2026/genius-act-treasury-proposal-on-state-level-regulatory-regimes-reg-alert.pdf)
- **Treasury interim final rule (single source, unverified):** reportedly published **Sep 30, 2026** (FR 2026-19966) to set Stablecoin Certification Review Committee procedures, described as the first binding, effective-on-publication GENIUS rule. — [Yahoo Finance, "Treasury's First GENIUS Act Rule Draws a $10 Billion Line"](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/crypto/articles/treasury-first-genius-act-rule-073833593.html)
- **Federal Reserve:** the Fed had **not** issued its proposed rule as of mid-May 2026. — [CryptoTimes (May 18, 2026)](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/05/18/genius-act-10-months-stablecoin-rulemaking-federal-state-divide/)
- **Conflicting report on a joint August package:** one source claims the OCC, FDIC and Treasury published proposals on **Aug 18, 2026**, including a $10B threshold and AML obligations for all issuers. This conflicts with the Mar/Apr dates above and is unverified. — [Licentium (Aug 2026)](https://www.licentium.io/post/occ-fdic-treasury-propose-genius-act-stablecoin-implementing-rules-august-2026)
- **State vs federal path:**
  - Who can choose a state regime: issuers that are not insured-depository subsidiaries and have **< $10B** outstanding.
  - Condition: the state regime must be certified "substantially similar," and the Stablecoin Certification Review Committee must **unanimously** find that it "meets or exceeds" federal standards.
  - Issuers above $10B must transition to federal oversight. One source says they have **360 days** to do so or must stop issuing.
  - Sources: [CryptoTimes (May 18, 2026)](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/05/18/genius-act-10-months-stablecoin-rulemaking-federal-state-divide/); [Alston & Bird](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2026/05/genius-act-substantially-similar-state-frameworks)
- **Yield/rewards:**
  - GENIUS bars issuers from paying interest. The fight in 2026 was over whether **third parties (exchanges)** can pay "rewards" on stablecoins.
  - Bank trade groups pushed to close this; Coinbase resisted. The dispute moved into the market-structure bill (see §2).
  - The White House claimed stablecoin yield does not threaten deposits; bankers rebutted this in **Apr 2026**.
  - Sources: [Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/crypto-stablecoin-yield-tillis-alsobrooks-coinbase-bank-trade-groups-occ/819477/); [CoinDesk (Apr 13, 2026)](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/04/13/bankers-rebuff-white-house-claim-that-stablecoin-yield-doesn-t-threaten-deposits)

### Inferences
- Because CLARITY stalled (§2), the exchange-rewards question is **unresolved by statute**. Rewards paid by non-issuers remain a live grey zone governed only by GENIUS's issuer-level ban and whatever the final OCC/FDIC rules say about affiliates and "indirect" payments.
- The window between enactment and operative rules lets non-issuer startups and state-chartered issuers launch now. Two risks follow:
  - They may face transition and licensing costs once the rules land, likely in **early 2027**.
  - State regimes cannot be certified until the Committee process runs, which leaves a gap for state issuers.

- I could not confirm the Fed NPRM date.
- I could not confirm the precise Treasury IFR text or the Aug 18 package.
- I found no source on NYDFS's posture toward GENIUS "substantially similar" certification.

## 2. Market structure legislation (CLARITY Act / Senate bill) and SEC/CFTC split, status as of Oct 2026

### Takeaway
The House passed CLARITY in July 2025. The Senate Banking Committee advanced its version in May 2026, but **Senate cloture failed 49–50 on Sep 15, 2026**, so the bill is stalled. A motion to reconsider is pending, and a lame-duck revival is possible but uncertain. If nothing passes, it must be reintroduced in the 120th Congress (Jan 2027). Regulators (the SEC and CFTC) are now filling the gap by rulemaking.

### Cited Findings
- **House passage:** passed **Jul 17, 2025**, 294–134. — [bit.com CLARITY explainer](https://www.bit.com/knowledge-hub/clarity-act); [FinTech Weekly tracker](https://www.fintechweekly.com/news/what-is-the-clarity-act-digital-asset-market-structure-explained-2026)
- **Senate Banking Committee:** released a **309-page** substitute on **May 12, 2026** and advanced it **15–9 on May 14, 2026**. It was placed on the Senate calendar around **Jun 1, 2026**. — [Davis Wright Tremaine (May 2026)](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/financial-services-law-advisor/2026/05/senate-banking-crypto-market-structure-bill)
- **Tillis–Alsobrooks yield compromise (~May 1, 2026):**
  - Bans rewards that are "economically or functionally equivalent" to interest on a deposit, or paid "solely for holding" payment stablecoins.
  - Regulators would list permissible activity-based rewards, which could include governance, validation and staking.
  - Banks objected that "membership program" rewards are a loophole. Coinbase's Armstrong backed the deal.
  - Sources: [CoinDesk (May 1, 2026)](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/05/01/clarity-act-text-lets-crypto-firms-offer-stablecoin-rewards-while-shielding-bank-yield); [Forbes (May 1, 2026)](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jasonbrett/2026/05/01/tillis-alsobrooks-reach-compromise-on-stablecoin-yield-in-clarity-act/); [ABA Banking Journal (May 2026)](https://bankingjournal.aba.com/2026/05/report-senators-reach-deal-on-stablecoin-yield/); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/cryptocurrency/2026/crypto-firms-keep-stablecoin-rewards-under-senate-deal/)
- **Cloture vote (Sep 15, 2026):**
  - Cloture on the motion to proceed (H.R. 3633) **failed 49–50**, short of 60.
  - The reported sticking point was **ethics language** on officials' (including the President's) crypto holdings and income, not the SEC/CFTC framework.
  - Sources: [FinTech Weekly](https://www.fintechweekly.com/news/clarity-act-cloture-vote-fails-49-50-september-2026); [Orrick (Oct 2026)](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/10/The-CLARITY-Act-Stalls-in-the-Senate-Whats-Next-for-Digital-Asset-Regulation); [bit.com vote explainer](https://www.bit.com/knowledge-hub/clarity-act-senate-vote)
- **Conflict on the Republican defectors:** some reports list Collins, Hawley and Moran, with Tillis switching to "no" procedurally to enter a motion to reconsider. Others list four GOP no votes, including Tillis. The official roll call (reportedly Vote 234) should be checked. — [bit.com](https://www.bit.com/knowledge-hub/clarity-act-senate-vote); [FinTech Weekly](https://www.fintechweekly.com/news/clarity-act-cloture-vote-fails-49-50-september-2026)
- **Procedural status:** a motion to reconsider was filed, but no new vote has been scheduled. Senators point to the post-midterm (**Nov 3, 2026**) lame duck. Lummis reportedly called the bill dead, while trackers describe it as "stalled." — [FinTech Weekly tracker (updated Sep 26, 2026)](https://www.fintechweekly.com/news/what-is-the-clarity-act-digital-asset-market-structure-explained-2026); [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/the-clarity-act-stalled-cryptos-election-money-faces-a-senate-test)
- **CFTC responds with rulemaking (Oct 5, 2026):**
  - Issued an ANPRM on "Regulation CTX" and "Regulation CAM" for **leveraged/margined retail crypto** trading.
  - Spot-only platforms stay under state money-transmission rules plus CFTC anti-fraud authority.
  - Leveraged venues must register as a DCM or as a new, narrower "crypto asset market" (CAM).
  - Chair Selig said the CFTC **lacks authority to require spot crypto to trade on CFTC-registered platforms without Congress**.
  - Sources: [The Block (Oct 5, 2026)](https://www.theblock.co/news/markets/2026-10-05-cftc-rulemaking-leveraged-retail-crypto-trading-regulation-ctx-cam-417701); [Forbes (Oct 5, 2026)](https://www.forbes.com/sites/ninabambysheva/2026/10/05/cftc-moves-to-set-rules-for-leveraged-crypto-trading-in-the-us/); [Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/cftc-previews-regulation-ctx-and-cam-federal-charter-crypto-exchanges); [Unchained](https://unchainedcrypto.com/cftc-seeks-comment-on-first-crypto-rules-for-leveraged-trading-leaving-spot-markets-out/)

### Inferences
- With no statute, the SEC/CFTC split rests on **interpretation**: the Mar 2026 joint taxonomy plus proposed SEC rules. That is reversible by a future Commission, and it is weaker than statute in court.
- **Spot "digital commodity" exchanges have no federal registration path.** They remain state-licensed (money transmitter licenses / BitLicense), which is the core grey area for US exchange startups.

## 3. SEC posture: dropped cases, Crypto Task Force / Project Crypto, staff statements, token taxonomy, innovation exemption, tokenized securities; CFTC spot initiatives

### Takeaway
The SEC moved from enforcement to rulemaking in four steps:
1. 2025 dismissals plus staff statements (meme coins, protocol staking, mining).
2. A **Mar 17, 2026 joint SEC-CFTC interpretive release** with a five-category token taxonomy.
3. The proposed **"Regulation Crypto Assets"** (Aug 18, 2026), with a safe harbor and startup exemption.
4. An **innovation exemption order for tokenized NMS stock trading** (Sep 17, 2026).

Staff statements are non-binding, and the August rule is only proposed.

### Cited Findings
- **Coinbase dismissal (Feb 27, 2025):** the SEC formally abandoned its case against Coinbase. — [Cozen O'Connor SEC Crypto 2.0 roadmap](https://www.cozen.com/news-resources/news/2025/sec-crypto-2-0-roadmap-of-sec-developments-on-cryptocurrency-and-digital-assets-in-2025)
- **Meme coin statement (Feb 27, 2025):** the Division of Corporation Finance said trading of the described meme coins is not an offer or sale of securities. — [Cozen](https://www.cozen.com/news-resources/news/2025/sec-crypto-2-0-roadmap-of-sec-developments-on-cryptocurrency-and-digital-assets-in-2025)
- **Protocol staking statement (May 29, 2025):**
  - Covers protocol staking only. It does **not** address liquid staking or restaking, and it is not binding.
  - It came hours after the SEC dismissed the **Binance** case.
  - Commissioner Crenshaw dissented, noting it conflicts with prior court rulings in the SEC's favor.
  - Sources: [DLA Piper (Jun 2025)](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2025/06/sec-staff-concludes-protocol-staking-activities); [Securities Docket](https://securitiesdocket.beehiiv.com/p/sec-says-crypto-staking-doesn-t-violate-securities-law-afcf)
- **Enforcement volume:** from inauguration to Sep 2025 the SEC brought **91** enforcement suits, versus **126** in the same 2024 period. The SEC dropped its Gemini probe. The DOJ disbanded its National Cryptocurrency Enforcement Team. — [Katten](https://katten.com/state-regulators-poised-to-increase-enforcement-efforts-as-trump-administration-executes-deregulation-agenda); [Pillsbury](https://www.pillsburylaw.com/en/news-and-insights/trends-crypto-enforcement.html); [Securities Docket](https://securitiesdocket.beehiiv.com/p/the-sec-s-reverse-sweep-in-crypto-enforcement-rolls-on)
- **Joint SEC-CFTC interpretive release (Mar 17, 2026):**
  - **Digital commodities** (examples: BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP) fall under CFTC jurisdiction.
  - **Digital collectibles** are not securities, except fractionalized collectibles.
  - **Digital tools** are not securities, but may be sold via an investment contract.
  - **Stablecoins:** only qualifying payment stablecoins are statutorily excluded; others get a facts-and-circumstances analysis.
  - **Digital securities** (tokenized securities) remain fully subject to securities law.
  - Protocol mining, protocol staking, wrapping and qualifying airdrops do not require registration if conditions are met.
  - Investment contracts **can end**.
  - The effective date conflicts across sources: immediate versus **Mar 23, 2026** Federal Register publication.
  - Sources: [Ballard Spahr (Mar 2026)](https://www.ballardspahr.com/insights/alerts-and-articles/2026/03/sec-and-cftc-clarify-when-digital-assets-are-and-are-not-securities); [Willkie (May 2026)](https://www.willkie.com/publications/2026/05/crypto-coordination-takes-shape-an-in-depth-review-of-the-landmark-sec-and-cftc-joint-release); [Neal Gerber](https://www.nge.com/news-insights/publication/sec-and-cftc-issue-interpretive-guidance-clarifying-when-digital-assets-fall-outside-the-purview-of-securities-laws); [Free Writings (Mar 2026)](https://www.freewritings.law/2026/03/joint-interpretation-from-the-sec-and-cftc-on-certain-types-of-crypto-assets-and-certain-transactions-involving-crypto-assets/)
- **Proposed "Regulation Crypto Assets":**
  - Announced **Aug 18, 2026** and published in the Federal Register **Aug 21, 2026**. It codifies the Howey-based framework for non-security crypto assets sold under investment contracts.
  - Secondary sources report a **$5M startup exemption**, **$20M/$75M offering tiers**, a "Form TR" safe harbor and **state blue-sky preemption**. These figures were not verified against the rule text.
  - Sources: [Atkins statement, SEC.gov (Aug 18, 2026)](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/atkins-statement-regulation-crypto-assets-081826); [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/sec-proposes-regulation-crypto-assets-rulemaking); [Astraea Law founder's guide (Aug 2026)](https://astraea.law/insights/sec-innovation-exemption-founders-guide)
- **Innovation exemption order (Sep 17, 2026):**
  - Exempts venues trading **tokenized NMS stock** from the "exchange" definition.
  - Exempts certain AMM liquidity providers from the "dealer" definition.
  - Runs for **five years**.
  - Provides **no Investment Company Act relief**, so tokenized funds are excluded.
  - Earlier explainers describing an April "12–36 month sandbox" are outdated.
  - Sources: [The Block (Sep 17, 2026)](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-09-17-sec-releases-innovation-exemption-415324); [Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/sec-s-innovation-exemption-opens-path-tokenized-stock-trading); [S&C via Harvard Corp Gov (Sep 25, 2026)](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2026/09/25/sec-issues-innovation-exemption-for-tokenized-securities/)
- **CFTC:** see §2 (Regulation CTX/CAM ANPRM, Oct 5, 2026). A report says the CFTC is partnering with the SEC on rules after the CLARITY failure. — [cryptobreaking (low-quality source)](https://www.cryptobreaking.com/cftc-partners-with-sec-on/)

### Inferences
- The grey zones that remain:
  - Liquid staking and restaking, which the 2025 statement excluded. The March 2026 release's conditions should be checked.
  - Tokens still inside an "investment contract" before decentralization or the end of that contract.
  - Tokenized funds, which have no 1940 Act relief.
  - Anything relying on a proposed, not final, safe harbor.
- Interpretive releases and exemptive orders can be withdrawn by a future Commission without notice-and-comment, or with a short one. This is the main reversal risk.

- I could not retrieve the SEC primary text for the Reg Crypto Assets proposal, so the thresholds, comment deadline and dissents are unverified.
- I could not confirm the substance and date of the 2025 PoW mining staff statement.

## 4. Banking: withdrawal of crypto guidance, charters, debanking EO

### Takeaway
The federal banking agencies rescinded their 2022–23 crypto prior-approval regimes in **Mar–Apr 2025**. A **debanking EO (Aug 7, 2025)** ordered regulators to remove "reputational risk." The OCC has granted **conditional national trust charters** to at least 9 crypto firms (Dec 2025 – Apr 2026). The open questions are whether conditional approvals convert to final ones, Fed master-account access, and replacement interagency guidance.

### Cited Findings
- **OCC IL 1183 (Mar 7, 2025):** withdrew the 2020–21 letters' supervisory non-objection requirement and rescinded IL 1179. The OCC also withdrew from the 2023 joint crypto statements. — [Boies Schiller PDF](https://www.bsfllp.com/a/web/gS9BFcbtWsVq5LQ7qfZh4p/a8jt3T/20250325-occ-issuances-addressing-crypto-asset-activities-final.pdf); [Hunton](https://hunton.com/blockchain-legal-resource/federal-regulators-continue-crypto-rationalization)
- **FDIC FIL-7-2025 (Mar 28, 2025):** rescinded FIL-16-2022, so no prior FDIC approval is needed for permissible crypto activities. — [Simpson Thacher (Apr 28, 2025)](https://www.stblaw.com/about-us/publications/view/2025/04/28/federal-banking-agencies-take-steps-to-accommodate-banks-involvement-in-crypto-related-activities)
- **Federal Reserve (Apr 24, 2025):** withdrew its 2022 crypto advance-notification letter and its 2023 dollar-token non-objection letter. — [Arnold & Porter (Apr 2025)](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2025/04/fed-approach-to-bank-permissible-crypto-asset-activities)
- **Executive Order "Guaranteeing Fair Banking for All Americans" (Aug 7, 2025):**
  - Targets "politicized or unlawful debanking," including of lawful digital-asset businesses.
  - Directs regulators to remove reputational risk from supervision, review institutions, and remediate.
  - Sources: [DLA Piper (Aug 11, 2025)](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2025/08/executive-order-on-fair-banking); [Steptoe](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/president-trump-signs-executive-order-directing-review-of-alleged-debanking.html)
- **OCC conditional trust charters:**
  - **Dec 12, 2025:** Circle (First National Digital Currency Bank), Ripple, BitGo, Fidelity Digital Assets and Paxos.
  - **Feb 2026:** Bridge, Crypto.com and Protego. Crypto.com's charter covers custody only, with no deposits or lending.
  - **Apr 2, 2026:** Coinbase National Trust Co. (custody and staking).
  - Sources: [Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/occ-national-trust-bank-charter-approve-circle-paxos-ripple-bitgo-gould-crypto/807799/); [Banking Dive on Crypto.com](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/crypto-com-occ-conditional-approval-national-trust-bank-charter-circle-ripple-paxos-bridge/812925/); [Buchanan Ingersoll](https://www.bipc.com/occ-grants-conditional-approval-to-crypto-firms-for-national-trust-bank-charters); [FinTech Weekly](https://www.fintechweekly.com/news/occ-national-trust-bank-charter-crypto-fintech-2026)
- **Conflict on Circle final approval:** one report says Circle got final approval on **Jul 10, 2026**; others say most approvals remain conditional. — [crypto.news OCC explainer](https://crypto.news/us/occ/)

### Inferences
- Trust charters allow custody and fiduciary services but not deposits or lending. Charter scope ("permissible activities" of a national trust bank) has been contested by bank trade groups, and litigation or reversal risk remains.
- Withdrawn guidance has not yet been replaced with new interagency crypto risk guidance. Banks' risk appetite for serving crypto startups therefore still varies examiner by examiner.

### Gaps
- I found no source on Fed master-account grants to crypto firms in 2026.
- I could not find a final status for the Circle, Paxos and others' charters.
- I could not confirm whether replacement interagency guidance has issued.

## 5. DeFi, tokenized RWAs, crypto tax, MiCA

### Takeaway
DeFi front-ends were spared IRS broker reporting by the **CRA repeal (Apr 10, 2025)**, but users' tax obligations remain. Centralized brokers began issuing 1099-DA in 2026, with cost-basis reporting starting for 2026 transactions. Tokenized stock got a five-year SEC exemption, but tokenized funds did not. In the EU, the **MiCA transitional period ended Jul 1, 2026**, so unauthorized CASPs must have wound down.

### Cited Findings
- **DeFi broker rule repeal:** Congress nullified the IRS DeFi broker rule via the CRA (House vote 292–131), and the President signed it **Apr 10, 2025**. Non-custodial DeFi without fiat on/off ramps is exempt from 1099-DA and KYC collection. — [RSM (2025)](https://rsmus.com/insights/tax-alerts/2025/congress-nullifies-irs-crypto-reporting-regulations-for-defi-platforms); [Blockworks](https://blockworks.com/news/house-overturns-irs-broker-rule)
- **Centralized brokers:**
  - Issue Form 1099-DA in 2026 for transactions on or after Jan 1, 2025.
  - Cost-basis reporting starts for 2026 transactions.
  - DeFi users still bear their own reporting burden.
  - Sources: [RSM](https://rsmus.com/insights/tax-alerts/2025/congress-nullifies-irs-crypto-reporting-regulations-for-defi-platforms); [Bloomberg Tax](https://news.bloombergtax.com/daily-tax-report/congress-nixing-crypto-reporting-rule-doesnt-alter-compliance)
- **Tokenized RWAs:** the SEC innovation exemption (Sep 17, 2026) covers tokenized NMS stock trading venues and AMM LPs, but provides no 1940 Act relief for tokenized funds. The joint release treats "digital securities" as fully regulated. — [Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/sec-s-innovation-exemption-opens-path-tokenized-stock-trading); [Ballard Spahr](https://www.ballardspahr.com/insights/alerts-and-articles/2026/03/sec-and-cftc-clarify-when-digital-assets-are-and-are-not-securities)
- **MiCA:**
  - The CASP transitional period expired EU-wide on **Jul 1, 2026**. Some member states ended it earlier: the Netherlands, Finland, Latvia, Hungary and Slovenia on **Jun 30, 2025**, and Sweden on **Sep 30, 2025**.
  - A pending application is not authorization.
  - ESMA expected unauthorized CASPs to have executed wind-down plans by Jul 1, 2026.
  - Stablecoin (EMT/ART) rules have applied since **Jun 30, 2024**.
  - Sources: [ESMA statement (Apr 2026)](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-04/ESMA75-113276571-1679_Statement_on_the_end_of_transitional_periods_under_MiCA.pdf); [AMF](https://www.amf-france.org/en/news-publications/depth/mica); [Elliptic](https://www.elliptic.co/blog/the-end-of-micas-transitional-period)

### Inferences

## 6. What startups are doing and key risks (reversal, statute of limitations, state regulators)

### Takeaway
Startups are acting on the federal thaw:
- Seeking OCC trust charters.
- Launching stablecoins and rewards programs ahead of the GENIUS rules.
- Building tokenized-stock venues under the Sep 2026 exemption.
- Planning token launches around the proposed SEC safe harbor.

The binding constraints are state regulators and AGs (NY AG, California DFPI), the absence of a statute, and the reversibility of interpretive relief after the Nov 2026 midterms and the 2028 election.

### Inferences
- **Reversal risk:** most 2025–26 relief comes from staff statements, interpretive releases, exemptive orders and proposed rules. A future SEC or CFTC majority could withdraw it. Conduct undertaken now could be scrutinized later, subject to limitations periods.
- **Limitations periods (unsourced, needs a citation):** my understanding is that SEC civil penalties have a 5-year limit (28 U.S.C. §2462) and that disgorgement runs up to 10 years for scienter violations under the 2021 NDAA. Under that reading, 2025–26 conduct could still be actionable in 2029–2036.
- **State preemption gaps:** state blue-sky preemption exists only in the proposed SEC rule. GENIUS state certification is not yet operational. NY (BitLicense / AG Martin Act) remains the strictest chokepoint.
- **What could close the gaps:**
  1. A lame-duck or 2027 market-structure statute.
  2. Final GENIUS rules (OCC targeting Nov 2026) and the Fed NPRM.
  3. A final Reg Crypto Assets with preemption.
  4. CFTC CTX/CAM final rules.
  5. Interagency replacement banking guidance and master-account policy.
