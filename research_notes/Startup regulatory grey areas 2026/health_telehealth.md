# US Health / Telehealth Policy Grey Areas for Startups (2025 – Oct 2026)

Status reflects sources found as of 2026-10-09. Most sources are law-firm alerts, trade press, and SEC filings; FDA/Federal Register primary documents are cited where found. Single-source claims are flagged.

## 1. Compounded GLP-1s after the shortage ended (FDA enforcement, 503A/503B, telehealth marketing, DTC ad crackdown, TrumpRx)

### Takeaway
The shortage-era "copy" window has closed. FDA has moved from tolerance to repeated waves of warning letters (Sept 2025, Feb/Mar 2026, June 2026) and a proposal (Apr 30, 2026) to keep semaglutide, tirzepatide and liraglutide off the 503B bulks list. The 503A "patient-specific clinical difference" pathway is still formally open, though, and that is the main grey area. Enforcement so far has focused on marketing and labeling claims. FDA has not yet tested whether documented "personalized" 503A compounding is lawful at scale. Large players (Hims & Hers) are moving to branded drugs at cash prices, helped by TrumpRx and manufacturer DTC pricing.

### Cited Findings
**Shortage end and the 503A/503B structure**
- Once the shortages ended (2025), a compounded GLP-1 may not be made as an "essentially a copy" of an approved drug unless the prescriber documents a clinically significant difference for that patient. FDA's own April 2026 guidance still describes individualized compounding for a documented clinical reason as permitted. — [Weight Loss Rankings / secondary](https://www.weightlossrankings.org/research/compounded-tirzepatide-current-fda-status); [Health Law Alliance](https://www.healthlawalliance.com/blog/tirzepatide-compounding-enforcement)
- Apr 30, 2026: FDA proposed excluding semaglutide, tirzepatide and liraglutide from the 503B bulks list, finding no clinical need for outsourcing facilities to compound them from bulk. Sources disagree on the comment deadline (June 29, June 30, or July 30, 2026 after an extension). No final action was found as of Oct 2026. — [FDA press release](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-proposes-exclude-semaglutide-tirzepatide-and-liraglutide-503b-bulks-list); [Orrick, May 2026](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/05/FDA-Moves-to-Shut-the-Door-on-Large-Scale-Compounding-of-GLP1-Drugs); [Pharmacy Times](https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/fda-moves-to-permanently-close-the-door-on-compounded-glp-1s)
- 503A pharmacies do not rely on the 503B bulks list, so the 503B exclusion has no independent legal effect on 503A operations. — [Metabolic Ledger timeline / secondary](https://metabolicledger.com/regulatory/fda-compounding-cliff); [Trycora](https://www.trycora.io/blog/is-compounded-semaglutide-legal-2026/)
- Outsourcing Facilities Association (OFA) litigation: OFA lost at the district court in March 2025 and appealed to the Fifth Circuit. One source alone reports that on Aug 27, 2026 the Fifth Circuit upheld FDA's shortage-end decisions without deciding anything about patient-specific 503A compounding. This is unverified. — [Remevi Health (single source)](https://remevihealth.com/is-compounded-glp1-legal/)
- Eli Lilly has sued compounders and telehealth platforms over marketing tirzepatide as "personalized" medicine. — [Health Law Alliance](https://www.healthlawalliance.com/blog/tirzepatide-compounding-enforcement)

**Warning letters and the DTC ad crackdown**
- Sept 9, 2025: HHS/FDA announced "thousands" of letters to pharma companies to remove misleading ads and ~100 cease-and-desist letters. Telehealth companies, pharmacies and compounders were among the recipients. — [FDA press release](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-launches-crackdown-deceptive-drug-advertising); [Holland & Knight, Sept 2025](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/09/fda-hhs-taking-action-against-telehealths-compounded-drug-advertising); [Latham](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/fda-begins-crackdown-on-direct-to-consumer-pharmaceutical-advertising)
- Telehealth intermediaries had largely sat outside FDA ad enforcement and are now direct targets. FDA reportedly uses AI surveillance tools and flagged "attention-grabbing visuals" and implied off-label uses. — [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/fda-takes-aim-drug-ads-what-it-means-compounding-pharmacies-medspas-and-telehealth)
- Sept 2025 wave: more than 50 letters to compounders and manufacturers (tracker count). — [GLPOneRx tracker (secondary)](https://glponerx.com/journal/fda-glp1-warning-letters-enforcement-2026); [Fierce Pharma](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/fda-ramps-crackdown-glp-1-drug-compounders-fresh-batch-30-warning-letters)
- Mar 3, 2026 (one source says February): 30 warning letters to telehealth companies marketing compounded GLP-1s. The main violations were (a) claims implying sameness with FDA-approved products and (b) branding compounded drugs with the telehealth firm's own name or trademark, which hides who actually made them. — [Foley](https://www.foley.com/p/102mmr0/glp1-compliance-fda-targets-telehealth-marketing-in-30-new-warning-letters/); [Venable, Mar 2026](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2026/03/fdas-latest-glp-1-crackdown-what-compounders); [McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/fda-takes-aim-at-misleading-online-claims-for-compounded-glp-1-drugs/)
- Week of June 15, 2026: 25 more warning letters to telehealth firms citing misbranding under FDCA §502(a) and §502(n). That makes at least 55 telehealth letters in 2026. — [RxIndex tracker (secondary)](https://therxindex.com/research/compounded-glp1-enforcement-tracker/); [Sheppard Mullin](https://www.sheppard.com/insights/blogs/fdas-focus-returns-to-compounding-and-telehealth-another-wave-of-warning-letters)
- Warning letters are "informal and advisory," not final enforcement actions. One firm speculates that the synchronized letters suggest FDA/DOJ may prepare civil actions; no such filing was confirmed. — [Health Data Consortium](https://healthdataconsortium.org/fda-telehealth-warning-letters-2026/); [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/united-states/food-drugs-healthcare-life-sciences/fda-releases-enforcement-letters-related-to-dtc-advertising)
- July 2026: FDA proposed eliminating the "adequate provision" option for broadcast Rx ads, which would force full risk disclosure in TV ads (RIN 0910-AJ14). — [Foley Hoag, July 2026](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/publications/alerts-and-updates/2026/july/fda-proposes-to-eliminate-adequate-provision-for-broadcast-prescription-drug-advertising/)

**Hims & Hers / Novo**
- Hims announced a $49/month compounded oral semaglutide pill, undercutting Novo's Wegovy pill. After FDA said it would act against mass-marketers of unapproved compounded GLP-1s, Hims said by Feb 7, 2026 it would stop offering the pill. — [Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/health-news/hims-hers-compounded-wegovy-pill-novo-nordisk-lawsuit); [HMP Global](https://www.hmpgloballearningnetwork.com/site/pln/news/novo-nordisk-sues-hims-hers-fda-moves-restrict-compounded-glp-1-drugs)
- Feb 9, 2026: Novo sued Hims in D. Del. for patent infringement, seeking an injunction until the patent expires on Dec 5, 2031. Mar 9, 2026: Novo dismissed without prejudice after Hims agreed to stop promoting compounded GLP-1s and to offer branded Ozempic/Wegovy at self-pay prices. Novo reserved the right to refile. — [Hims 10-Q Q1 2026 (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001773751/000177375126000076/hims-20260331.htm); [HMP Global](https://www.hmpgloballearningnetwork.com/site/pln/news/novo-nordisk-drops-patent-suit-hims-hers-shifts-fda-approved-semaglutide-products)

**TrumpRx / DTC pricing**
- Nov 2025 MFN deals set cash GLP-1 prices via TrumpRx starting at ~$350/month, falling toward $250 over two years. Oral starting doses would be $150/month if approved for obesity. — [Conference Board](https://www.conference-board.org/research/CED-Newsletters-Alerts/administration-strikes-deal-on-glp1-drug-pricing-and-coverage); [AMCP](https://www.amcp.org/letters-statements-analysis/federal-update-trump-administration-announces-deal-bring-most-favored-nation-pricing-glp-1s)
- TrumpRx launched in February 2026, with initial demand concentrated in GLP-1s (per GoodRx SEC filings). KFF notes that TrumpRx does not itself dispense drugs: for most drugs it links to manufacturer coupons and sites. AJMC warns that cash-pay can disadvantage insured patients. — [GoodRx 10-Q (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001809519/000180951926000152/gdrx-20260630.htm); [KFF](https://www.kff.org/patient-consumer-protections/trumprx-whats-the-value-for-customers/); [AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/trumprx-launch-brings-savings-and-uncertainty)

### Inferences
- **Grey area:** "personalized" 503A compounding (dose tweaks, added B12, etc.) is legally permitted only with a genuine, documented patient-specific clinical difference. Mass-marketed "personalized" programs sit in the gap between that statutory standard and FDA's so-far marketing-only enforcement. The legal risk is shifting to the marketer (the telehealth brand) under misbranding provisions, plus private patent and Lanham Act suits from Novo and Lilly.
- Cheaper branded cash prices (TrumpRx, manufacturer DTC at roughly $150–$350) erode the economic case for compounding. That may do more to close the gap than enforcement does. Startups are pivoting to "platform plus branded cash-pay" models (the Hims model).
- What could close the gap: finalizing the 503B exclusion; FDA guidance or enforcement defining "clinical difference" for 503A; state board of pharmacy action; DOJ civil cases; further price drops in branded drugs.

### Gaps
- No primary-source confirmation of the reported Aug 27, 2026 Fifth Circuit OFA ruling.
- Final status of the 503B bulks exclusion as of Oct 2026 is unknown; no final notice was found.
- No confirmed DOJ or state AG civil actions against telehealth GLP-1 marketers were found.
- Could not fetch the Venable alert (egress blocked); its details are taken from search summaries.

## 2. DEA telemedicine controlled-substance flexibilities and Medicare telehealth waivers — status Oct 2026

### Takeaway
DEA/HHS COVID-era tele-prescribing flexibilities run through Dec 31, 2026 under a fourth temporary extension. A final Special Registration rule went to OIRA on Aug 25, 2026 and had not been published as of mid-September 2026. Startups therefore face a hard year-end cliff with unknown final rules. Medicare telehealth waivers lapsed briefly (Jan 30 – Feb 3, 2026) and were then extended retroactively through Dec 31, 2027.

### Cited Findings
- Fourth temporary extension (Federal Register, Dec 31, 2025): DEA-registered practitioners may prescribe Schedule II–V drugs via audio-video telemedicine without a prior in-person evaluation through Dec 31, 2026. Audio-only is allowed for Schedule III–V OUD drugs. — [Federal Register 2025-24123](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/12/31/2025-24123/fourth-temporary-extension-of-covid-19-telemedicine-flexibilities-for-prescription-of-controlled); [Holland & Knight, Jan 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/01/dea-and-hhs-extend-telemedicine-prescribing-flexibilities-through-2026); [McDermott+](https://www.mcdermottplus.com/insights/dea-extends-telemedicine-flexibilities-for-controlled-substance-prescribing-for-2026/)
- The Jan 17, 2025 Special Registration NPRM proposed three registrations, including a Telemedicine Prescribing Registration for certain Schedule III–V drugs and an Advanced Telemedicine Prescribing Registration (for Schedule II), plus limited state registrations. It drew more than 6,400 comments. — [Federal Register 2025-01099](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/17/2025-01099/special-registrations-for-telemedicine-and-limited-state-telemedicine-registrations); [Jones Day, Feb 2025](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2025/02/dea-releases-rules-on-telemedicine-prescribing-of-controlled-substances-proposes-special-registrations)
- The final Special Registration rule was sent to OIRA on Aug 25, 2026. Its text is not public. The Unified Agenda targets November 2026 for final action. Coverage around Sept 18, 2026 still described it as pending review. — [McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/dea-appears-close-to-finalizing-telemedicine-special-registration/); [NASCSA](https://nascsa.org/dea-gets-closer-to-finalizing-telemedicine-special-registration/); [Telehealth.org](https://telehealth.org/news/dea-telemedicine-special-registration-rule-enters-final-review/)
- Medicare: the waivers expired Jan 30, 2026. H.R. 7148 (Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026), signed Feb 3, 2026, extended them retroactively through Dec 31, 2027. The extension covers home as an originating site, geographic waivers, the behavioral-health in-person requirement waiver, expanded practitioner types, and audio-only. — [ABA](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/health_law/news/2026/medicare-telehealth-flexibilities-extended-2027/); [Greenbaum](https://www.greenbaumlaw.com/news-insights/insights-alerts-medicare-telehealth-flexibilities-extended-through-2027-telehealth-gets-a-two-year-lifeline-in-fiscal-year-2026-spending-package); [Foley](https://www.foley.com/p/102memo/medicare-telehealth-flexibilities-countdown-to-january-30-2026-and-what-comes/)
- The Acute Hospital Care at Home waiver was reportedly proposed for extension to Sept 30, 2030 (Jan 20, 2026 congressional package). Its enactment was not confirmed. Permanent bills (CONNECT for Health Act, Telehealth Modernization Act) are pending. — [Telehealth.org](https://telehealth.org/news/federal-telehealth-policy-in-2026-what-the-medicare-extensions-mean/); [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/medicare-telehealth-flexibilities-extension-2027/)

### Inferences
- Tele-psychiatry, ADHD (Schedule II stimulants), testosterone (Schedule III) and weight-loss (phentermine) startups face a Dec 31, 2026 cliff. Their compliance posture depends on rule text nobody can see yet. Holding a DEA registration in every state where patients live, and adding in-person referral networks, are prudent hedges.
- Repeated one-year extensions plus a lapse-and-retro-fix in Medicare create planning uncertainty rather than lasting legal clarity. What closes the gap is either the final DEA rule or permanent legislation.

### Gaps
- Whether the final DEA rule was published between Sept 18 and Oct 9, 2026 is unconfirmed, as is whether a fifth extension is being drafted.
- Enactment of the hospital-at-home extension is unconfirmed.

## 3. FDA posture on AI devices, CDS, wellness wearables, LDTs, peptides, psychedelics

### Takeaway
FDA has clearly loosened its posture through enforcement discretion rather than rule changes. The Jan 6, 2026 General Wellness and CDS guidance revisions bring wearables that estimate physiological parameters, and single-recommendation (including some AI) CDS, outside active oversight. The LDT rule is dead. Peptides moved partly out of the "Category 2" prohibition, and psychedelics got fast-track vouchers. Because most of this relief is discretionary guidance with undefined terms, it is reversible and ambiguous.

### Cited Findings
**Wellness wearables / Whoop**
- July 2025: FDA issued a warning letter to WHOOP over its Blood Pressure Insights feature. — [PMC commentary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12822547/)
- Jan 6, 2026: the revised General Wellness guidance expressly covers wearables. Non-invasive products that estimate blood pressure, SpO2, glucose or HRV fall under enforcement discretion if their outputs are for wellness only. Claims of clinical accuracy, diagnosis, or prompting a medical intervention push a product back into device territory. — [Cooley, Jan 20, 2026](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-20-fda-opens-aperture-for-wearables-in-latest-general-wellness-guidance); [Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/fda-exempts-wearable-ai-features-guidance/809102/)
- Jan 23, 2026: separate draft guidance on evidence expectations for cuffless BP devices regulated as medical devices. June 23, 2026: FDA closed the WHOOP warning letter after WHOOP modified the feature and labeling. — [Hardian Health](https://www.hardianhealth.com/insights/fda-wearables-guidelines-update-2026); [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/01/07/whoop-fda-2026-guidance-wellness-win/)
- June 2026: a Nature Biomedical Engineering comment criticized the guidance for leaving validation standards and definitional coherence unaddressed. Commentators call the line "hard to figure out." — [Recatools summary](https://recatools.com/news/researchers-push-back-on-the-fdas-2026-wellness-device-guidance-what-it-means-for-wearable-owners/); [Blythe Karow](https://blythekarow.substack.com/p/whoop-won-but-good-luck-figuring)

**CDS / AI**
- The Jan 2026 CDS guidance extends enforcement discretion to CDS that gives a single recommendation where only one is "clinically appropriate." FDA did not change its statutory interpretation, and the guidance does not define "clinically appropriate." It applies to some AI/genAI features if clinicians can independently review the basis. Sources conflict on the dates (Jan 6 vs Jan 29 vs Mar 11, 2026). — [Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/01/5-key-takeaways-from-fdas-revised-clinical-decision-support-cds-software-guidance); [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/insights/2026/01/fda-eases-oversight-for-ai-enabled-clinical-decision-support-software-and-wearables)
- Nov 6, 2025: the Digital Health Advisory Committee met on generative-AI mental health devices. As of late 2025, FDA had authorized more than 1,200 AI devices, none generative-AI for a clinical purpose and none for mental health. FDA said it would prioritize enforcement toward higher-risk uses. The August 2025 PCCP guidance for AI-enabled device software was reiterated. — [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/fdas-digital-health-advisory-committee-weighs-guardrails-for-generative-ai-in-mental-health-devices); [Sidley, Nov 2025](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2025/11/us-fda-and-cms-actions-on-generative-ai-enabled-mental-health-devices-yield-insights-across-ai)
- CDRH has proposed no genAI-specific policy (MedTech Dive). — [MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/fda-seeks-feedback-on-generative-ai-regulations/828274/)

**LDTs**
- Mar 31, 2025: E.D. Tex. (ACLA v. FDA; AMP v. FDA) vacated the LDT final rule in full, holding that FDA lacks authority to regulate LDTs as devices. FDA did not appeal. FDA subsequently issued a notice to rescind the rule (the exact date was not confirmed in these results; reportedly September 2025). — [Baker Donelson](https://www.bakerdonelson.com/federal-court-vacates-ldt-final-rule); [FDA Law Blog, Apr 2025](https://www.thefdalawblog.com/2025/04/federal-district-court-vacates-fdas-laboratory-developed-tests-final-rule/); [Duane Morris](https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/fda_issues_notice_intent_rescind_rule_extending_authority_laboratory_developed_tests_0825.html)

**Peptides**
- Apr 15, 2026: per Kennedy, 12 peptides (including BPC-157, TB-500 and CJC-1295) were removed from Category 2 after their nominations were withdrawn. Removal is not approval: they are not FDA-approved, not lawful supplement ingredients, and not affirmatively cleared for 503A compounding. A PCAC meeting was scheduled for July 23–24, 2026 to consider BPC-157 for the 503A bulks list; its outcome was not found. — [DJ Holt Law](https://djholtlaw.com/regulatory-alert-the-legal-status-of-bpc-157-in-compounding-and-clinical-practice/); [BSCG](https://www.bscg.org/blogs/single/whats-changing-with-peptide-regulation-in-2026); [Texas Orthobiologics](https://www.texasorthobiologics.com/blog/the-truth-about-peptides-compounding-pharmacies-and-fda-regulation/)

**Psychedelics**
- Apr 18, 2026: an executive order prioritized psychedelic treatments. FDA granted national priority vouchers to Compass Pathways and Usona (psilocybin) and Transcend (methylone, for PTSD). The order directs FDA and DEA to create a Right to Try pathway, including for ibogaine compounds. Psilocybin and MDMA remain Schedule I. Compass expects to complete its NDA in Q4 2026. — [CNN, Apr 24, 2026](https://www.cnn.com/2026/04/24/health/fda-psychedelic-drugs-priority-vouchers); [Foley, Apr 2026](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/04/psychedelics-and-the-executive-order-from-schedule-i-to-treatment-priority/); [Penn LDI](https://ldi.upenn.edu/our-work/research-updates/trumps-executive-order-gives-psychedelic-therapy-a-boost/)
- July 14, 2026: Federal Register notice of a public hearing (Sept 14, 2026) on the future therapeutic use of psychedelics. — [Federal Register 2026-14155](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/14/2026-14155/considerations-for-potential-future-therapeutic-use-of-psychedelic-drugs-public-hearing-request-for)

### Inferences
- Wearables, longevity and "AI copilot" startups are using the wellness and single-recommendation carve-outs. The risk is that the line depends on claims and marketing copy, and that enforcement discretion can be withdrawn without notice-and-comment. Clear statutory change, final guidance with definitions, or adverse-event-driven enforcement would close the gap.
- Peptide clinics and telehealth "peptide stacks" operate in limbo: no longer Category 2, but not lawful to compound either. A PCAC or bulks-list decision would resolve it.
- Psychedelic clinics still face Schedule I. Right to Try and state programs (e.g., Texas ibogaine) are the near-term access routes; DEA rescheduling after any approval would close the gap.

### Gaps
- Outcome of the July 2026 PCAC meeting on BPC-157 and of the Sept 14, 2026 psychedelics hearing.
- Exact date and text of the LDT rescission rule.
- Any 2026 FDA genAI device guidance; none was found.

## 4. HHS/FDA deregulation under RFK Jr. and state AI-in-healthcare laws

### Takeaway
At the federal level the pattern is deregulation through guidance and enforcement discretion (wellness, CDS, LDTs, peptides, psychedelics), combined with aggressive marketing and advertising enforcement (DTC ads, GLP-1 telehealth). States are filling the AI gap with bans on AI therapy. Illinois (Aug 2025) and Nevada (July 2025) were first, followed by several 2026 laws. The result is a patchwork that national AI mental-health startups must geofence.

### Cited Findings
- May 2025: HHS/FDA issued an RFI on outdated regulations under a January 2025 executive order setting a 10-for-1 deregulation rule. — [America's Essential Hospitals](https://essentialhospitals.org/federal-agencies-issue-deregulation-digital-health-rfis/)
- July 2025: Kennedy told Congress he aimed to make psychedelics available for hard-to-treat conditions within a year. — [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/04/24/health/fda-psychedelic-drugs-priority-vouchers)
- Illinois WOPR Act, signed Aug 1, 2025: bars providing or advertising therapy unless a licensed professional delivers it. Enforced by IDFPR, with civil penalties up to $10,000 per violation. — [Health Policy Ohio, Oct 2025](https://www.healthpolicyohio.org/health-policy-news/2025/10/03/states-struggle-to-regulate-ai-chatbots-for-mental-health-therapy-amid-rising-need-for-care); [Quartz](https://qz.com/state-laws-restricting-ai-mental-health-care-guide-072826)
- Nevada AB 406, signed June 5, 2025 and effective July 1, 2025: bars AI systems designed to provide professional mental or behavioral health care and bars representing AI as able to do so. Penalties up to $15,000. Administrative AI use by providers is allowed. — [Quartz](https://qz.com/state-laws-restricting-ai-mental-health-care-guide-072826)
- 2026: trackers disagree on the count. One says that as of July 20, 2026 four states ban AI therapy (IL, NV, RI, ME). Becker's/Transparency Coalition counts five new 2026 laws. Another lists CO, ME, RI, TN and VT, with Colorado HB 1195 effective Aug 12, 2026 and Maine LD 2082 effective July 29, 2026. Utah takes a disclosure and privacy approach rather than a ban. There is no federal law. — [Becker's](https://www.beckersbehavioralhealth.com/ai-2/5-states-restrict-ai-therapy-chatbots-in-2026/); [AIMOCS tracker](https://aimocs.com/guides/us-state-laws-on-ai-mental-health-chatbots-tracker); [Psychology.com map](https://psychology.com/ai-therapy/state-bans)

### Inferences
- The key ambiguity is where "wellness/companion/coaching" chatbots end and "therapy" begins. Startups are rebranding as "support" or "coaching," adding licensed-clinician-in-the-loop models, disclosing that the user is talking to AI, and geoblocking ban states. FDA's posture toward genAI mental-health devices remains unsettled, so neither federal clearance nor federal preemption is available yet.
- Possible closers: an FDA genAI device framework, a federal preemptive AI statute (stalled), state AG enforcement test cases, and harmonized state definitions.

### Gaps
- No 2026 HHS-wide AI strategy or deregulation document was found in this search; the federal deregulation evidence here is mostly 2025.
- No enforcement actions under the Illinois or Nevada laws were found.
- The exact 2026 state list conflicts across trackers; statute texts should be verified.
