# AI Regulation Changes (2025 – Oct 2026) That Leave Legal Grey Areas for Startups

Research date: 2026-10-09. Method: web search (several fetches were blocked by the egress proxy, so many claims rest on search-result excerpts of law-firm alerts and trackers rather than full-text reads; these are marked below). ## 1. US federal: executive orders, preemption push, DOJ task force, FTC (status as of Oct 2026)

### Takeaway
The administration's tools are an EO (Dec 11, 2025), DOJ litigation, funding conditions and FTC policy statements, but **Congress has not enacted preemption** as of Oct 2026. State AI laws therefore stay legally operative while under federal attack. That leaves startups with dual exposure and a moving target, not a single federal standard.

### Cited Findings
- **Jan 2025:** Trump revoked Biden's EO 14110 and issued "Removing Barriers to American Leadership in AI," which directs agencies to prioritize innovation and cut regulatory burden. — [BakerHostetler](https://www.bakerlaw.com/insights/navigating-the-emerging-federal-state-ai-showdown-doj-establishes-ai-litigation-task-force/)
- **July 2025:** The AI Action Plan directed the FTC to review its final orders and set aside any that "unduly burden AI innovation." — [Wiggin and Dana](https://www.wiggin.com/publication/ftc-sets-aside-rytr-llc-final-order-citing-the-trump-administrations-ai-action-plan/); [Covington Global Policy Watch, Jan 2026](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/01/ftc-sets-aside-rytr-final-order-pursuant-to-white-house-ai-action-plan/)
- **Mid-2025, Congress:** A 10-year state-AI-law moratorium in the 2025 reconciliation bill was stripped after a 99-1 Senate vote. A later attempt to add one to the NDAA also failed. — [StateScoop](https://statescoop.com/state-ai-law-moratorium-omitted-2026-defense-bill-trump-eo/); [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/proposed-federal-moratorium-on-us-state-level-ai-regulation-passes-house-committee); a House preemption bill exists as H.R.5388, the "American AI Leadership and Uniformity Act" — [Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/5388/text)
- **Dec 11, 2025:** EO 14365, "Ensuring a National Policy Framework for AI," was signed. It:
  - orders the AG to create an AI Litigation Task Force within 30 days to challenge state AI laws on dormant Commerce Clause, preemption or other grounds
  - orders Commerce to publish, within 90 days, an evaluation that names "onerous" state laws
  - conditions federal grant funding
  - carves out child safety, compute and data-center infrastructure, and state procurement laws from the preemption push
  - [White House EO text](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/12/eliminating-state-law-obstruction-of-national-artificial-intelligence-policy/); [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/ai-executive-order-targets-state-laws-and-seeks-uniform-federal-standards)
- After the Commerce evaluation, the FCC is to start a proceeding on a federal AI reporting and disclosure standard that would preempt state rules. — [Baker Donelson](https://www.bakerdonelson.com/emerging-federal-ai-policy-what-to-know-and-how-to-prepare)
- **BEAD broadband funding is the lever:** Paul Hastings reports Commerce is directed to condition $42B in BEAD funds on states dropping "onerous" AI rules. Other coverage cites up to $21B, so the figure is contested. — [Paul Hastings](https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/client-alerts/president-trump-signs-executive-order-challenging-state-ai-laws); [Mintz, Feb 13, 2026](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/54731/2026-02-13-emerging-federal-ai-strategy-ftc-sets-aside-rytr)
- **Jan 9–10, 2026:** AG Bondi memo established the AI Litigation Task Force. — [DOJ memo](https://www.justice.gov/ag/media/1422986/dl?inline); [Baker Botts](https://ourtake.bakerbotts.com/post/102me4r/inside-the-dojs-new-ai-litigation-task-force)
- **Mar 11, 2026:** The Commerce "onerous laws" evaluation was due. As of a late-April 2026 Jenner alert (via search excerpt), no list had been publicly released. I found no later confirmation that it has been published. — [Jenner](https://www.jenner.com/en/news-insights/client-alerts/doj-joins-xai-in-lawsuit-challenging-colorado-ai-act) (fetch blocked; summarized from search excerpt)
- **Mar 20, 2026:** The White House issued a non-binding legislative framework that puts federal preemption at its center. It creates no compliance obligations. — [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/03/white-house-ai-framework-puts-federal-preemption-at-the-center-of-the-debate)
- **Apr 9, 2026:** xAI sued Colorado over the Colorado AI Act in the District of Colorado, and DOJ later joined. Jenner says DOJ's claims include an Equal Protection theory. This is the first visible Task Force action. — [Jenner](https://www.jenner.com/en/news-insights/client-alerts/doj-joins-xai-in-lawsuit-challenging-colorado-ai-act) (search excerpt)
- **June 4, 2026:** A bipartisan draft (the "Great American AI Act") proposed **three-year** preemption of state laws. — [Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/06/04/bipartisan-ai-draft-proposes-three-year-preemption-of-state-laws/)
- **Late July 2026, per a secondary tracker:** The draft was reworked as the "FRONTIER Act," with narrower preemption limited to frontier-developer catastrophic-risk transparency, third-party audits and incident reporting. The tracker also says Speaker Johnson is skeptical of Congress "leading." I could not verify the bill's stage. — CASRAI tracker (fetch blocked; via search summary): casrai.org/news/federal-ai-moratorium-state-preemption-fight-2026
- **Oct 7, 2026:** Commentary notes preemption still unresolved. — [Regulatory Review](https://www.theregreview.org/2026/10/07/knapp-the-hidden-costs-of-ai-preemption/)
- **White & Case on legal limits:** Preemption normally flows from statute, so EO 14365 "would likely not independently displace state AI laws." — [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/state-ai-laws-under-federal-scrutiny-key-takeaways-executive-order-establishing)
- **FTC posture:**
  - **Dec 22, 2025:** The FTC (Ferguson and Meador, the only two sitting commissioners) set aside the 2024 Rytr consent order over AI-generated fake reviews. It cited the Action Plan and found no cognizable Section 5 violation. — [Wiggin and Dana](https://www.wiggin.com/publication/ftc-sets-aside-rytr-llc-final-order-citing-the-trump-administrations-ai-action-plan/); [Frankfurt Kurnit](https://technologylaw.fkks.com/post/102lz3c/no-more-rytrs-block-ftc-reverses-course-on-ai-enforcement)
  - **July 2026:** The FTC proposed a "Policy Statement Concerning the Suppression of Accuracy in AI Systems," published in the Federal Register on July 7, 2026. It targets companies that steer outputs against consumer expectations of objectivity. It also asserts that state laws requiring "alterations to the accurate outputs" of models can conflict with the FTC Act, which reads as a preemption hook against state algorithmic-discrimination laws. — [Federal Register 2026-13628](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/07/2026-13628/policy-statement-concerning-the-suppression-of-accuracy-in-artificial-intelligence-systems); [Holland & Knight, Aug 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/a-look-at-the-ftcs-current-approach-to-ai-regulation)

### Inferences
- The federal approach is deregulatory toward "innovation burdens" (Rytr) and aggressive toward state "bias" or "output-altering" mandates (the accuracy policy statement and DOJ v. Colorado). Startups get lighter federal consumer-protection risk on AI-washing-adjacent conduct. But they face a new theory, "ideological output steering as deception," whose limits are undefined.
- Until Congress acts, federal pressure works through litigation and funding conditions. A state law stays enforceable until a court enjoins it. Startups that skip state compliance on the bet that preemption is coming are taking a real legal risk.
- Any preemption that does pass looks increasingly **narrow** (frontier-developer duties only). Most startup-relevant state laws would likely survive it: chatbots, child safety, employment ADMT and deepfakes. Child safety is already carved out of EO 14365.

### Gaps
- Whether the Commerce "onerous laws" list was ever published, and whether the FCC proceeding has started (no source after April 2026).
- Current docket status of xAI/DOJ v. Colorado after Colorado's May 2026 rewrite (moot? amended complaint?). Sources conflict.
- Whether the FTC finalized the accuracy policy statement, and the status of the FTC's Sept 2025 6(b) inquiry into AI companion chatbots and kids (not surfaced in searches).
- Legislative stage of the FRONTIER Act. The only source is a secondary tracker.

## 2. State AI laws: Colorado, California, Texas, New York, Illinois, Utah, chatbot/companion laws

### Takeaway
State law is the binding layer in 2026, and it is fragmenting by use case: frontier transparency in CA and NY, ADMT/discrimination in CO, companion chatbots in CA and NY, therapy bans in IL and NV, disclosure in UT and TX. Effective dates keep slipping and texts keep being rewritten. That creates "regulated-but-not-yet-effective" windows that startups are operating in.

### Cited Findings
- **Colorado:**
  - **Aug 2025:** SB 25B-004 delayed the Colorado AI Act (SB 24-205) from Feb 1, 2026 to June 30, 2026 after compromise talks failed in a special session. — [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/enforcement-of-colorado-ai-act-delayed-until-june-2026); [Troutman, Aug 2025](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2025/08/colorado-ai-act-effective-date-delayed/); [Akin](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/ai-law-and-regulation-tracker/colorado-postpones-implementation-of-colorado-ai-act-sb-24-205)
  - **Mar–May 2026:** A Polis workgroup agreed a replacement framework in March. It was introduced as SB 26-189 on May 1, 2026, and the legislature passed it to "repeal and replace" the Act. — [Troutman, May 2026](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/05/colorado-legislature-passes-bill-to-repeal-and-replace-colorado-ai-act/) (headline only; fetch blocked)
  - A secondary guide says SB 26-189 was signed May 14, 2026. It reframes the law around "automated decision-making technology" instead of "high-risk AI systems," reportedly with a Jan 1, 2027 effective date. **The signing date and effective date are single-source and unverified.** — CASRAI guide (casrai.org/guides/colorado-ai-act-explained)
- **California:**
  - **SB 53 (Transparency in Frontier AI Act):** Signed Sept 2025 and effective Jan 1, 2026. It covers developers that train models above 10^26 FLOPs. "Large frontier developers" (>$500M revenue) must publish safety frameworks and report incidents within 15 days. Only the AG enforces, with penalties up to $1M per violation. — [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20251001-transparency-in-frontier-artificial-intelligence-act-sb-53-california-requires-new-standardized-ai-safety-disclosures); [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251001-california-enacts-ai-safety-transparency-regulation-tfaia-sb-53); [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-is-californias-ai-safety-law/)
  - **Unverified 2026 report:** California regulators sent a cybersecurity-risk subpoena to a leading developer, testing the gap between published frameworks and internal practice. — [ailearningguides (low-quality source)](https://ailearningguides.com/california-ai-cyber-risk-subpoena-sb-53-compliance/); see also [Jenner on CA enforcement steps](https://www.jenner.com/en/news-insights/client-alerts/california-continues-to-lead-on-ai-with-new-legislation-and-enforcement-steps)
  - **SB 243 (companion chatbots):** Signed Oct 13, 2025 and effective Jan 1, 2026. It requires:
    - disclosure that the user is talking to AI where a reasonable person could be misled
    - suicide and self-harm protocols
    - for known minors, a break reminder every 3 hours plus sexual-content limits
    - It also creates a **private right of action** for the greater of actual damages or $1,000 per violation, and annual reporting to the Office of Suicide Prevention from July 1, 2027.
    - [Jones Walker](https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/ai-regulatory-update-californias-sb-243-mandates-companion-ai-safety-and-accoun.html?id=102lq7c); [Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/chatbot-compliance-wave-californias-new-law-not-only-one-watch); [Orrick, Apr 2026](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/04/2026-State-Chatbot-Laws-Key-Provisions-and-Regulatory-Trends)
  - **Sept 2026 package:** Reportedly includes SB 1119 ("Adam's Law"), effective Jan 1, 2027, which adds age assurance and pre-launch child-safety risk assessments for chatbots. Secondary source only. — [getlimina](https://www.getlimina.ai/en/blog/california-sb-243-companion-chatbot-law)
  - California also enacted a law barring defendants from arguing that "the AI autonomously caused the harm." — [Baker McKenzie](https://connectontech.bakermckenzie.com/us-legal-accountability-for-ai-agents-when-ai-agents-act-who-is-responsible-under-us-laws/)
- **New York:**
  - **AI Companion Models law (GBL Art. 47):** In effect Nov 5, 2025. Enforcement is by the AG only, with no private right of action. — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=b96c2918-7314-445d-a324-65854b171cdd)
  - **RAISE Act:** Originally signed Dec 19, 2025. A chapter amendment signed Mar 27, 2026 aligned it with SB 53: the same 10^26 FLOP and $500M thresholds, penalties of $1M for a first violation and $3M thereafter, and a **72-hour** incident-reporting window (stricter than SB 53's 15 days). Effective **Jan 1, 2027**. — [Wiley](https://www.wiley.law/alert-New-York-Finalizes-RAISE-Act-for-Frontier-AI-Models-Law-Takes-Effect-January-1-2027); [MoFo, Apr 3, 2026](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260403-new-york-amends-the-raise-act-to-align-more-closely); [DWT, Apr 2026](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/artificial-intelligence-law-advisor/2026/04/ny-overhauls-frontier-ai-transparency-law); [Covington, Dec 2025](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2025/12/new-york-governor-signs-frontier-ai-safety-legislation/)
- **Texas TRAIGA (HB 149):**
  - In effect Jan 1, 2026. AG enforcement only, no private right of action, a 60-day cure period and a regulatory **sandbox**.
  - Separately, SB 1188 requires providers to disclose AI use in diagnosis and treatment.
  - Single compliance-tracker source; verify against the statute. — [livecompliance](https://livecompliance.com/learn/ai-healthcare-regulations/)
- **Illinois:**
  - **WOPR Act (HB 1806):** Effective Aug 1, 2025. It bans AI from providing therapy. Licensed professionals may use AI only for administrative and supplementary tasks. Advertising an "AI therapist" is itself prohibited. Penalties run up to $10,000 per violation, enforced by IDFPR. General wellness apps are reportedly outside scope.
  - AEI notes that **enforcement questions remain**.
  - [AEI](https://www.aei.org/technology-and-innovation/illinois-bans-ai-therapy-questions-about-enforcement-remain/); [ManageEngine](https://insights.manageengine.com/artificial-intelligence/illinois-ai-therapy-ban/)
  - Illinois also has an AI-in-employment disclosure law taking effect (HB 3773 amending the Human Rights Act, effective Jan 1, 2026). — [Seyfarth](https://www.seyfarth.com/news-insights/artificial-intelligence-legal-roundup-colorado-postpones-implementation-of-ai-law-as-california-finalizes-new-employment-discrimination-regulations-and-illinois-disclosure-law-set-to-take-effect.html)
- **Utah:** HB 452 (2025) takes a disclosure-first approach to mental-health chatbots: AI disclosure, a ban on selling or sharing identifiable health data, and advertising limits. The effective date is reported as May 7, 2025. — [Quartz](https://qz.com/state-laws-restricting-ai-mental-health-care-guide-072826); [Blueprint](https://www.blueprint.ai/blog/breaking-down-current-legislation-regulating-ai-in-mental-health-care)
- **States split three ways on AI mental health:** bans (IL, NV), disclosure (UT) and general licensing law (TX). A 2026 wave reportedly added therapy restrictions in CO, ME, RI, TN and VT (vendor-blog sourcing). — [Quartz](https://qz.com/state-laws-restricting-ai-mental-health-care-guide-072826); [aimocs tracker](https://aimocs.com/guides/us-state-laws-on-ai-mental-health-chatbots-tracker)

### Inferences
- **Definitional ambiguity is the core grey area:**
  - "Companion chatbot" in CA SB 243 vs. a general-purpose assistant
  - "therapy" vs. "wellness" in Illinois
  - "high-risk AI" vs. "ADMT" in Colorado
  - These boundaries decide whether a product is regulated at all. Startups are positioning themselves on the unregulated side through product framing ("coach," "wellness," "productivity assistant").
- The frontier laws (SB 53, RAISE) use 10^26 FLOP plus $500M thresholds, which in practice exempt nearly all startups as developers. Startups building on frontier APIs carry no direct duty, though downstream use-case laws still apply.
- The SB 243 private right of action ($1,000 per violation) is the single largest litigation exposure for consumer chatbot startups in 2026. Most other state AI laws are AG-only.
- Colorado's repeated rewrite (2024 → delay → May 2026 replacement) together with live DOJ litigation means companies cannot plan to a stable text. Many are waiting rather than building compliance programs.

### Gaps
- Final enacted text and effective date of Colorado SB 26-189, and whether it includes a private right of action or cure period. Primary sources were not reachable.
- Whether any state has actually brought an enforcement action under SB 243, the NY companion law, WOPR or TRAIGA. No reports found.
- Confirmation of California's Sept 2026 chatbot bills (SB 1119) from the legislature site.

## 3. Copyright and AI training: what was decided and what is still open

### Takeaway
District courts split their reasoning in 2025: Bartz says training is transformative but pirated acquisition is not protected, while Kadrey says training is transformative but market dilution could defeat fair use. Anthropic then paid $1.5B, with final approval in July 2026. The only appellate ruling, Thomson Reuters v. Ross (3d Cir., Sept 29, 2026), went **against** fair use, but its opinion is sealed and its facts are non-generative. Fair use for generative training remains unsettled at the appellate level as of Oct 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **Feb 11, 2025, D. Del.:** In *Thomson Reuters v. Ross*, Judge Bibas held that copying Westlaw headnotes to train a non-generative legal search tool was not fair use. — [Ballard Spahr, Oct 2026](https://www.ballardspahr.com/insights/alerts-and-articles/2026/10/third-circuit-addresses-fair-use-in-ai-training-but-leaves-generative-ai-questions-unresolved)
- **June 23, 2025, *Bartz v. Anthropic* (N.D. Cal., Alsup):** Training on books was "exceedingly transformative" fair use, and scanning lawfully purchased print books was also fair use. The pirated-library acquisition was left for trial. — [Jackson Walker](https://www.jw.com/news/insights-kadrey-meta-bartz-anthropic-ai-copyright/); [Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/client-alert/making-sense-of-the-first-two-often-contradictory-sometimes-confounding-gen-ai-fair-use-rulings)
- **June 25, 2025, *Kadrey v. Meta* (N.D. Cal., Chhabria):**
  - Training was "highly transformative," but the outcome should turn on market effect, including "market dilution."
  - Meta won partial summary judgment only because the plaintiffs failed to prove market harm. The ruling applies only to the 13 named plaintiffs.
  - The case remains in further pleading and discovery in 2026.
  - [Public Knowledge](https://publicknowledge.org/courts-agree-ai-training-ruled-as-fair-use-in-bartz-v-anthropic-and-kadrey-v-meta/); [Taft](https://www.taftlaw.com/news-events/law-bulletins/two-federal-judges-rule-in-separate-cases-that-training-ai-models-using-copyrighted-works-is-fair-use/); [Polsinelli](https://www.polsinelli.com/publications/ai-vs-authors-update-court-approves-historic-anthropic-settlement-while-meta-litigation-continues)
- **May 2025:** The Copyright Office issued a pre-publication Part 3 report on generative AI training, covering fair use, licensing and market dilution. No final version was found in searches. — [aivortex summary](https://www.aivortex.io/legal/guides/ai-copyright-training-data-2026-landscape/)
- ***Bartz* settlement: $1.5B, final approval July 2026.**
  - The fund covers 482,460 works. The judgment dismisses the case with prejudice, and all 53 objections were overruled.
  - Sources conflict on fees: $101.56M awarded vs. $122M including costs.
  - The release does **not** cover output claims, future conduct or works not on the list.
  - First payments are expected on or before Nov 15, 2026 per one source, but the date is unconfirmed.
  - Note: one tracker names Judge Martínez-Olguín as approving the settlement, while the 2025 rulings were Alsup's. A reassignment is plausible but unverified.
  - [Polsinelli](https://www.polsinelli.com/publications/ai-vs-authors-update-court-approves-historic-anthropic-settlement-while-meta-litigation-continues); [ailawsuittracker](https://ailawsuittracker.com/cases/bartz-v-anthropic/); [Authors Alliance FAQ](https://www.authorsalliance.org/resources/generative-ai/bartz-v-anthropic-settlement-faq/)
- **Sept 29, 2026, 3d Cir.:**
  - Affirmed: ROSS infringed 2,243 headnotes and the copying was not fair use. The court reportedly found harm to Westlaw's market **and to an emerging market for licensing training data**.
  - The reasoning is under seal. The public judgment is one page reading "AFFIRMED."
  - Commentators say it leaves the generative-AI questions unresolved. Authors Alliance criticizes it for conflating "competing users with competing uses."
  - [IPWatchdog, Sept 30, 2026](https://ipwatchdog.com/2026/09/30/third-circuit-affirms-revised-fair-use-ruling-against-ross-ai-legal-research-platform-in-sealed-opinion/); [Ballard Spahr](https://www.ballardspahr.com/insights/alerts-and-articles/2026/10/third-circuit-addresses-fair-use-in-ai-training-but-leaves-generative-ai-questions-unresolved); [Authors Alliance, Oct 2, 2026](https://www.authorsalliance.org/2026/10/02/third-circuit-affirms-in-thomson-reuters-v-ross-the-court-gets-merger-wrong-and-does-a-disservice-to-fair-use/); [Patently-O](https://patentlyo.com/patent/2026/10/ease-is-not-necessity-third-circuit-affirms-no-fair-use-in-thomson-reuters-v-ross.html)

### Inferences
- **Still unsettled:**
  - fair use for *generative* training at the appellate level (no 9th or 2nd Circuit ruling found)
  - market dilution as a cognizable harm
  - whether a "training-data licensing market" counts under factor four (Ross suggests yes, which is circular and favors rightsholders)
  - liability for outputs, which Bartz expressly left out of the release
  - liability for data acquired from shadow libraries or by scraping
- **Practical effect:** The $1.5B Bartz number, roughly $3,000 per work, sets a de facto price for using pirated data. Startups are shifting toward licensed and synthetic data and documenting provenance. Fine-tuning startups whose products compete with their source data (legal research, as in Ross) face the clearest risk.

### Gaps
- Final status of the Copyright Office Part 3 report.
- Other major pending cases (NYT v. OpenAI, Getty v. Stability in the US, music-label suits) were not researched in this pass.
- The Ross opinion's reasoning is sealed, so its exact holding cannot be verified.

## 4. EU AI Act (GPAI, Digital Omnibus, codes of practice) and the UK approach

### Takeaway
GPAI obligations applied from Aug 2, 2025, and AI Office **enforcement powers and fines began Aug 2, 2026** for models placed on the market after Aug 2025. The Digital Omnibus (agreed May 7, 2026; adopted June 2026) pushed high-risk obligations to Dec 2027 and Aug 2028. The UK still has no AI bill and deferred copyright reform in March 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **GPAI Code of Practice and signatories:**
  - The Commission published the GPAI Code of Practice in July 2025, and it was endorsed ahead of the Aug 2, 2025 GPAI start date. — [Jones Day, Aug 2025](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2025/08/eu-ai-act-european-commission-publishes-generalpurpose-ai-code-of-practice); [Law Society Gazette, July 2025](https://www.lawsociety.ie/gazette/top-stories/2025/july/eus-ai-guide-leaves-key-questions-unresolved/)
  - Reported signatories include OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft, Google and Mistral. Meta declined on July 18, 2025, and xAI signed only the safety and security chapter.
  - Signing creates a presumption-like evidence of compliance and mitigates fines, but it does not exempt anyone from the Act.
  - Secondary source. — [CSA Labs, Aug 3, 2026](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-gpai-enforcement-20260803-csa-st/)
- **GPAI enforcement:**
  - From Aug 2, 2026 the AI Office can request documentation, evaluate models, order corrective measures, restrict or withdraw models, and fine up to €15M or 3% of global turnover.
  - Fines for models placed on the market before Aug 2, 2025 apply only from Aug 2, 2027.
  - It is **unclear** whether models first placed after Aug 2025 but derived from older models fall under the 2026 or the 2027 date.
  - [CSA Labs](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-gpai-enforcement-20260803-csa-st/); [pagecrawl](https://pagecrawl.io/blog/eu-ai-act-august-2026-gpai-enforcement-monitoring)
- **Digital Omnibus on AI:**
  - Provisional agreement May 7, 2026; Parliament approval June 16, 2026 (423-57-174); final Council adoption June 29, 2026.
  - One source says it was published as Reg. (EU) 2026/1744 and in force from July 27, 2026 (unverified).
  - **New dates:** Annex III high-risk systems move to Dec 2, 2027; Annex I product-embedded systems to Aug 2, 2028; Art. 50(2) generative-output marking/watermarking to Dec 2, 2026.
  - It also adds a new prohibition on AI that generates non-consensual intimate imagery or CSAM without safeguards.
  - Reportedly, it did not change the GPAI Chapter V timeline.

### Inferences
- The EU grey area for startups is the **18+ month gap** before high-risk obligations apply. HR, credit, education and biometrics tools can ship in the EU with only GPAI-downstream, Art. 5 prohibited-practice and Art. 50 transparency duties. Harmonised standards are still pending, which keeps the "how to comply" question open even after the deadlines.

- In the UK, the copyright status quo (no commercial TDM exception) plus no reform timeline means training in the UK remains legally risky. Startups are training elsewhere and serving the UK.

## 5. Where startups are exploiting gaps, the key risks, and what could close them

### Takeaway
The live grey zones are where regulation is use-case-specific, definitionally fuzzy, or enacted but not yet effective: AI companions, AI "wellness" and therapy, AI legal advice, agentic systems, voice cloning and digital replicas, and synthetic or licensed data. The closing mechanisms are SB 243-style private rights of action, test cases such as Nippon Life v. OpenAI, federal bills like NO FAKES, and the EU 2027–28 deadlines.

### Cited Findings
- **Companions:**
  - CA SB 243 (Jan 1, 2026) and NY GBL Art. 47 (Nov 5, 2025) apply. Many other states have chatbot disclosure bills pending, including CO, ME, TX and UT. — [Orrick, Apr 2026](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/04/2026-State-Chatbot-Laws-Key-Provisions-and-Regulatory-Trends); [Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/chatbot-compliance-wave-californias-new-law-not-only-one-watch); [BillTrack50](https://www.billtrack50.com/info/blog/regulating-ai-companions-before-they-raise-our-kids)

### Inferences
- **Patterns of gap exploitation:**
  - framing a product to fall outside definitions ("wellness coach," not therapist; "assistant," not companion; "information," not legal advice)
  - shipping during not-yet-effective windows (RAISE and Colorado in 2027; EU high-risk in Dec 2027 and Aug 2028; EU watermarking in Dec 2026)
  - choosing jurisdictions (avoiding IL and NV for therapy, training outside the UK)
  - relying on the federal deregulatory posture and the expectation of preemption
- **Biggest risks:**
  - SB 243 private suits and class actions over minors and self-harm
  - the UPL doctrine being applied to AI legal tools
  - copyright output claims, and data-acquisition claims after Bartz
  - state AG actions, which survive federal preemption efforts in the child-safety carve-out
  - EU GPAI fines from Aug 2026 for startups that become "providers" through substantial fine-tuning
- **What could close the gaps:**
  - a narrow federal preemption statute (FRONTIER Act-style), which would cover frontier duties only and leave most use-case laws standing
  - NO FAKES, which would set a federal replica standard and preempt the state patchwork
  - appellate fair-use rulings (Kadrey or other 9th Circuit cases)
  - final EU harmonised standards and Commission guidelines
  - court rulings in xAI/DOJ v. Colorado on dormant Commerce Clause theories
  - FTC finalization of the accuracy policy statement

### Gaps
- No systematic data found on how many startups use "wellness" or "coach" framing to avoid therapy bans. The claim is inferential.
- Synthetic-data-specific regulation was not found. There appears to be no dedicated law, only indirect copyright and privacy exposure.
- The TAKE IT DOWN Act (signed May 2025; platform takedown compliance by May 2026) was not confirmed in searches and should be verified separately.
- Status of OpenAI's response or motion in Nippon Life.
