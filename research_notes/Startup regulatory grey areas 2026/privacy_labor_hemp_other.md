# Startup Regulatory Grey Areas (2025 – Oct 2026): Privacy, Labor, Hemp/Cannabis, and Cross-Cutting Policy

_Research status as of 2026-10-09. Firecrawl search was rate-limited (HTTP 429), so I used WebSearch result summaries. Most citations are law-firm alerts and trade press; primary documents (Federal Register, court orders) are cited where the search turned them up. Items I could not verify are listed under Gaps._

## Data privacy and kids' online safety

### Takeaway
In 2025–26 US privacy law became more fragmented, not less. About 19 states now have comprehensive laws (three more took effect Jan 1, 2026). There are new kids' and age-verification mandates, and a 5th Circuit stay let Texas's app-store law take effect in mid-2026. The CCPA's risk-assessment and ADMT rules are now in force. The only federal fix, the GOP SECURE Data Act, would preempt state laws, but it lacks bipartisan support. In the EU, the Digital Omnibus would loosen the GDPR (AI training under legitimate interest, a narrower definition of "personal data"), but it is still only a proposal.

### Cited Findings
**State patchwork**
- Indiana, Kentucky and Rhode Island comprehensive privacy laws took effect **Jan 1, 2026**. Indiana largely tracks Virginia's VCDPA. Penalties are up to $7,500 per violation (IN, KY) and $10,000 (RI). IN and KY give a 30-day cure period; **RI has no cure period**. — [Koley Jessen](https://www.koleyjessen.com/insights/publications/new-state-privacy-laws-effective-january-1-2026-indiana-kentucky-and-rhode-island); [Hunton (Jan 2026)](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/new-u-s-state-privacy-social-media-and-ai-laws-take-effect-in-january-2026)
- Sources disagree on how many states have comprehensive laws in force: 18, 19, or "over 20," depending on how they count. — [TrustArc](https://trustarc.com/resource/new-in-2026-state-privacy-laws-in-indiana-kentucky-and-rhode-island/); [Baker Donelson](https://bakerdonelson.com/privacy-laws-ring-in-the-new-year-state-requirements-expand-across-the-us-in-2026)
- **CCPA regulations:** the CPPA's finalized rules on risk assessments, cybersecurity audits and ADMT took effect **Jan 1, 2026**, with obligations phased in through 2030. — [O'Melveny 2026 checklist](https://omm.com/insights/alerts-publications/2026-data-security-and-privacy-compliance-checklist-key-us-state-law-updates-ai-rules-coppa-changes-and-global-data-protection-risks); [AFS Law](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/privacy-counsel/new-year-new-privacy-obligations). The exact ADMT compliance date is reported inconsistently; see Gaps.

**Federal privacy bill**
- **SECURE Data Act (H.R. 8413)** was introduced **Apr 22, 2026** by Rep. John Joyce (R-PA). It came out of the E&C Privacy Working Group that Chair Guthrie set up in Feb 2025. It would **preempt all state privacy laws** that relate to the Act's provisions, making federal law a ceiling. Enforcement would be split between the FTC and state AGs, with **no private right of action**. — [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/secure-data-act-analysis-of-the-new-federal-privacy-bill); [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/springtime-in-washington-means-its-time-for-another-round-of-federal-privacy-legislation/)
- Analysts describe the bill as close to a lowest common denominator of state laws. Democrats say it would weaken stronger state protections, and unlike ADPPA and APRA it lacks broad bipartisan support. An E&C subcommittee held a hearing on **June 3, 2026**. It was introduced together with the GUARD Financial Data Act. — [JD Supra / Epstein Becker](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/one-nation-one-privacy-law-gop-6584212/); [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/springtime-in-washington-means-its-time-for-another-round-of-federal-privacy-legislation/)

**Kids / COPPA / age verification**
- **Amended COPPA Rule:** published in the Federal Register **Apr 22, 2025**, effective **June 23, 2025**, with full compliance due **Apr 22, 2026** (Safe Harbor disclosures were due Oct 22, 2025). "Personal information" now covers biometric identifiers (face templates, fingerprints, voiceprints). Operators must keep a written data-retention policy that names a time period. — [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule); [BBB National Programs](https://bbbprograms.org/media/insights/blog/coppa-amended); [Toy Association](https://www.toyassociation.org/ta/PressRoom2/News/2026-News/updated-coppa-rule-requirements-take-effect-april-22.aspx)
- **Texas App Store Accountability Act (SB 2420):** W.D. Tex. preliminarily enjoined it on **Dec 23, 2025**, applying strict scrutiny under the First Amendment, before its Jan 1, 2026 start. The **5th Circuit stayed the injunction (reported May 28, 2026)**, holding the Act likely survives intermediate scrutiny, so the law is **now in effect** while litigation continues. Plaintiffs said they would ask SCOTUS to vacate the stay. — [MoFo update](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251111-texas-targets-app-stores-with-new-accountability-law); [FPF comparison of enacted app-store acts](https://fpf.org/blog/comparing-enacted-app-store-accountability-acts/); [Pearl Cohen](https://www.pearlcohen.com/?p=18365). Sources differ slightly on the stay date (FPF says May 28; MoFo's alert is dated June 10).
- A second challenge, a student plaintiff case against Paxton, has its own preliminary-injunction filing dated Aug 2026. — [Courthouse News PDF](https://courthousenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/student-vs-paxton-preliminary-injunction.pdf)

**EU Digital Omnibus (GDPR changes)**
- The Commission proposed it on **Nov 19, 2025** (COM(2025) 837). It is still a **proposal**, not law. — [Bird & Bird](https://cm.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/introduction-to-the-the-european-commission’s-digital-omnibus-package); [Nemko](https://digital.nemko.com/news/eu-digital-omnibus-proposes-key-ai-act-gdpr-reforms)
- Main GDPR proposals:
  - a contextual or relative definition of "personal data"
  - legitimate interest as a legal basis for AI training
  - limits on abusive DSARs
  - breach notification moved from 72h to **96h**, and only for high-risk breaches
  - cookie rules moved into a new ePrivacy/GDPR provision

  — [Bright Defense](https://www.brightdefense.com/news/eu-digital-omnibus-seeks-96-hour-gdpr-breach-deadline/); [Council compromise text ST-10729-2026](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-10729-2026-INIT/en/pdf)
- **EDPB/EDPS joint opinion of Feb 11, 2026:** they firmly oppose the contextual definition of personal data and the softening of the automated-decision-making ban. They are mixed on AI legitimate interest and DSAR limits, and they support the 96h breach deadline. — [Innovires summary](https://www.innovires.com/en/blog/digital-omnibus-gdpr-edpb-edps-stanovishche-2-2026.html)
- On **Mar 26, 2026** the Parliament plenary adopted its position on the separate **AI** Omnibus (the IMCO/LIBE text) ahead of talks with Council. — [MediaLaws](https://www.medialaws.eu/the-eu-parliament-plenary-adopts-text-to-amend-the-digital-omnibus-on-ai-ahead-of-council-negotiations/)

### Inferences
- **Why it is ambiguous:** startups face roughly 19 different applicability thresholds, cure periods (some none, as in RI) and definitions of "sensitive data." Federal preemption could arrive and erase all of it, but it is unlikely before the 2026 midterms because the SECURE Data Act lacks Democratic support. Building to the strictest common denominator (CA plus CO/CT plus RI) is still the rational default.
- Age assurance is the sharpest grey area for consumer apps. Courts disagree on the level of scrutiny (W.D. Tex. applied strict scrutiny; the 5th Circuit applied intermediate scrutiny). That split tracks *FSC v. Paxton* (June 2025), which applied intermediate scrutiny to porn-site age verification. In the 5th Circuit, more age-gating laws are likely to survive. Developers on app stores in Texas (and Utah and Louisiana, per FPF's comparison) must now consume age signals from the app stores and handle parental consent. The industry response is age-signal APIs from Apple and Google and third-party age-assurance vendors.
- The EU Omnibus would legitimize AI training on personal data under legitimate interest, which matters a great deal to AI startups. Until trilogues conclude, no one can rely on it. DPAs (via the EDPB) are signaling resistance, so expect enforcement to follow current GDPR in the meantime.
- **What would close the gap:** a federal preemptive law; a SCOTUS ruling on app-store or social-media age-verification laws; adoption of the Omnibus (most likely 2027).

## Worker classification, labor and non-competes

### Takeaway
Federal labor policy is swinging back toward employers, but through proposals rather than final rules. DOL's proposal to restore the employer-friendly 2021-style independent-contractor test (Feb 2026) has not been finalized as far as I found. The NLRB regained a quorum, but its independence is legally weakened after *Trump v. Slaughter*. Portable-benefits safe-harbor laws are spreading state by state. The FTC's non-compete ban is dead, so non-compete law is now state-by-state plus case-by-case FTC enforcement.

### Cited Findings
- **DOL IC rule:** on **Feb 26, 2026**, WHD proposed rescinding the 2024 six-factor, totality-of-the-circumstances rule and returning to a modified 2021 framework. The proposal gives primary weight to two "core factors": **control** and **opportunity for profit or loss**. It would also apply the analysis to the FMLA and MSPA. Comments closed **Apr 28, 2026**. The proposal has no effect until a final rule is published, and timing is uncertain. — [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/03/dol-proposes-to-rescind-2024-independent-contractor-rule-and-reinstate-2021-framework); [Mayer Brown (Mar 2026)](https://www.mayerbrown.com/zh-hans/insights/publications/2026/03/dol-proposes-new-independent-contractor-rule-to-replace-biden-era-regulation); [J.J. Keller](https://www.jjkeller.com/news/article/U-S-DOL-proposes-to-rescind-Biden-era-2024-independent-contractor-rule_id-272f62bb-ac22-434a-e900-92afd3a135ba)
- **NLRB quorum:**
  - The Board lacked a quorum after Member Wilcox was removed in Jan 2025.
  - The Senate confirmed James Murphy and Scott Mayer in **Dec 2025**, which restored the quorum (alongside GC Crystal Carey).
  - The first decision under the new quorum was *Satellite Healthcare*, 374 NLRB No. 25 (**Jan 15, 2026**), which held that Regional Directors may act without a quorum.

  — [Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/new-year-new-board-nlrb-nominations-confirmed-board-regains-quorum); [Employers Council](https://members.employerscouncil.org/blogs/curtis-graves2/2026/01/21/nlrb-regains-quorum-after-year-of-paralysis)
- Prouty's term was due to expire **Aug 27, 2026**. The Senate confirmed James Macy (R, term to Aug 2030) and re-confirmed David Prouty (D, term to Aug 2031) in a 74-nominee package (51-47). One source says this secured a 3-1 Republican majority. — [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/nlrb-confirmations-secure-republican-majority/827418/); [CWC](https://cwc.org/CWC/Updates/2026/President-Trump-Sends-Bipartisan-NLRB-Nominees-To-Senate-As-August-Quorum-Deadline-Looms.aspx)
- **Trump v. Slaughter:** SCOTUS, 6-3, overruled *Humphrey's Executor*. One law firm reads the decision to make the NLRA's removal protections for Board members unconstitutional. — [Barnes & Thornburg](https://laborandemployment.btlaw.com/post/102n7k9/trump-v-slaughter-what-does-it-mean-for-the-nlrb). I could not confirm the decision date (see Gaps).
- **Portable benefits:**
  - Utah, Tennessee and Alabama enacted laws that let platforms contribute to workers' benefit accounts without those contributions counting toward employee status.
  - Georgia (HB 987, signed **May 2026**) followed.
  - Kansas (HB 2602) passed a similar bill in Mar 2026; I did not confirm a signature.
  - About a dozen more states introduced bills in H1 2026, and federal versions are pending.
  - Pennsylvania has no statute. DoorDash runs a company pilot there contributing 4% of eligible earnings.

  — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/daily-labor-report/doordash-backed-policy-on-driver-benefits-spreads-across-states); [Bloomberg Tax: Georgia](https://news.bloombergtax.com/payroll/georgia-enables-gig-worker-benefit-funds-without-status-change); [Bloomberg: Kansas](https://news.bgov.com/daily-labor-report/bill-passed-in-kansas-allows-gig-worker-benefit-contributions); [Beancount guide (Jul 2026, secondary)](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/10/portable-benefits-gig-workers-state-laws-2026-guide)
- **FTC non-compete rule:** on **Sept 5, 2025** the FTC voted 3-1 to dismiss its appeals in *Ryan LLC v. FTC* and *Properties of the Villages v. FTC* and accept the vacatur. It said it will pursue unlawful non-competes case by case under antitrust law. — [Repairer Driven News](https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2025/09/09/ftc-vacates-non-compete-clause-rule-and-dismisses-court-appeals/); [Paul Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/ftc-shifts-approach-to-non-compete-enforcement); [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/297790)

### Inferences
- **The core grey area:** the federal FLSA test is moving toward control plus profit/loss, but state tests (California's AB5 ABC test, and the ABC tests in MA and NJ) are unchanged. A contractor who passes the federal test can still be an employee in California. Startups using 1099 labor need to comply state by state.
- Portable-benefits statutes create a **safe harbor** in red states only. A platform that offers benefits nationally risks those contributions being treated as evidence of employment in states without such laws. Expect DoorDash, Uber and similar platforms to keep pushing model bills and a federal version.
- After *Slaughter*, the NLRB's direction can change with each administration and its precedents are unstable. Startups should expect a rollback of Biden-era doctrine (for example *Stericycle* handbook rules and *Cemex* bargaining orders), but timing is uncertain.
- Non-competes: with no federal ban, the rules are CA/MN/ND/OK bans plus a growing set of state income thresholds, and FTC case-by-case enforcement (especially against low-wage non-competes).
- **What would close the gap:** a final DOL IC rule (possible late 2026 or 2027, and it would be litigated); federal portable-benefits legislation; NLRB decisions overruling *Cemex* and *Stericycle*.

### Gaps
- I found no source confirming whether DOL published a **final** IC rule between May and Oct 2026.
- I could not confirm the *Trump v. Slaughter* decision date or the exact wording of its holding.
- I did not research California AB5 amendments or any 2025–26 gig-worker unionization law (e.g., California's AB 1340 for rideshare drivers, Massachusetts' 2024 ballot measure). Their current status is unverified.
- Tennessee's and Utah's enactment dates are not individually cited.

## Hemp and cannabis

### Takeaway
The FY2026 appropriations law (Nov 2025) redefines hemp, which bans most intoxicating hemp-derived THC (delta-8, THCA flower, and gummies and drinks over 0.4 mg THC per container). It was due to take effect Nov 12, 2026. A September 2026 stopgap CR (H.R. 6500, signed Sept 2) pushed the date to **Dec 11, 2026**, except for synthetic cannabinoids. The administration says there will be no more extensions. Meanwhile, marijuana itself has been only **partly** moved to Schedule III (medical and FDA-approved only, Apr 2026), and the broader rescheduling hearing awaits an ALJ recommendation.

### Cited Findings
- **Federal redefinition:** the FY2026 appropriations law resets federally legal hemp to **0.3% total THC** (closing the delta-9-only and THCA loophole), caps finished products at **0.4 mg total THC per container**, and bans synthesized cannabinoids such as delta-8. It was effective **Nov 2026**. — [MJBizDaily](https://mjbizdaily.com/ohio-will-allow-hemp-thc-drinks-amid-federal-ban-with-conditions/); [Brewbound](https://www.brewbound.com/news/intoxicating-hemp-regulations-take-shape-in-several-states)
- **Delay:** the Senate passed a short-term CR in Aug 2026. The House passed H.R. 6500 **370-48 on Sept 1, 2026**, and President Trump **signed it Sept 2, 2026**. It funds the government through **Dec 11, 2026** and delays the hemp ban to that date. — [The Hill](https://thehill.com/policy/healthcare/6064552-hemp-ban-delayed-house-funding-bill/); [Forbes (Sept 3, 2026)](https://www.forbes.com/sites/ajherrington/2026/09/03/trump-signs-bill-delaying-hemp-thc-ban-until-december/); [Bloomberg Government](https://news.bgov.com/bloomberg-government-news/hemp-ban-delay-passed-by-congress-after-blowback-from-hardliners)
- **Carve-out:** products with cannabinoids the plant cannot naturally produce (synthetics) still faced the original **Nov 12** date. The administration says **no further extensions**. As of Sept 30, 2026, none of the bills that would replace the ban with a regulatory framework had received a hearing or markup. — [Cannabis Law Blog](https://www.cannabis-law-blog.com/federal-intoxicating-hemp-ban-changes-pushed-back-until-december-11-2026/); [Hytiva](https://www.hytiva.com/news/congress-delays-federal-hemp-thc-ban-to-december-11-as-industry-presses-for-rules)
- **Competing bills:**
  - In Jan 2026, Sens. Klobuchar, Paul and Merkley introduced a bill to delay the ban two years from its Nov 13, 2026 date.
  - Other bipartisan bills would set up age-restricted, potency-capped regulation.
  - Sen. Ted Budd (R-NC) pushes to permanently close the "hemp loophole."

  — [Todd Harrison Substack (Jan 2026)](https://toddharrison.substack.com/p/january-hemp-round-up); [WLOS](https://wlos.com/news/local/north-carolina-senator-ted-budd-pushes-federal-ban-hemp-thc-congress-senate-house-loophole-products-marketing-bill-cannabis-intoxicating-republican). Sources give the original effective date as both Nov 12 and Nov 13.
- **States:**
  - **California AB 8** (signed Oct 2, 2025, effective Jan 1, 2026) bars hemp raw extract in food, drinks and supplements. It follows the Sept 2024 emergency ban.
  - **Ohio SB 56**, signed with partial vetoes, recriminalizes certain intoxicating hemp beverages as of **Mar 20, 2026**. Low-dose drinks have a runway to Dec 31, 2026; sources do not reconcile these dates.
  - **Texas SB 3**, a total THC ban, was pending the governor's decision in June 2025.

  — [Vicente LLP (Oct 2025)](https://vicentellp.com/insights/vicente-llp-hemp-law-and-policy-briefing-october-2025/); [Jackson Lewis via JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/ohio-recriminalizes-certain-9425812/); [IVN](https://ivn.us/posts/texas-about-be-more-chill-hemp-california-2025-06-19)
- **Rescheduling:**
  - DEA's **Apr 22, 2026** final rule moved marijuana in FDA-approved drugs and state-licensed **medical** marijuana to Schedule III, effective immediately.
  - Adult-use marijuana remains Schedule I.
  - A broader hearing before Chief ALJ Derek Julius ran **June 29–July 15, 2026**; the ALJ recommendation and the Administrator's final decision are pending.
  - Litigation risk exists over the Acting AG's use of treaty-implementation authority to reschedule.

  — [Morgan Lewis (Sept 2026)](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/09/high-time-for-a-change-post-dea-hearing-broader-marijuana-rescheduling-questions-remain); [FDA Law Blog (Apr 2026)](https://www.thefdalawblog.com/2026/04/fda-approved-marijuana-products-and-those-subject-to-state-issued-licenses-rescheduled-marijuana-hearing-calendared-dispensary-applications-accepted/)

### Inferences
- Hemp is the clearest "cliff" grey area for startups in 2026. DTC hemp-gummy, THC-beverage and THCA-flower brands have about 2 months of legal life left unless Congress acts by Dec 11. Their responses include:
  - pivoting to licensed state-cannabis channels, or reformulating to 0.4 mg per container ("microdose" or CBD-dominant products)
  - building in states that run their own regulated hemp-THC markets (e.g., MN), while the federal definition will still bar interstate commerce
  - running down inventory and lobbying (e.g., the US Hemp Roundtable)
- Beverage and alcohol distributors that took on hemp-THC SKUs face write-down risk. Payments and ad platforms are likely to de-risk before the deadline.
- Partial Schedule III helps state-licensed **medical** operators with 280E tax relief. Whether it applies to dual-license (medical plus adult-use) operators is itself a grey area; Morgan Lewis flags "broader questions."
- **What would close the gap:** a federal hemp-cannabinoid regulatory framework attached to the Dec 11 CR or FY27 appropriations; a DEA final rule on full rescheduling; SAFER Banking.

### Gaps

- Whether CDPH's permanent California rules were finalized.
- Whether any hemp framework bill has been attached to the next CR (post-Oct 9).

## Other cross-cutting changes (trade, immigration, consumer protection, corporate, securities, tax, gaming)

### Takeaway
Several "deregulatory" moves (CTA, click-to-cancel, non-competes, QSBS, accredited investor) helped startups. Trade and immigration moves (end of de minimis, the $100k H-1B fee) imposed new costs, and their legal bases are still in litigation. Sweepstakes-casino bans show states closing grey-area business models outright.

### Cited Findings
**Tariffs / de minimis**
- **EO 14324** suspended de minimis duty-free treatment for all countries effective **Aug 29, 2025**. Shipments up to $2,500 now need informal entries and duties. — [Ordoro](https://blog.ordoro.com/2026/02/25/de-minimis-exemption-2026/); [Thomson Reuters](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/posts/corporates/sunset-of-de-minimis/)
- **SCOTUS ruled on Feb 20, 2026** (*Learning Resources v. Trump* / *V.O.S. Selections*, 6-3) that IEEPA does not authorize broad tariffs. The de minimis suspension **was not restored**: the administration now relies on Section 321/TFTEA authority. A Section 122 surcharge (reported at 15%) took effect Feb 24, 2026 for up to 150 days. CBP opened an IEEPA refund portal in Apr 2026. — [Novadata](https://novadata.io/resources/news/scotus-ieepa-tariffs-section-122-surcharge-february-2026); [eFulfillment Service](https://www.efulfillmentservice.com/2026/02/u-s-tariffs-overhauled-february-2026-what-ecommerce-sellers-must-know-right-now/); [Thomson Reuters](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/institute/articles/sunset-of-de-minimis)
- OBBBA codifies the end of de minimis with a statutory date of **July 1, 2027**. — [Thomson Reuters](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/posts/corporates/sunset-of-de-minimis/); a federal court also upheld the end of de minimis — [Chain Store Age](https://chainstoreage.com/federal-court-confirms-end-de-minimis-exemption)

**H-1B $100k fee**
- The proclamation was issued in Sept 2025. On **Dec 23, 2025**, Judge Beryl Howell (D.D.C.) granted the government summary judgment in *Chamber of Commerce v. DHS*, finding an express statutory grant of authority to the President. The Chamber appealed (D.C. Cir. No. 25-5473), and review was expedited.
- Separate suits are pending: 20 state AGs (filed Dec 12, 2025), and unions plus a nurse-staffing firm (Oct 2025).

— [Virginia Lawyers Weekly](https://valawyersweekly.com/2026/02/03/federal-court-upholds-100000-h-1b-visa-fee-pending-appeal/); [Staffing Industry Analysts](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/chamber-appeals-verdict-on-100000-fee-for-h-1b-visas); [TechServe Alliance](https://techservealliance.org/news/u-s-chamber-of-commerce-appeals-ruling-in-100k-h-1b-visa-lawsuit/)

**FTC click-to-cancel**

**QSBS (OBBBA, signed July 4, 2025)**
- The per-issuer exclusion cap rises to **$15M** (or 10x basis), inflation-indexed from 2027.
- The gross-assets ceiling rises from $50M to **$75M**.
- A tiered holding period applies: **3 years = 50%, 4 years = 75%, 5 years = 100%**.
- It applies to stock issued after enactment (sources say "after July 4" or "on/after July 5, 2025").

**Sweepstakes casinos**
- **California AB 831** was signed **Oct 11, 2025** and took effect **Jan 1, 2026**. It bans dual-currency online sweepstakes games and reaches payment processors, geolocation providers and affiliates.
- California was the fourth state to legislatively ban sweeps casinos in 2025, after MT, NJ and CT. LA, NY, MD and MI regulators issued cease-and-desist orders. New York passed a ban in June 2025, pending Gov. Hochul's action as of late 2025.
- California was projected at 17.3% of national sweeps revenue.

### Inferences
- **De minimis and tariffs:** DTC startups shipping from China or overseas (the Temu/Shein model, small Shopify brands, dropshippers) lost their cost advantage. Their responses include US bonded warehouses and 3PLs, Section 321 alternatives, bulk import followed by domestic fulfillment, and price increases. The legal basis has shifted twice (IEEPA, then Section 321/TFTEA, then the 2027 statute), and the IEEPA refunds are an open question, so cost forecasting is a grey area.
- **H-1B:** the $100k fee falls hardest on startups that can't absorb it. Workarounds include O-1 visas, hiring offshore or through EOR services, and change-of-status filings for people already in the US (which, per the proclamation's later guidance, may not be subject to the fee; unverified here). The fee is legally exposed until the D.C. Circuit rules.
- **Click-to-cancel and non-competes:** a federal rule being vacated does not end the obligation. State auto-renewal laws (CA ARL amendments effective July 2025, NY, MN) and FTC ROSCA/Section 5 cases still apply. Subscription startups should design to California's standard.
- **CTA:** domestic startups are free of BOI reporting. Startups with foreign founders or non-US entities registered to do business in the US still must file. New York's LLC Transparency Act (effective Jan 1, 2026) may still apply (unverified here).
- QSBS expansion plus the accredited-investor proposal are clear tailwinds for fundraising. Under the tiered holding periods, exits at years 3–4 are now partly tax-advantaged, which changes secondary and M&A timing.
- Sweepstakes bans show how quickly states close business models that exploit a gap ("no purchase necessary" dual currency). The same template could reach prediction markets, skill gaming and social casinos.
