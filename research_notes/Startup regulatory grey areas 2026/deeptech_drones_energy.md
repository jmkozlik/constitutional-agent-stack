# US Deep-Tech / Physical-Sector Policy Changes (2025 to Oct 2026): Grey Areas and Startup Openings

Status as of 2026-10-09. Research method: web search (WebSearch; Firecrawl was rate-limited (HTTP 429)). Most findings come from search-result summaries of law-firm alerts, trade press and agency pages. I did not fetch full primary texts. Where sources disagreed, I say so. All claims carry dates.

## Drones (EO 14307, Part 108 BVLOS, FCC Covered List, counter-UAS, defense drone procurement)

### Takeaway
The drone stack is in an unusual position as of Oct 2026. Federal policy strongly promotes domestic drones: a June 2025 EO, the Pentagon's Drone Dominance buys, and the FCC barring new foreign drones. But the main civil enabling rule, Part 108 BVLOS, is still not final, eight months after the EO deadline. The legal ground is therefore uneven. Domestic hardware makers have protected demand, but operators still depend on waivers. Counter-UAS has just opened to state and local buyers under an interim final rule.

### Cited Findings
**Part 108 BVLOS rule**
- Part 108 BVLOS NPRM published Aug 7, 2025; comments closed Oct 6, 2025. On Jan 28, 2026 FAA reopened comments on three topics: ADS-B Out equipage, alternate electronic conspicuity devices, and detect-and-avoid requirements. — [PickDrones (2026)](https://pickdrones.com/faa-part-108-bvlos-rule-delayed-oira-2026/); [Airdata blog (2026)](https://airdata.com/blog/2026/part-108)
- EO 14307 ("Unleashing American Drone Dominance," June 2025) gave FAA 240 days to finalize, a deadline of about Feb 1, 2026. A separate FAA Reauthorization Act deadline of Jan 16, 2026 also passed. — [PickDrones (2026)](https://pickdrones.com/faa-part-108-bvlos-rule-delayed-oira-2026/)
- FAA sent the final Part 108 text to OIRA on July 10, 2026. Reporting in Sept 2026 expected OIRA's ~90-day review to run to about early Oct 2026. As of ~Sept 30, 2026, no final rule had appeared in the Federal Register. — [lowaltitudeeconomy.aero](https://lowaltitudeeconomy.aero/en/aam-events/faa-part-108-oira-review); [NextMSC (Sept 2026)](https://www.nextmsc.com/news/faa-part-108-bvlos-rule-nears-finalization-as-utm-market-gains-momentum); [ai2.work](https://ai2.work/blog/faa-part-108-bvlos-rule-enters-final-review-at-the-white-house)
- Main open dispute: under the NPRM, equipped Part 108 drones would have right-of-way over crewed aircraft that do not broadcast ADS-B Out or electronic conspicuity. AOPA, EAA, NATA and six other groups formally opposed this. Estimates for publication range from late 2026 into 2027, with staggered compliance dates after publication. — [PickDrones (2026)](https://pickdrones.com/faa-part-108-bvlos-rule-delayed-oira-2026/)

**FCC Covered List (foreign drones)**
- Dec 2025: the FCC added all foreign-made UAS and critical UAS components to its Covered List, which bars new equipment authorizations. DJI and Autel were the statutory targets, but the determination covers all foreign UAS. Previously authorized models can still be sold. — [Wiley Law alert](https://www.wiley.law/alert-FCC-Issues-Exemptions-Clarifications-for-Sweeping-Prohibition-of-Foreign-Made-Drones); [Akin "Autonomous" update, Feb 2026 (Mondaq)](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/unitedstates/telecoms-mobile-cable-communications/1754858/autonomous-akin-update-february-2026)
- The FCC later exempted Blue UAS-listed products and domestic end products. A June 15, 2026 public notice exempted a narrow class of foreign toy drones: lightweight, no GPS or internet, no cameras or sensors, about 10-minute flight time. DJI's smallest drones reportedly do not qualify. — [Android Authority (June 2026)](https://androidauthority.com/fcc-eases-drone-import-rules-3679457/); [Wiley Law](https://www.wiley.law/alert-FCC-Issues-Exemptions-Clarifications-for-Sweeping-Prohibition-of-Foreign-Made-Drones)
- DJI filed an FCC petition for reconsideration on Jan 21, 2026 (comments closed May 11, 2026) and a petition for review in the Ninth Circuit. I found no ruling on either as of my searches. — [DroneLife (Feb 24, 2026)](https://dronelife.com/2026/02/24/dji-lawsuit-fcc-covered-list/); [DroneLife (May 5, 2026)](https://dronelife.com/2026/05/05/public-comment-window-closing-fcc-weighs-dji-challenge-to-covered-list-ruling/)

**Counter-UAS**
- The FY2026 NDAA (P.L. 119-60), including the "SAFER SKIES Act," gave state, local, tribal and territorial (SLTT) law enforcement and correctional agencies authority to mitigate UAS threats. The authority depends on DOJ/DHS training and certification and on using technology from a federally authorized list. Trade groups describe the scope as prisons, large public events and critical infrastructure. — [CRS Insight IN12661 (Feb 27, 2026)](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IN12661.html); [SIA (Dec 11, 2025)](https://www.securityindustry.org/2025/12/11/sia-drone-security-subcommittee-applauds-inclusion-of-key-counter-uas-legislation-in-the-house-passed-2026-national-defense-authorization-act/)
- DOJ and DHS published a joint interim final rule on July 6, 2026 (91 FR, No. 127). It sets a two-tier structure (detection/warning vs. mitigation), an authorized-technology process, spectrum coordination and airspace approval, and is open for comment. — [Federal Register via GovInfo (July 6, 2026)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-07-06/html/2026-13609.htm)
- Federal facility C-UAS authority under 6 U.S.C. §124n is now set to expire Sept 30, 2031. Sheriffs' groups argued in Dec 2025 that a pilot-style framework would leave most agencies without authority. World Cup 2026, America 250 and LA 2028 were cited as drivers. — [CRS IN12661](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IN12661.html); [NSA sheriffs letter (Dec 2, 2025)](https://sheriffs.org/sites/default/files/Final%20%20C-UAS%20Letter%20-%20December%202%20%202025.pdf)

**Defense drone procurement**
- Hegseth's July 2025 memo ("Drone dominance is a process race as much as a technological race") followed the June 2025 EO. The Drone Dominance Program, about $1B or more across four phases, named 25 vendors for Phase I on about Feb 3, 2026, including several Ukraine-based firms, Kratos SRE and Halo Aeronautics. The Phase I "Gauntlet" at Fort Benning was to lead to about $150M in prototype orders, with a second gauntlet about 6 months later. — [DefenseScoop (Feb 3, 2026)](https://defensescoop.com/2026/02/03/drone-dominance-program-hegseth-military-uas-vendors/); [Military Times (Feb 3, 2026)](https://www.militarytimes.com/air/2026/02/03/pentagon-taps-25-firms-for-small-cheap-attack-drone-competition/); [Inside Unmanned Systems](https://insideunmannedsystems.com/pentagon-names-25-vendors-for-phase-i-of-drone-dominance-one-way-attack-uas-program/)
- Volume targets conflict. One outlet reports about 340,000 small drones to units by 2027. Others report a first order of 30,000 systems by July 2026, or "tens of thousands" in 2026. — [Military Times](https://www.militarytimes.com/air/2026/02/03/pentagon-taps-25-firms-for-small-cheap-attack-drone-competition/); [ThreatBeat](https://threatbeat.com/pentagon-unveils-drone-dominance-program-with-gauntlets-to-rapidly-expand-its-small-uas-arsenal/)

### Inferences
- **Grey area 1: BVLOS still runs on waivers.** Until Part 108 publishes, commercial BVLOS (delivery, inspection, ag, DFR) still operates under Part 107 waivers and exemptions. Startups that build fleets against draft Part 108 assumptions risk changes in the final text, especially on right-of-way, ADS-B/EC equipage and DAA. Staggered compliance dates would extend the transition further.
- **Grey area 2: "foreign-made" and "critical component" boundaries.** The Covered List turns on these lines, so domestic assemblers that use foreign subcomponents, and the Blue UAS / domestic-end-product exemptions, determine who can sell new models. This is a large protected-demand opening for US hardware startups, such as Skydio-type and Blue UAS vendors. It is also a compliance risk if the component definitions tighten. The DJI litigation (9th Cir.) could reopen the market, though that looks unlikely near term.
- **Opening: SLTT counter-UAS.** SLTT buyers became a new C-UAS market in 2026. However, sales depend on the authorized-technology list and DOJ/DHS certification, and the July 2026 rule is interim and could change after comments.
- **Defense:** Gauntlet-style procurement gives startups a short path to orders. Because the volume figures conflict, revenue projections tied to Drone Dominance are uncertain.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm whether OIRA cleared Part 108 or whether FAA published it between Oct 1 and Oct 9, 2026. Check reginfo.gov and the Federal Register.
- I did not retrieve the exact text of EO 14307 or the companion June 6, 2025 airspace-sovereignty EO, or their Section 2209 (critical-infrastructure flight restriction) directives.
- I found no outcome for DJI's FCC reconsideration petition or the Ninth Circuit case.
- I did not verify Drone Dominance Phase I award winners or Phase II status.

## eVTOL / Advanced Air Mobility and Supersonic

### Takeaway
Two federal sandboxes now exist: the eVTOL Integration Pilot Program (eIPP), with eight projects in 26 states selected Mar 9, 2026, and a proposed rule to end the 1973 overland supersonic ban (NPRM June 30 / July 2, 2026). Neither is a full certification or operating framework yet. That leaves operational, noise and state/local questions open, and early movers can help shape the answers.

### Cited Findings
- On Mar 9, 2026, DOT/FAA selected 8 eIPP projects across 26 states from about 30 proposals. Use cases are air taxi, regional passenger, cargo and medical. Testing could begin as early as summer 2026. — [FAA newsroom](https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/future-aviation-here-trumps-transportation-secretary-sean-p-duffy-and-faa-unveil-eight); [DOT](https://www.transportation.gov/briefing-room/future-aviation-here-trumps-transportation-secretary-sean-p-duffy-and-faa-unveil); [AAAE alert (Mar 10, 2026)](https://alerts.aaae.org/latest/regulatory_031026)
- Partners include Joby (Port Authority NY/NJ, Texas DOT and Utah DOT projects), Archer (Port Authority, Texas), BETA, Electra, Wisk, Ampaire, Elroy Air and Reliable Robotics. — [eVTOL Insights](https://evtolinsights.com/us-transportation-secretary-sean-p-duffy-and-faa-unveil-companies-selected-for-countrys-evtol-integrated-pilot-program-eipp/); [AltexSoft](https://www.altexsoft.com/travel-industry-news/faa-picks-8-evtol-projects-as-us-air-taxi-plans-get-real/)
- Archer has publicly accused Joby of misleading regulators about reliance on Chinese-made parts, and Joby sued Archer (reported 2026). — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3100766)
- Supersonic: the June 2025 EO directed FAA to repeal the overland ban. FAA's NPRM would rescind the prohibition for en route operations meeting an interim noise standard. Reported metric: surface sonic-boom overpressure below 0.11 psf, effectively "boomless" Mach cutoff, based on Boom's XB-1 flights. Takeoff/landing noise standards are to come in a separate proposal later in 2026. Sources date the NPRM to June 30, 2026 (AAAE) or July 2, 2026 (DLA Piper). One report cites a goal of commercial supersonic by mid-2027. — [AAAE alert](https://alerts.aaae.org/latest/regulatory_063026); [DLA Piper (July 2026)](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2026/07/proposed-faa-rule-would-allow-civil-supersonic-flight); [Raptor Group / Boom](https://www.raptorgroup.com/news/boom-supersonic-faa-proposes-ending-usas-53-year-ban-on-civilian-overland-supersonic-flight/)

### Inferences
- The eIPP gives selected teams operating authority before type certification and before a full powered-lift operating rule set exists. Companies outside the eight projects lack that path, which creates incumbency advantages. Vertiport zoning, noise and local airspace use stay with states and localities. That is an opening for infrastructure and software startups and a patchwork risk for operators.
- The Joby/Archer supply-chain dispute suggests FEOC-style sourcing scrutiny is reaching AAM. Chinese battery and motor content is a latent regulatory risk.
- Until the supersonic final rule is issued, overland supersonic stays illegal. The NPRM only covers en route operations, so airport noise certification is unresolved.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the status of FAA powered-lift rules (SFAR Part 194, final Oct 2024) or any 2026 amendments, or Joby/Archer type-certification status as of Oct 2026.
- I found no reporting confirming actual eIPP passenger or cargo flights as of Oct 2026.
- The supersonic NPRM comment deadline and final-rule timing are unconfirmed.

## Nuclear (May 2025 EOs, NRC reform, DOE/Army pilots, microreactors)

### Takeaway
Nuclear policy moved fastest of these sectors. Four advanced reactors reached criticality under DOE authorization (not NRC licensing) by about July 4, 2026. NRC finalized Part 53 in March 2026. The Army picked five microreactor vendors for Janus on Aug 26, 2026. The main grey area is the line between DOE-authorized test reactors and NRC-licensed commercial plants, plus the NRC rewrite slipping past the Nov 23, 2026 EO deadline.

### Cited Findings
- EO 14300 (May 23, 2025) required NRC to issue proposed rules within 9 months (about Feb 23, 2026) and final rules within 18 months (about Nov 23, 2026). In Mar 2026, NRC said it was on target. — [ANS (Mar 13, 2026)](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-03-13/article-7842/nrc-provides-timeline-update-on-rules-meeting-eo-deadline/)
- NRC's Part 53 risk-informed framework for advanced reactors was finalized in late March 2026, more than a year ahead of the NEIMA end-2027 deadline. Sources differ on the exact date: Mar 27 vs. Mar 30, 2026. — [ANS](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-03-13/article-7842/nrc-provides-timeline-update-on-rules-meeting-eo-deadline/); [Nuclear Innovation Alliance rule tracker (Sept 1, 2026)](https://www.nuclearinnovationalliance.org/sites/default/files/2026-09/Public%20Rule%20timeline%209-1.pdf)
- The NIA tracker (Sept 1, 2026) shows several EO 14300 final rules scheduled after Nov 23, 2026. Examples: "Organizational Changes and Conforming Amendments" and "Regulatory Enhancements for Reactor Licensing," both at 3/31/2027. — [NIA tracker (Sept 1, 2026)](https://www.nuclearinnovationalliance.org/sites/default/files/2026-09/Public%20Rule%20timeline%209-1.pdf)
- DOE Reactor Pilot Program (announced June 2025): companies seek DOE authorization under the Atomic Energy Act rather than NRC licensing. Goal: 3 reactors critical by July 4, 2026. — [DOE NE program page](https://www.energy.gov/ne/us-department-energy-reactor-pilot-program); [ExchangeMonitor (June 19, 2025)](https://www.exchangemonitor.com/doe-unveils-pilot-program-to-test-advanced-nuclear-projects/)
- DOE reported Aalo Atomics' Aalo-X at INL as the 4th DOE-authorized reactor to achieve zero-power criticality, ahead of the July 4 goal. Other reporting lists the four as Antares Mark-0, Valar Atomics Ward 250, Deployable Energy Unity and Aalo-X. Exact dates vary by source. — [DOE](https://www.energy.gov/articles/department-energy-celebrates-fourth-criticality-ahead-july-4th-goal); [E&E News](https://www.eenews.net/articles/first-reactor-reaches-criticality-under-does-nuclear-pilot-program/); [ANS](https://www.ans.org/news/tag-doe/step-1783368245/)
- Army Janus: on Aug 26, 2026 the Army selected Antares (Fort Bragg), BWXT (Fort Campbell), General Atomics EMS (Fort Hood), Radiant (Fort Benning) and Westinghouse (Fort Drum). The effort is up to about $2.2B over FY2027 to FY2031 for 20+ contractor-owned, contractor-operated microreactors, with milestone payments and a sustained-operation target of Sept 30, 2028. — [POWER Magazine](https://powermag.com/u-s-army-mobilizes-2-2b-janus-program-to-deploy-more-than-20-commercial-microreactors); [Army.mil](https://www.army.mil/article/294897/army_selects_fort_drum_for_janus_program_microreactor); [Air University](https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/News/MAJCOM/Display/Article/4584583/army-selects-vendors-sites-for-nuclear-microreactors/)

### Inferences
- **Grey area: the jump from DOE to NRC.** DOE-authorized criticality is not a commercial license. Startups must still cross to NRC (Part 53 or Part 50/52) to sell power to private customers. How NRC will credit DOE pilot data is the key ambiguity. DOE has said the pilot is meant to "enable future commercial licensing activities," but I found no binding NRC rule on that.
- **Grey area: military sites.** Janus reactors are contractor-owned and operated on military land, which raises the question of NRC vs. DoD/DOE jurisdiction. I did not confirm which regulator licenses Janus units.
- NRC final rules slipping into 2027 means part of the EO 14300 overhaul will not meet its 18-month deadline. Companies planning around fixed licensing timelines face schedule risk, and litigation over rushed rules is possible.
- Startup clustering: Aalo, Valar, Antares, Radiant, Deployable Energy and others are using the DOE fast path and DoD offtake as stepping stones.

### Gaps
- I did not research ADVANCE Act (July 2024) implementation details, such as fee reductions and the microreactor licensing provisions, or 2026 NRC actions under it.
- I did not verify the other May 2025 nuclear EOs (DOE reactor testing reform, DoD reactors, nuclear industrial base and fuel) in detail, including HALEU allocation status.
- Janus licensing authority (NRC vs. DOE/DoD) is unconfirmed.

## Energy and Permitting (NEPA, permitting bills, data-center power, FERC co-location, OBBBA credit phase-outs and FEOC)

### Takeaway
NEPA has become decentralized: CEQ rules rescinded (IFR Feb 2025, final Jan 8, 2026), Seven County (May 2025) narrowed review, and agencies now write their own procedures. Federal permitting legislation (SPEED Act) passed the House in Dec 2025 but faces a 60-vote Senate. FERC chose show-cause orders to RTOs (June 18, 2026) rather than a clear final large-load rule, and Texas SB 6 is setting the most concrete co-location precedent. OBBBA set hard wind/solar cliffs and FEOC "material assistance" tests whose entity-level rules are still incomplete.

### Cited Findings
**NEPA**
- Seven County Infrastructure Coalition v. Eagle County (May 2025, 8-0) told courts to defer to agencies on NEPA scope, especially indirect effects. — [Vermont Journal of Environmental Law (Jan 2026)](https://vjel.vermontlaw.edu/top-ten/2026/01/nepa-without-ceq-environmental-review-under-trump-and-seven-county/); [NACo](https://www.naco.org/resource/changes-national-environmental-policy-act-nepa)
- CEQ rescinded all its NEPA regulations by IFR on Feb 25, 2025 (effective Apr 11, 2025) and finalized the rescission unchanged on Jan 8, 2026, relying partly on Seven County. CEQ guidance of Sept 29, 2025 directed agencies to revise their procedures. Multiple agencies issued interim or final NEPA procedures in July 2025, leaving agency-specific procedures as the operative framework. — [Orrick (Jan 2026)](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/01/CEQ-Issues-Final-Rule-Eliminating-NEPA-Regulations-Path-to-NEPA-Reform); [Pillsbury](https://www.pillsburylaw.com/en/news-and-insights/ceq-rescission-nepa-implementing-regulations.html); [VJEL](https://vjel.vermontlaw.edu/top-ten/2026/01/nepa-without-ceq-environmental-review-under-trump-and-seven-county/)

**Permitting bills**
- The SPEED Act (H.R. 4776) passed the House on Dec 18, 2025, 221-196. It narrows NEPA timelines and litigation windows. Bipartisan "permit certainty" language was removed late under pressure from offshore-wind opponents, and the American Clean Power Association withdrew support. The bill needs 60 Senate votes. Capito and Whitehouse (EPW) have signaled interest in a bipartisan package, and Sen. Schatz tied his support to addressing the administration's actions against solar and wind. The PERMIT Act (H.R. 3898, CWA §401) also passed the House in Dec 2025. — [World Oil (Dec 18, 2025)](https://worldoil.com/news/2025/12/18/u-s-house-passes-bipartisan-speed-act-advancing-federal-permitting-reform/); [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/senate-permitting-reform-speed-act/808471/); [RCRC](https://www.rcrcnet.org/general/house-approves-speed-act-setting-up-senate-talks/)

**FERC large-load / co-location**
- Oct 2025: DOE directed FERC to start an ANOPR on large-load interconnection, including co-location, and to take "final action" by Apr 30, 2026 (Docket RM26-4-000). On Apr 16, 2026 FERC issued an "Order Regarding Intent to Act" by end of June 2026. On June 18, 2026 FERC issued show-cause orders to all six jurisdictional RTOs/ISOs (PJM, MISO, SPP, CAISO, ISO-NE, NYISO) to justify or revise large-load and co-location tariff provisions. — [McGuireWoods (Feb 2026)](https://www.mcguirewoods.com/client-resources/alerts/2026/2/2026-regulatory-outlook-key-federal-and-state-proceedings-shaping-data-center-electric-supply/); [Bracewell (July 9, 2026)](https://www.bracewell.com/resources/ferc-show-cause-orders-data-center-interconnection/); [Sidley Energy Infrastructure Pulse (July 2026)](https://energyinfrastructurepulse.sidley.com/2026/07/)
- PJM track: a Dec 2025 FERC order requires PJM co-location generators to name an "Eligible Customer" taking one of three new transmission services. On Apr 16, 2026 FERC partly accepted and partly rejected PJM's compliance filing. — [Mondaq (FERC directs PJM)](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/unitedstates/energy-law/1731716/ferc-directs-pjm-to-facilitate-co-location-arrangements); [McGuireWoods](https://www.mcguirewoods.com/client-resources/alerts/2026/2/2026-regulatory-outlook-key-federal-and-state-proceedings-shaping-data-center-electric-supply/)
- Texas SB 6 (2025): generators must notify ERCOT before a net-metering (co-location) arrangement, and ERCOT runs a 120-day system impact study. PUCT rulemakings (interconnection standards, net metering, forecasting, large-load demand reduction) were targeted for adoption across 2026. The draft net-metering rule required PUCT approval for new loads of 75 MW or more paired with existing generators. — [Weil (July 2025)](https://www.weil.com/-/media/files/pdfs/2025/july/weil-energy-alert--senate-bill-6-reforms-interconnection-and-colocation-rules-for-data-centers-and-o.pdf); [Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/sb-6-implementation-shaping-data-center-future-texas)
- July 24, 2026: in PUCT Docket 59220 (Goodnight Wind 265.5 MW + a Crusoe 260 MW data center, Armstrong County), the PUCT approved net metering and held that emergency curtailment of co-located data centers is not capped at the paired generator's capacity. White & Case calls it the "first" net-metering order. An ERCOT June 1, 2026 update, however, says two arrangements were already approved (Apr and May 2026), with three pending. These accounts conflict. — [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/puct-affirms-curtailment-authority-over-co-located-data-centers-first-net-metering); [ERCOT (June 1, 2026)](https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/06/01/ERCOT-Trending-Topic-Grid-Reliability-Data-Centers-Net-Metering-Arrangements.pdf)

**OBBBA credit phase-outs and FEOC**
- OBBBA added §45Y(d)(4) and §48E(e)(4). Wind and solar facilities that begin construction after July 4, 2026 must be placed in service by Dec 31, 2027. Projects that began construction earlier keep a 4-year continuity runway: through Dec 31, 2029 if begun before end-2025, or Dec 31, 2030 if begun Jan 1 to July 4, 2026. Energy storage is not subject to this wind/solar cliff. — [Withum](https://www.withum.com/resources/navigating-safe-harbor-rules-for-solar-and-wind-sec-48e-facilities/); [The Tax Adviser (Feb 2026)](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2026/feb/navigating-safe-harbor-rules-for-solar-and-wind-sec-48e-facilities.html); [Baker Botts (Aug 2025)](https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2025/august/clean-energy-tax-credits-new-guidance-on-beginning-of-construction-for-wind-and-solar-facilities)
- Notice 2025-42 (Aug 15, 2025) removed the 5% cost safe harbor for wind and solar, except solar under 1.5 MW, and required the physical work test. Novogradac reports litigation over Notice 2025-42. One summary claims a court vacated it, but I could not confirm this and treat it as unverified. — [A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/highlights-of-the-new-sections-45y-and-48e-wind-and-solar-beginning-of-construction-guidance); [Novogradac](https://www.novoco.com/notes-from-novogradac/unpacking-the-complex-compliance-timeline-for-sections-48e-45y-45x-and-what-the-markets-should-be-tracking-including-notice-2025-42-litigation-on-solar-and-wind-beginning-of-construction)
- FEOC: OBBBA bars "prohibited foreign entities" from claiming credits and applies "material assistance" cost-ratio tests to projects beginning construction after Dec 31, 2025. IRS Notice 2026-15 gave interim material-assistance guidance. Reported storage threshold under 48E: 55% non-PFE content for 2026 construction starts, rising to 60% in 2027 and 75% by 2030. Rules for determining PFE status (the "effective control" test) were still forthcoming. Latitude Media called the guidance "underwhelming" for punting on the key question. — [Foley](https://www.foley.com/p/102mis0/irs-releases-guidance-regarding-material-assistance-rules/); [Latitude Media](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/underwhelming-feoc-guidance-punts-on-industrys-key-question/); [Etica AG (Feb 2026)](https://eticaag.com/treasurys-updated-feoc-and-its-impact-on-bess-feb-2026/); [Novogradac](https://www.novoco.com/notes-from-novogradac/unpacking-the-complex-compliance-timeline-for-sections-48e-45y-45x-and-what-the-markets-should-be-tracking-including-notice-2025-42-litigation-on-solar-and-wind-beginning-of-construction)
- FTI estimated in July 2025 that over 100 GW of US solar and wind projects "no longer pencil out" under OBBBA. — [pv magazine USA (July 23, 2025)](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2025/07/23/over-100-gw-of-u-s-solar-wind-projects-no-longer-pencil-out-says-fti-consulting/)

### Inferences
- **NEPA:** with no CEQ baseline, each agency has its own procedures, so the review standard depends on which agency leads a project. Seven County's deference lowers litigation risk, but challenges to agency procedures (APA claims) and state "little NEPA" laws remain open questions.
- **FERC:** the June 18, 2026 show-cause orders push the main co-location rules into six RTO compliance proceedings rather than one national rule, so behind-the-meter data-center deals face region-by-region uncertainty through at least late 2026. Texas (non-FERC ERCOT) is ahead, but the Goodnight/Crusoe order shows co-located loads remain curtailable, which limits the "islanded" appeal.
- **Openings:** these include load-flexibility and curtailment software, on-site generation (gas, storage, eventually SMRs and microreactors), FEOC supply-chain tracing and compliance tools, and domestic-content component makers. Storage, nuclear and geothermal are better placed than wind and solar after OBBBA.
- **FEOC risk:** until Treasury defines "effective control" and PFE status, tax-equity and transferability buyers will discount credits or require extra indemnities.

## Space, Autonomous Vehicles and Defense-Tech Procurement

### Takeaway
EO 14335 (Aug 2025) ordered a Part 450 rethink and a Commerce "novel space activity" authorization process, but I found no published rule yet. Part 450 became the sole launch licensing regime on Mar 9, 2026. NHTSA is reshaping AV deployment through exemptions rather than comprehensive standards: a Zoox exemption, a July 31, 2026 IFR allowing pre-exemption production, and the AV STEP withdrawal in June 2026. Defense procurement is shifting toward fast-cycle competitions.

### Cited Findings
- EO 14335 (Aug 2025) directed DOT to re-evaluate Part 450 and identify waivable or inapplicable requirements. It directed Commerce to propose an individualized authorization process for "novel" space activities under Outer Space Treaty Art. ### Inferences
- **Space:** until Commerce issues a rule, "novel" activities such as in-space manufacturing, servicing, debris removal and private stations have no clear federal authorizer. This is the classic Art. VI "mission authorization gap." It is an opening for first movers but carries risk: an FCC, NOAA or FAA payload review could be used ad hoc, or a future process could add conditions.
- **AV:** the 2,500-vehicle-per-year exemption cap and the exemption-by-exemption approach make scale deployment of vehicles without manual controls (e.g., Zoox, Tesla Cybercab) depend on agency discretion and the July 2026 IFR, which could be challenged or changed. State law on operations, permits and liability still applies. No federal AV statute preempts it.
- **Defense:** fast-cycle buys favor venture-backed hardware startups, but small initial orders such as about $150M across many vendors make follow-on production funding the real risk.

## What Startups Are Doing and Key Risks

### Takeaway
Startups are using the 2025 to 2026 fast lanes: DOE reactor authorization, DoD gauntlets and Janus, eIPP, FCC protectionism and SB 6 co-location. Many are building ahead of final rules. The biggest risks are final rules that differ from proposals (Part 108, supersonic, NRC), slow agency follow-through (FEOC entity rules, FERC co-location), litigation (DJI, Notice 2025-42, NEPA procedures), and reliance on executive orders a future administration could reverse.

### Cited Findings
- Nuclear startups Aalo, Valar, Antares and Deployable Energy reached criticality via DOE authorization by about July 2026. Antares and Radiant also won Janus sites in Aug 2026. — [DOE](https://www.energy.gov/articles/department-energy-celebrates-fourth-criticality-ahead-july-4th-goal); [POWER](https://powermag.com/u-s-army-mobilizes-2-2b-janus-program-to-deploy-more-than-20-commercial-microreactors)
- Crusoe (data-center developer) paired a 260 MW load with an existing wind farm under SB 6, and the PUCT approved it with uncapped emergency curtailment (July 24, 2026). — [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/puct-affirms-curtailment-authority-over-co-located-data-centers-first-net-metering)
- AAM firms Joby, Archer, BETA, Electra, Wisk, Elroy Air and Reliable Robotics joined eIPP projects (Mar 2026). — [eVTOL Insights](https://evtolinsights.com/us-transportation-secretary-sean-p-duffy-and-faa-unveil-companies-selected-for-countrys-evtol-integrated-pilot-program-eipp/)
- 25 drone vendors, including Ukrainian firms, entered Drone Dominance Phase I (Feb 2026). — [Military Times](https://www.militarytimes.com/air/2026/02/03/pentagon-taps-25-firms-for-small-cheap-attack-drone-competition/)
- Boom used XB-1 Mach-cutoff data to underpin FAA's proposed 0.11 psf overland standard (2026 NPRM). — [Raptor Group / Boom](https://www.raptorgroup.com/news/boom-supersonic-faa-proposes-ending-usas-53-year-ban-on-civilian-overland-supersonic-flight/); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2026/07/proposed-faa-rule-would-allow-civil-supersonic-flight)

### Inferences
- **Common pattern:** startups secure government-sanctioned "pilot" status (DOE, eIPP, Janus, Blue UAS) to get de facto authority ahead of general rules, then lobby to have the pilot terms written into the final regulation.
- **Durability risk:** many openings rest on EOs and interim final rules (C-UAS IFR, NHTSA IFR, CEQ rescission). These are easier to reverse than statutes after the 2028 election. Statutory anchors (FY26 NDAA C-UAS, OBBBA, ADVANCE Act, NEIMA/Part 53) are more durable.
- **Gaps could close through:** Part 108 publication, a Commerce novel-activities rule, FERC/RTO compliance outcomes, Treasury PFE "effective control" rules, NRC's remaining EO 14300 rules (Nov 2026 to Mar 2027), a Senate permitting deal, and court outcomes (DJI v. FCC, Notice 2025-42 litigation).

- State-level preemption fights (drones, AVs, vertiports) were not researched.
