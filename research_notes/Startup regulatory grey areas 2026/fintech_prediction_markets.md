## 1. CFPB: funding and staff cuts, rescinded guidance and rules, status as of Oct 2026, and states stepping in

### Takeaway
As of Oct 2026 the CFPB still exists and is legally required to stay funded. Courts have twice ruled that leadership must request Federal Reserve funding: the D.D.C. ruling (FY2026 Q2 funds requested under protest in Jan 2026) and the D. Or. ruling for 22 state AGs (late Sept 2026). But the bureau is much smaller and has withdrawn dozens of guidance documents. Congress repealed the digital-payments larger-participant rule and moved to kill the overdraft rule. Federal rules have been withdrawn, while states such as NY (BNPL), MD and IN (EWA) are building their own regimes. For startups that means looser federal supervision but a growing patchwork of state rules.

Dec 23, 2025) it issued a new advisory opinion that "Covered EWA" is not "credit" under Reg Z, that expedite fees and voluntary tips are not finance charges, and it withdrew the 2024 proposed interpretive rule that had treated most EWA as credit. Products outside the "Covered EWA" definition were left unaddressed. — [Federal Register 2025-23735](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/12/23/2025-23735/truth-in-lending-regulation-z-non-application-to-earned-wage-access-products); [DWT (Jan 2026)](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/financial-services-law-advisor/2026/01/cfpb-revisits-2020-opinion-earned-wage-access); [Payments Dive](https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/some-ewa-products-arent-loans-cfpb-consumer-protection-paycheck/808587/)

**States stepping in**
- NY DFS formally proposed BNPL licensing rules (3 NYCRR Part 423) on July 15, 2026, with comments due Sept 14, 2026. The rules implement a BNPL licensing law enacted in New York's FY2026 budget. Reported provisions include licensing, separate category permissions (interest-free vs interest-bearing), monthly statements, an $8 late-fee cap, dispute resolution, and, per one summary, a 16% rate cap on interest-bearing BNPL. Rules would take effect 180 days after adoption, with a transition for existing providers. Banks need DFS authorization, not a license. — [NY DFS proposed text (July 2026)](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2026/07/rp-bank-3nycrr-423-text.pdf); [Orrick (Mar 2026)](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/03/New-York-Proposes-Comprehensive-BNPL-Rule-What-BNPL-Providers-Need-to-Know); [Holland & Knight (Mar 2026)](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/from-statute-to-supervision-preparing-for-new-yorks-new-bnpl); [McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/new-york-proceeds-with-expansive-regulation-of-buy-now-pay-later-products/)
- One firm observes that NY's proposal adopts many of the consumer protections in the CFPB's withdrawn Biden-era BNPL guidance. — [McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/new-york-proceeds-with-expansive-regulation-of-buy-now-pay-later-products/)
- State AGs are suing to keep the CFPB funded: 22 AGs in New York v. Vought. — [MN AG, Sept 28, 2026](https://www.ag.state.mn.us/Office/Communications/2026/09/28_CFPB.asp)
- Analysts frame 2026 as a "deregulatory pendulum swing", with enforcement moving toward states. — [Capstone DC 2026 preview](https://capstonedc.com/insights/consumer-finance-2026-preview/)

### Inferences
- The CFPB is currently funded by court order, not by agreement. Three things could change that: the en banc D.C. Circuit decision, an appeal of the Oregon ruling, and H.R. 10184, which would put the bureau on appropriations. Startups cannot count on federal supervision or enforcement staying at today's low level after a change of administration. CFPA claims carry long statutes of limitations, so conduct during this lull could be pursued by a future CFPB or by state AGs, who can enforce the CFPA themselves (12 U.S.C. 5552).
- The federal pullback on BNPL, overdraft and payment-app supervision makes it easy to launch nationally. The NY BNPL regime shows that states will reimpose roughly the withdrawn federal standards, so a multistate BNPL product is now more complex to run.
- Federal EWA relief applies only to "Covered EWA" (employer-integrated, no-recourse). Direct-to-consumer EWA models still face state-by-state treatment and the risk of being classed as credit.

## 2. Open banking (Section 1033): litigation, reconsideration, JPMorgan data-access fees, and current status

### Takeaway
The Oct 2024 Section 1033 rule is enjoined (preliminary injunction, E.D. Ky., Oct 29, 2025). The CFPB has told the court that it considers its own rule unlawful, and it is rewriting the rule after an Aug 2025 ANPR. Banks have started charging aggregators for data, starting with JPMorgan. A revised proposal that reportedly drops the ban on such fees is under OIRA review, though that is unverified. Data access is in a grey area of contract-based fees and a rule that is not in force.

### Cited Findings
- The CFPB finalized the 1033 rule in Oct 2024. The banks' suit (E.D. Ky.) produced a preliminary injunction on Oct 29, 2025, and the CFPB now tells the court the rule is unlawful. — [Open Banking Tracker, Section 1033 status (2026)](https://openbankingtracker.com/guides/section-1033-status) (secondary tracker); [CRS IF13117](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF13117.html)
- The Aug 2025 ANPR reopened four issues: who qualifies as a "representative", data-access fees, data security, and data privacy. — [Open Banking Tracker](https://openbankingtracker.com/guides/section-1033-status); [PYMNTS (2026)](https://www.pymnts.com/legal/bank-regulation/2026/data-aggregators-push-secure-access-as-rule-1033-rewrite-looms/)
- The first compliance date, Apr 1, 2026, passed with no compliance obligation because of the injunction. — [Open Banking Tracker](https://openbankingtracker.com/guides/section-1033-status)
- **Unverified:** one source reports that the CFPB sent a revised 1033 proposal to OIRA on Aug 6, 2026, reportedly reversing the ban on bank fees for data access. — [Open Banking Tracker](https://openbankingtracker.com/guides/section-1033-status) (single secondary source; no primary confirmation)
- JPMorgan began billing data aggregators for account-data access in July 2025, access that had previously been free. JPMorgan and Plaid reached a paid data-access deal in Sept 2025. Forbes estimated a cost of about $300M per year to Plaid; neither party disclosed terms. — [Open Banking Tracker, data access fees guide](https://www.openbankingtracker.com/guides/open-banking-data-access-fees) (secondary)
- New York introduced open-banking bills in Mar 2026 that would ban data-access fees at the state level. — [Open Banking Tracker](https://openbankingtracker.com/guides/section-1033-status) (secondary; bill status not verified)
- The Financial Technology Association opposes data-access fees. — [FTA](https://www.ftassociation.org/looking-ahead-to-the-open-banking-rule-protecting-americans-right-to-securely-access-and-share-their-financial-data-without-fees/)
- Superseded source: an Ozone API post saying the rule "remains the law of the land", with July 2026 deadlines, predates the injunction. — [Ozone API](https://ozoneapi.com/blog/the-current-status-of-section-1033-in-the-united-states-a-year-on-the-regulatory-rollercoaster/)

### Inferences
- There is no enforceable federal right to data access, and fees are allowed in practice. Aggregators and fintechs that depend on bank data (lending underwriting, PFM, pay-by-bank) face rising per-call costs and bank-by-bank bilateral contracts. Smaller aggregators are at a structural disadvantage to Plaid-scale players that can negotiate deals.
- A final rewritten rule could legalize fees and narrow who counts as a "representative", which could exclude some data uses such as resale or secondary use. State laws, if NY's bill passes, could then conflict with federal policy, creating a preemption question.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the OIRA submission or the contents of the revised NPRM from the Federal Register or reginfo.gov. Whether an NPRM was published by Oct 2026 is unknown.
- I did not verify the exact case caption and docket, reported as Forcht Bank / Kentucky Bankers Ass'n / BPI v. CFPB, E.D. Ky.
- I found no confirmed list of other banks charging data fees beyond JPMorgan.

## 3. Bank-fintech partnerships, BaaS, Synapse fallout, OCC charters, and FDIC brokered-deposit withdrawal

### Takeaway
Federal regulators withdrew the Biden-era tightening on brokered deposits (FDIC, Mar 2025) but left the post-Synapse custodial-account recordkeeping proposal pending, at least as of 2025. Meanwhile the OCC is approving national trust bank charters for crypto and fintech firms at a rapid pace. That gives startups a new path to a federal charter but no settled answer on what such charters may do.

### Cited Findings
- On Mar 3, 2025 (Fed. Reg. Mar 14, 2025), the FDIC Board withdrew its Aug 2024 brokered-deposit restrictions proposal along with its corporate-governance and Change in Bank Control proposals. It said it does not intend to finalize them but could re-propose. — [Federal Register 2025-04088](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/03/14/2025-04088/unsafe-and-unsound-banking-practices-brokered-deposits-restrictions-guidelines-establishing-standards-for-corporate-governance-and-risk-management-for-covered-institutions-with-total-consolidated-assets-of-10-billion-or-more); [FDIC FIL](https://www.fdic.gov/news/financial-institution-letters/2025/fdic-withdraws-proposed-rules-related-brokered-deposits); [Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/fdic-withdraws-merger-policy-brokered-deposits-proposal-hot-money/741531/)
- The FDIC's Oct 2024 proposal on recordkeeping for custodial accounts, made after Synapse, was not among the withdrawn proposals. It would require banks to keep beneficial-owner ledgers for pooled "for benefit of" (FBO) accounts with transactional features. Bank trade groups urged withdrawal, arguing it shifts the cost of nonbank middleware failures onto banks. — [Steptoe](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/fdic-board-of-directors-rolls-back-several-biden-era-rulemaking-actions.html); [DWT (Oct 2024)](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/financial-services-law-advisor/2024/10/fdic-proposes-bank-fintech-rules-after-synapse); [ABA joint trades letter](https://www.aba.com/advocacy/policy-analysis/letter-to-fdic-on-recordkeeping-for-custodial-accounts)
- In Dec 2025 the OCC conditionally approved five national trust bank charters (two de novo, three conversions). ICBA opposed them. — [ICBA](https://www.icba.org/web/payments/w/occ-approves-icba-opposed-national-trust-charter-applications); [Paul Hastings](https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/crypto-policy-tracker/occ-conditionally-approves-national-trust-bank-charter-applications-and-issues-interpretive-letter-1188)
- In Feb 2026 Crypto.com (Foris DAX National Trust Bank) got conditional OCC approval. The charter allows custody but not deposit-taking or lending. Stripe's Bridge (Bridge National Trust Bank) and Protego were also approved in Feb 2026. — [Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/occ-conditional-approval-national-trust-bank-charter-circle-ripple-paxos-bridge/812925/); [Crypto.com](https://crypto.com/us/company-news/cryptocom-receives-conditional-approval-from-occ-for-national-trust-bank-charter)
- Later approvals were reported for Morgan Stanley's digital-asset trust (June 2026) and World Liberty (Aug 2026, a 221-day review against the 120 days Comptroller Gould had advertised). Bastion Platforms, Catena Trust Bank and Agora National Trust Bank received preliminary conditional approval on Sept 18, 2026. One report says Circle obtained final approval on July 10, 2026; this is unverified against OCC records. — [American Banker, "Fintechs asking for, and receiving, bank charters in 2026"](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/fintechs-asking-for-and-receiving-bank-charters-in-2026); [FinTech Weekly](https://www.fintechweekly.com/news/occ-national-trust-bank-charter-crypto-fintech-2026); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/crypto/articles/occ-just-approved-three-digital-141125609.html) (mixed secondary)
- BPI warned that these charters could "blur the statutory boundary" of what a "bank" is. — [Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/occ-conditional-approval-national-trust-bank-charter-circle-ripple-paxos-bridge/812925/)

### Inferences
- The grey area is whether a national trust charter, which has no deposits and no lending, can support payments and stablecoin activity, and whether such charters preempt state money-transmitter licensing. Bank trade groups are signalling possible litigation or legislative pushback. A change of OCC leadership could slow approvals.
- With the brokered-deposit tightening withdrawn, sweep and deposit-network models (BaaS, cash-management fintechs) face less federal classification risk. The custodial-ledger proposal is unresolved, however, so FBO-account reconciliation expectations are set through bank-by-bank consent orders and supervisory pressure rather than a clear rule.

- The current status of the FDIC custodial-recordkeeping proposal (finalized, withdrawn or dormant) as of Oct 2026 was not confirmed.
- I found no data on new de novo full-service fintech bank charters (SoFi-style), ILC applications, or OCC "payments charter" actions in 2026.

## 4. Prediction markets and event contracts: Kalshi litigation, state actions, Polymarket, CFTC rulemaking, tribal challenges

### Takeaway
As of early Oct 2026 the federal circuits are split on whether the CEA preempts state gambling law for sports event contracts. The 3rd Circuit (NJ, Apr 6, 2026) says yes. The 9th Circuit (NV, Aug 28, 2026) and 6th Circuit (OH/TN, late Sept 2026) say no, and the 6th also held that sports contracts are not "swaps". NJ's cert petition (No. 26-299) and others are pending at the Supreme Court. The CFTC under Chair Selig is going the other way from states: it withdrew the 2024 proposed ban and proposed on June 10, 2026 a flexible "public interest determination" framework. Startups therefore operate under federal permission and state prohibition at once, depending on where the user is.

IAGR and NAGRA filed an amicus brief in support on Oct 2, 2026, and 39 states and regulators reportedly back NJ. No cert grant had been reported as of early Oct 2026. — [SiaPredict, docket No. 26-299](https://siapredict.com/blog/kalshi-supreme-court-flaherty-docket-26-299); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/new-jersey-seeks-supreme-court-review-kalshi-decision-and-one-additional-argument); [RG.org](https://rg.org/news/prediction-markets/39-states-regulators-urge-supreme-court-nj-kalshi-case)

- Polymarket bought the CFTC-registered exchange QCEX for $112M in July 2025. A Polymarket US exchange reportedly launched on Dec 2, 2025 with CFTC approval. On May 20, 2026 it self-certified "Combinatorial Athletic Outcome Contracts", which are parlay-style multi-leg sports contracts. — [Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/264161/polymarket-adds-multi-leg-contracts-as-it-chases-kalshi-sportsbooks/); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/polymarket-poised-re-enter-us-market-amid-kalshis-ongoing-legal-battles-gaming); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/options/articles/polymarket-files-parlay-style-sports-184600834.html)

**CFTC rulemaking**
- In early 2026 Chair Michael Selig directed staff to withdraw the 2024 proposed rule that would have banned political and sports event contracts. — [Sidley (Feb 2026)](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2026/02/us-cftc-signals-imminent-rulemaking-on-prediction-markets); [CRS LSB11441](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/LSB11441)
- On Mar 12, 2026 the CFTC published an ANPRM on prediction markets. Forbes reported that the CFTC urged prediction markets to partner with sports leagues. — [CFTC Press Release 9194-26](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9194-26); [Forbes, Mar 14, 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jasonbrett/2026/03/14/cftc-urges-prediction-markets-to-partner-with-sports-leagues/); [SBA Advocacy, Mar 23, 2026](https://advocacy.sba.gov/2026/03/23/cftc-prediction-markets/)
- On June 10, 2026 the CFTC proposed "Prediction Markets; Public Interest Determinations" (RIN 3038-AF65), amending Rule 40.11. The CFTC "may determine" that contracts involving enumerated activities (unlawful activity, terrorism, assassination, war, gaming) are against the public interest, replacing the categorical bans, under a three-step framework. Comments were due July 27, 2026. I found no final rule as of Oct 2026. — [Ropes & Gray (June 2026)](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/06/rewriting-the-rulebook-cftc-proposes-rule-changes-for-prediction-market-contracts); [CRS LSB11441](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/LSB11441); [Sportico](https://www.sportico.com/business/sports-betting/2026/cftc-prediction-market-rule-proposal-explain-1234902902/)
- Selig presented a regulatory roadmap (event contracts, reporting, consumer safeguards) at the CFTC Innovation Advisory Committee on Aug 20, 2026. — [15m.com](https://15m.com/news/cftc-sets-three-part-plan-for-us-prediction-market-rules/) (secondary)
- On Apr 30, 2026, Sen. Merkley and colleagues urged Selig to bar insider trading and contracts on elections, war, sports and government actions that lack a hedging interest. — [Merkley letter](https://www.merkley.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/26.04.30-CFTC-Event-Contract-Letter.pdf)

### Inferences
- The central grey area is whether a CFTC-listed sports contract is a "swap" or event contract under exclusive federal jurisdiction or is state-regulated gambling. It will most likely be settled by SCOTUS, with cert possible in OT2026, or by Congress. Until then, legality depends on the user's state and the federal circuit it sits in. Startups listing sports contracts in 6th or 9th Circuit states, or in states with criminal exposure such as Arizona, face real enforcement risk.
- A CFTC final rule replacing categorical bans with discretionary public-interest review would make listing easier at the federal level. It would not resolve preemption, and the 6th Circuit's "not swaps" reasoning would undercut CFTC authority over sports contracts altogether.
- Parlays and combo contracts (Polymarket May 2026) bring prediction markets even closer to sportsbook products. That strengthens states' gambling characterization.
- Startup strategy seen in the sources: buy an already registered DCM/DCO (Polymarket/QCEX), self-certify contracts under Rule 40.2, and sue states pre-emptively (Kalshi v. Arizona, Polymarket v. Massachusetts).

## 5. Sweepstakes casinos and earned wage access state laws

### Takeaway
States are rapidly banning sweepstakes ("dual-currency") casinos. Sources agree on about 9 statutory bans as of mid/late 2026, including California's AB 831 (effective Jan 1, 2026), and Oklahoma's ban takes effect Nov 1, 2026. EWA has the reverse federal and state split: the CFPB says "Covered EWA" is not credit, while about 12 states have enacted EWA-specific laws. Some cap fees, and Maryland bans tips from Oct 1, 2026.

**Sweepstakes casinos**
- Statutory bans most consistently listed: California, Connecticut, Indiana, Louisiana, Maine, Montana, New Jersey, New York and Tennessee. ### Inferences
- Sweepstakes casinos are moving out of the grey area into clear illegality in a growing number of states. Remaining operators rely on geofencing and a shrinking list of permissive states. Payment processors and suppliers face derivative risk where statutes such as California's reach facilitators.
- For EWA, tip-based monetization is the main pressure point. Maryland bans tips and Indiana requires a $0 default, so tip-funded direct-to-consumer EWA models need to move to subscription or fee structures that fit state caps.

## 6. What startups are doing and key risks

### Takeaway
Sourced evidence shows four startup approaches. (1) Acquire or obtain federal licenses to claim federal preemption: Polymarket bought QCEX, and Crypto.com, Bridge/Stripe and others sought OCC trust charters. (2) Sue states pre-emptively: Kalshi in AZ, NJ, NV, OH and TN, and Polymarket in MA. (3) Push product boundaries during federal deregulation: parlay contracts, and EWA built around the CFPB's Dec 2025 safe harbor. (4) Sign bilateral data deals: Plaid with JPMorgan. The key risks are state enforcement (including criminal), a SCOTUS ruling, reversal under a future administration or CFPB, and per-state compliance costs.

### Cited Findings
- Polymarket regained US access by buying a CFTC-registered exchange (QCEX, $112M, July 2025) and then self-certified new sports product types (May 2026). — [Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/264161/polymarket-adds-multi-leg-contracts-as-it-chases-kalshi-sportsbooks/); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/polymarket-poised-re-enter-us-market-amid-kalshis-ongoing-legal-battles-gaming)
- Kalshi uses pre-emptive federal suits against states (e.g., Arizona, Mar 12, 2026), which prompted a criminal response. — [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/03/17/politics/arizona-charges-prediction-market-kalshi)
- Eleven crypto and fintech firms sought OCC national trust charters within about 83 days in early 2026. — [FinTech Weekly](https://www.fintechweekly.com/news/occ-national-trust-bank-charter-crypto-fintech-2026); [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/fintechs-asking-for-and-receiving-bank-charters-in-2026)
- Aggregators have accepted paid bilateral bank data-access agreements (JPMorgan–Plaid, Sept 2025) rather than wait for 1033. — [Open Banking Tracker](https://www.openbankingtracker.com/guides/open-banking-data-access-fees) (secondary)
- Industry is pushing for secure-access standards while the 1033 rewrite is pending. — [PYMNTS (2026)](https://www.pymnts.com/legal/bank-regulation/2026/data-aggregators-push-secure-access-as-rule-1033-rewrite-looms/)
- In alternative-access discussions, BettorEdge is promoted as a "Kalshi alternative" after the 6th Circuit ruling. — [Prediction News, Oct 8, 2026](https://predictionnews.com/story/bettoredge-lists-kalshi-alternatives-for-sports-and-event-contract-trading)

### Inferences
- **What could close the gaps:**
  - Prediction markets: a SCOTUS decision on the NJ, NV or Crypto.com petitions; a CFTC final rule under Rule 40.11; or federal legislation.
  - CFPB: the en banc D.C. Circuit ruling, H.R. 10184, and Senate confirmation of Brian Johnson.
  - Open banking: a re-proposed and final 1033 rule addressing fees, plus the result of the E.D. Ky. case.
  - BaaS: an FDIC decision on the custodial-recordkeeping rule.
  - Sweepstakes and EWA: continuing state legislation.
- **Key risks:**
  - Statutes of limitations outlast the current deregulatory window.
  - State AGs can enforce the CFPA and state UDAP laws.
  - Criminal exposure exists in some states for event contracts (Arizona).
  - Bank partners may tighten terms because of consent orders.
  - Charter approvals could be challenged or slowed by a change in OCC leadership.

- I found no reliable sourced data on startup failures or exits caused by these regulatory shifts in 2026.
- UK and EU developments were not researched. None surfaced as highly relevant in these searches.
