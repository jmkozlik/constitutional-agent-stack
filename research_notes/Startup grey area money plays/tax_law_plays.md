# Business Opportunities from 2025–2026 US Tax Law Changes (OBBBA, P.L. 119-21), status as of Oct 9, 2026

Scope note: Research was done on Oct 9, 2026. Each finding is dated to its source. "Inferences" covers the business-model analysis: startup costs, windows and risks that I reasoned out but that no source states. The search tool was rate-limited partway through, so some provisions have thinner sourcing (see the Gaps sections). Several sources are vendor or marketing blogs (beancount.io, striketax, onshore, bnbcalc). I flag them where they are used.

## 1. 100% bonus depreciation and Section 174A domestic R&E expensing: who sells into it?

### Takeaway
Both are verified, permanent provisions. The R&D window for small businesses was a one-time event, and its main retroactive-election deadline passed in July 2026. What remains is ongoing work: 2025-onward expensing elections, credit studies and cost segregation, which is now a permanent market rather than a sunset rush. Cost segregation for short-term rentals and small commercial real estate (CRE) is the clearest small-operator service play.

### Cited Findings
**Bonus depreciation**
- OBBBA permanently restores 100% first-year bonus depreciation for qualifying property acquired/placed in service after Jan 19, 2025 — [Citizens Private Banking](https://www.citizensbank.com/private-banking/insights/obbba-cre-tax-incentives.aspx); [ECJ Law](https://www.ecjlaw.com/clientalert-252)
- Improvements to real estate made after Jan 19, 2025 with a recovery period of 20 years or less qualify for 100% bonus — [CBIZ](https://www.cbiz.com/insights/article/questions-and-answers-breaking-down-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-act)
- IRS Notice 2026-11 reportedly clarified application. In April 2026 trade press reported that cost-segregation demand "surges in 2026" — [BreakingAC, Apr 17 2026](https://breakingac.com/news/2026/apr/17/with-100-bonus-depreciation-now-permanent-cost-segregation-demand-surges-in-2026/) (secondary source; I did not read Notice 2026-11 itself)
- Vendor pricing: cost segregation studies cost $3,000–$15,000, with most short-term rentals (STRs) at $4,000–$8,000. A $500k STR is claimed to generate $100k–$200k in year-1 deductions (marketing estimate) — [bnbcalc (vendor)](https://www.bnbcalc.com/blog/real-estate-tax/rental-income-tax/cost-segregation-to-maximize-bonus-depreciation)
- RSM says taxpayers must model bonus depreciation, 174A expensing and the restored depreciation/amortization add-back to the §163(j) interest limitation together — [RSM](https://rsmus.com/insights/tax-alerts/2025/irs-obbba-acceleration-election-procedures.html)

**Section 174A (domestic R&E)**
- New §174A allows immediate deduction of domestic R&E for tax years beginning after Dec 31, 2024. Foreign R&E stays at 15-year amortization. Taxpayers may elect ≥60-month amortization under §174A(c) instead — [Rev. Proc. 2025-28 (IRS)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-28.pdf); [EY Tax News](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2025-1914-irs-issues-guidance-on-obbba-elections-and-method-changes-for-research-or-experimental-expenditures)
- Small-business retroactive election: taxpayers with average gross receipts ≤$31M (the §448(c) test) may apply §174A retroactively to tax years beginning after Dec 31, 2021. If they elect it, they must amend 2022 and 2023 as applicable — [Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.com/insights/alerts/tax/2025/flash/irs-issues-procedural-guidance)
- All taxpayers may deduct unamortized 2022–2024 domestic R&E either fully in 2025 or ratably over 2025–2026 — [Rev. Proc. 2025-28](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-28.pdf)
- Rev. Proc. 2025-28 (effective Aug 28, 2025) says "the 1-year period beginning on July 4, 2025, ends on July 3, 2026" for the elections. Practitioner sources cite a "July 6, 2026" deadline, likely because of a weekend roll-forward — [Rev. Proc. 2025-28](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-28.pdf); [CLA](https://www.claconnect.com/en/resources/articles/26/r-d-credit-deadline); [CPA Practice Advisor, May 5 2026](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/05/05/the-rd-tax-credit-window-is-closing-what-cpas-must-do-before-july-6-and-what-comes-next/182683/). **The deadlines conflict by a few days, but both have passed as of Oct 2026.**
- Retroactive 174A also triggers retroactive amended §280C(c), which forces a trade-off between reducing the credit and reducing the deduction. EY says the simpler accelerate-unamortized-amount route may be preferable — [EY](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2025-1914-irs-issues-guidance-on-obbba-elections-and-method-changes-for-research-or-experimental-expenditures)
- Practitioners say a missed §41 R&D credit for 2022–2024 can still be claimed by amended return within the statute of limitations. This is separate from the 174A election. Outside dates cited for extended 2022 returns: Sep 15, 2026 (partnerships/S corps) and Oct 16, 2026 (C corps). Dates conflict across sources — [Strike Tax (vendor)](https://www.striketax.com/journal/r-d-tax-credit-refunds-on-amended-returns-the-2022-2023-and-2024-windows-explained); [Onshore (vendor)](https://onshore.com/blog/rd-credit-deadline)
- Sec. 174A planning is now nuanced, involving §59(e) elections and interactions with CAMT and §163(j). Immediate expensing is "might not be the optimal treatment" for some taxpayers — [The Tax Adviser, May 2026](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2026/may/practical-sec-174a-tax-planning-in-the-post-obbba-landscape/)

### Inferences
- **Cost segregation for STR/small CRE owners** is the most accessible service play. Who pays: STR investors and small landlords, at $3k–$15k per study. Startup cost is low, roughly $10k–$50k for software, an engineer or estimator partner, and CPA referral channels. Because bonus depreciation is now permanent, demand will be durable but competitive (many incumbents). The edge is low-cost "desk-based" studies for sub-$1M properties, sold through STR communities and CPAs. Risks: IRS scrutiny of STR "loophole" material-participation claims; studies of poor quality; liability for unsupported allocations.
- **R&D credit / 174A compliance SaaS for startups and CPAs.** The retroactive rush is largely over (the window closed July 2026). The ongoing market is annual §41 credit studies plus 174A/59(e)/280C modeling, payroll-tax offset for startups, and selling to CPA firms. Incumbents I know of from prior knowledge, not verified in this research, include MainStreet, Neo.Tax, Gusto/Pilot R&D add-ons, Strike Tax and Onshore. The remaining 2022 amended-credit windows close in Sep–Oct 2026. Risks: the IRS has tightened §41 refund-claim documentation; contingency-fee models draw scrutiny.
- **Equipment financing / Section 179 + bonus marketplaces** (used equipment, ag, trucking, solar-adjacent) benefit from permanent 100% expensing. This is capital-intensive and better suited to brokers or referral models than to lending.

### Gaps
- I did not read IRS Notice 2026-11 itself, and found no independent market-size data for cost segregation.
- I could not verify the current status of named R&D-credit startups (MainStreet, Neo.Tax) through search.
- The Section 179 limit changes ($2.5M per OBBBA, per my prior knowledge) were not verified in this research.

## 2. QSBS expansion (§1202): fundraising and structuring plays

### Takeaway
The changes are verified for stock issued after July 4, 2025: a $15M cap (or 10x basis), a $75M gross-asset test, both inflation-indexed from 2027, and a tiered 50/75/100% exclusion at 3/4/5 years. These make C-corp structuring more attractive and create advisory, "QSBS stacking" and secondary/early-exit plays.

### Cited Findings
- The per-issuer exclusion cap rises from $10M to $15M for post-enactment stock, inflation-indexed from 2027. The cap remains the greater of that amount or 10x basis — [Dickinson Wright](https://www.dickinson-wright.com/news-alerts/client-alert-federici-obbb-expands-tax-incentives); [Withum](https://www.withum.com/resources/new-legislation-expands-qualified-small-business-stock-exclusion/)
- The gross-asset test rises from $50M to $75M, indexed from 2027 — [Spencer Fane](https://www.spencerfane.com/insight/the-one-big-beautiful-bill-act-significantly-expands-qualified-small-business-stock-benefits/)
- Tiered exclusion: 50% after 3 years, 75% after 4, 100% after 5. Pre-enactment stock keeps the old rules. Most sources say the rules apply to stock issued after July 4, 2025, and one says "on or after July 5" (same effect) — [Lowenstein](https://www.lowenstein.com/news-insights/publications/client-alerts/one-big-beautiful-bill-and-opportunities-to-avoid-or-defer-tax-on-gains-te); [Surgent](https://blog.surgentcpe.com/section-1202-qsbs-2026-obbba-changes-)
- The non-excluded portion of a 3- or 4-year gain is reportedly taxed at 28% plus 3.8% NIIT (not verified against the statute). Non-conforming states such as California still tax QSBS gains — summarized from [Surgent](https://blog.surgentcpe.com/section-1202-qsbs-2026-obbba-changes) and the [beancount.io blog](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/06/04/section-1202-qsbs-after-one-big-beautiful-bill-act-tiered-holding-period-15-million-exclusion-75-million-gross-asset-threshold-stacking-strategy-founders-angel-investors-guide) (secondary)
- QSBS treatment in pass-through structures is an active practitioner topic as of Sep 2026 — [The Tax Adviser, Sep 2026](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2026/sep/navigating-the-qsbs-rules-in-pass-through-structures/)

### Inferences
- **QSBS compliance and attestation SaaS.** Founders and investors need a contemporaneous record of the gross-asset test at each issuance, of the active-business test, and of holding periods across three tiers. Customers: startups, VCs, angel syndicates and fund admins. Cost to build: low to moderate (software plus attorney partnerships). Incumbents to check include Carta (cap-table QSBS tracking) and law firms. The window is long-term because the provision is permanent.
- **Structuring advisory.** This covers LLC-to-C-corp conversions, "stacking" through non-grantor trusts and gifts to multiply the $15M cap, and exit timing at 3/4/5 years. The fee-based niche has a heavy legal-liability component and needs licensed professionals. Risk: IRS challenges to stacking trusts.
- **Fundraising angle.** Angel and rolling funds can market "QSBS-eligible" deals, and the 3-year 50% tier makes earlier secondaries more tax-efficient, which plausibly increases the market for early secondaries. This is speculative.
- **State arbitrage risk.** In CA, PA, AL and MS the exclusion does not apply (per my prior knowledge; only CA was cited above).

### Gaps
- I did not verify statute text for the 28% rate on the non-excluded portion, or the exact effective-date wording, against congress.gov.
- I found no data on how many companies converted to C-corps after OBBBA.

## 3. Opportunity zones made permanent (OZ 2.0)

### Takeaway
OZ 2.0 is verified. New 10-year zone maps take effect Jan 1, 2027, governors designated in mid/late 2026, and the eligibility income threshold tightens to 70% of area median income (AMI). OZ 1.0 zones accept new investment only through Dec 31, 2026. The fall 2026 transition creates fund-formation, map-data and compliance-SaaS opportunities. Rural-specific details were not confirmed in this research.

### Cited Findings
- New zones take effect Jan 1, 2027 and run 10 years. Governors began nominating tracts July 1, 2026, and Treasury certifies them — [California GO-Biz](https://business.ca.gov/?p=80605)
- The governor designation deadline conflicts across sources: one gives July 1, 2026 ([America First Policy](https://www.americafirstpolicy.com/issues/from-policy-to-practice-opportunity-zones-2.0-reforms-and-a-state-blueprint-for-impact)), others give Sep 29/30, 2026, citing Rev. Proc. 2026-14 ([Maryland Counties, May 7 2026](https://conduitstreet.mdcounties.org/2026/05/07/opportunity-zones-2-0-webinar-to-guide-local-govs-through-new-designation-process/); [JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/new-opportunity-zone-2-0-designation-1178450/)). The later dates fit the more specific sources.
- Under OZ 1.0, existing zones remain open for new investment only through Dec 31, 2026 — [Duane Morris OZ blog](https://blogs.duanemorris.com/opportunityzones/)
- The income threshold drops from 80% to 70% of AMI and the contiguous-tract option is eliminated. The eligible pool shrinks about 25%, and governors may designate up to 25% of eligible tracts — [NADO](https://www.nado.org/opportunity-zones-2-0-resource-guide/); [CA GO-Biz](https://business.ca.gov/?p=80605)
- Example: Maryland may designate up to 113 tracts, down from 149 — [Maryland Counties](https://conduitstreet.mdcounties.org/2026/05/07/opportunity-zones-2-0-webinar-to-guide-local-govs-through-new-designation-process/)
- Kiplinger says the new rules will "supercharge" rural investment but gave no specifics in the snippet — [Kiplinger](https://www.kiplinger.com/taxes/tax-planning/how-governors-pick-opportunity-zone-2-designations)

### Inferences
- **OZ 2.0 fund formation and admin.** Small sponsors can form single-asset QOFs for 2027 deals. Fund admin and compliance SaaS (90% asset test, substantial-improvement tracking and the new reporting requirements) is a recurring-revenue play. Who pays: sponsors and investors with capital gains. Startup cost is moderate (legal fees for each fund, roughly $25k–$100k+, an estimate). Window: permanent, but new-deal flow restarts Jan 1, 2027, so the build period is Q4 2026.
- **Designated-tract data and site-selection tools.** Map data on new versus old zones, plus land-banking within newly designated tracts before Jan 2027. Speculative; the risk is that land prices adjust quickly.
- **Rural OZ.** From prior knowledge, not verified here: OBBBA creates "Qualified Rural Opportunity Funds" with a 30% basis step-up (versus 10%) and a reduced substantial-improvement threshold (50%). If accurate, rural ag, housing and manufacturing QOFs are a niche for small regional sponsors.
- **Risk:** a gap year in late 2026, since 1.0 zones close and 2.0 zones are not yet live. Political and administrative delay in Treasury certification is possible.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm rural QROF specifics (step-up %, substantial-improvement threshold) or the final certified 2.0 map status as of Oct 2026.
- The designation deadline conflict was not resolved against Rev. Proc. 2026-14.

## 4. Federal tax-credit scholarship (ECCA / "Education Freedom Tax Credit") and state ESAs (Texas)

### Takeaway
The federal credit is verified: up to $1,700 per taxpayer for cash donations to scholarship-granting organizations (SGOs), starting Jan 1, 2027. 31 states had opted in and 6 had opted out as of Oct 8, 2026. Texas's $1B ESA, administered by Odyssey, was oversubscribed for 2026-27. This creates a 2026–2027 rush to stand up SGOs, donor-acquisition platforms and ESA-eligible providers (microschools, tutoring).

### Cited Findings
- Beginning Jan 1, 2027, individuals can claim a federal credit for cash contributions of up to $1,700 to an SGO — [IRS FSTC page](https://www.irs.gov/government-entities/federal-state-local-governments/federal-scholarship-tax-credit-fstc). The Education Department's fact sheet says "up to $3,400 for married couples filing jointly" — [ED fact sheet](https://www.ed.gov/media/document/education-freedom-tax-credit-fact-sheet-113147.pdf)
- Treasury and IRS issued companion temporary regulations establishing procedures for states and SGOs ahead of the Jan 1, 2027 launch. States must opt in and provide a list of eligible SGOs — [Treasury press release sb0641](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0641); [Treasury sb0527](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0527); [Miller Johnson, ~Oct 6 2026](https://millerjohnson.com/publication/treasury-and-irs-release-guidance-on-new-federal-scholarship-tax-credit-program/)
- As of Oct 8, 2026, 31 states had opted in: VA, CO, NY, AL, AR, FL, GA, ID, IN, IA, LA, MS, MO, MT, NE, NH, ND, OH, OK, SC, SD, TN, TX, UT, WV, WY, KS, KY, NC, AK, NV. 6 had opted out: HI, NM, OR, AZ, WI, MN — [Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/State_participation_in_the_Federal_Scholarship_Tax_Credit). (Notable: blue-leaning CO, NY and VA opted in, while AZ, a school-choice state, is listed as opting out. Worth double-checking.)
- **Texas ESA (TEFA):** By Feb 15, 2026, more than 101,000 students had applied. Funding is capped at $1B for 2026-27, so roughly 90k–100k students will likely be accepted. Amounts: $10,474 for private school, $2,000 for homeschool, up to $30,000 for students with disabilities. Odyssey (NY) administers the program. 80% of applicants planned private school and 20% homeschool or alternatives such as microschools. More than 700 private schools and pre-K providers were approved as of Jan 2026 — [Community Impact, Feb 16 2026](https://communityimpact.com/south-central-austin/texas-legislature/applications-for-texas-education-savings-accounts-set-to-exceed-available-funding/); [Community Impact, Jan 12 2026](https://beta2.communityimpact.com/austin/south-central-austin/texas-legislature/2026/01/12/over-700-texas-private-schools-pre-k-providers-approved-for-education-savings-account-program)

### Inferences
- **SGO formation and operations.** Nonprofit SGOs collect donations, and the 100% dollar-for-dollar credit makes fundraising easy for donors. A for-profit operator's play is SGO back-office SaaS (donor receipts, IRS reporting, state-list compliance, family eligibility verification) or management services. Startup cost is low (software). The window is now: SGOs must be listed by opt-in states before 2027 donations.
- **Donor-acquisition marketplaces** ("give $1,700, get $1,700 back") are similar to Georgia/Arizona state-credit SGO marketing. Fees are likely restricted by regulation (check the temp regs).
- **Microschools and tutoring as ESA vendors.** Texas pays $10,474 per private-school student. Startup cost for a microschool is roughly $20k–$150k (lease, accreditation if required, insurance); this is an estimate. Risks: state accreditation and vendor-approval rules, Odyssey payment timing (funds available in April 2027 per the article), political reversal, and oversubscription caps.
- **ESA platform administrators** (Odyssey, ClassWallet, Student First, from prior knowledge) are an incumbent-heavy B2G market that is hard for small operators. Better plays are vendor marketplaces and compliance tools for schools accepting ESA funds.
- **Risk:** opt-in is year-by-year and politically contingent. The federal credit has a national cap (the statute does not fix it per state; check). IRS SGO requirements (e.g., serving ≥10 students, income limits of 300% of AMI) are from prior knowledge and not verified here.

### Gaps
- Final Texas acceptance numbers for 2026-27 and the 2027-28 application status were not found. The Feb 2026 data was the latest available.
- I did not read the SGO temp-reg details (fee caps, eligibility, income limits).
- The opt-in deadline for states was not found.

## 5. "Trump Accounts" (§530A)

### Takeaway
The accounts are verified and live. They launched July 4, 2026, carry a $1,000 Treasury seed for children born 2025–2028, and more than 60M children were auto-enrolled by Oct 1, 2026. Treasury chose BNY and Robinhood as the financial agent, trustee and brokerage, so the core rails are taken. Startup opportunity lies in claim/activation assistance, employer-contribution administration, philanthropy and stock-donation tooling, and payroll W-2 code TA support.

### Cited Findings
- Accounts launched July 4, 2026 and are open to any US child under 18 with an SSN. The $1,000 pilot contribution covers children born Jan 1, 2025–Dec 31, 2028 who are US citizens — [CNBC, Oct 1 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/01/treasury-trump-accounts-auto-enroll-more-than-60-million-children.html); [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/trumpaccounts); [Treasury sb0508](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0508)
- More than 60M children were auto-enrolled under proposed regs released about Sep 29, 2026. Families must still claim the account in the Trump Accounts app to get the $1,000 seed. Before auto-enrollment, 7–8M children had signed up as of mid-September (Bessent) — [CNBC, Oct 1 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/01/treasury-trump-accounts-auto-enroll-more-than-60-million-children.html)
- Temporary regs (Federal Register, Sep 30, 2026) allow stock donations, including individual stocks, generally with a 5-year hold. Michael and Susan Dell committed $6.25B, which provides $250 to children born 2016–2024 in ZIP codes with median income ≤$150k — same CNBC source
- Commonwealth estimates 14.4M children eligible for the seed. At EITC-like take-up, about 20% would go unclaimed, roughly $2.88B among low- and moderate-income children. Only 5% of LMI families had opened accounts before auto-enrollment — same CNBC source
- Treasury picked BNY as financial agent, and Robinhood as brokerage and initial trustee, which is also building the white-label, Treasury-owned app (announced ~Apr 6, 2026). Individual contributions are capped at $5,000/yr — [Robinhood newsroom](https://robinhood.com/us/en/newsroom/robinhood-tapped-as-brokerage-for-trump-accounts-together-with-bny); [BNY press release](https://www.bny.com/content/bnymellon/global/en/about-us/newsroom/press-release/bny-named-financial-agent-for-trump-accounts.html); [401k Specialist](https://401kspecialistmag.com/treasury-department-pins-bny-robinhood-for-trump-account-management/)
- The draft 2026 Form W-2 adds Box 12 code "TA" for employer Trump account contributions — [Thomson Reuters](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/news/payroll-professionals-weigh-overtime-reporting-changes-as-related-irs-drafts-forms-surface/)

### Inferences
- **Claim/activation outreach as a service.** Hospitals, VITA sites, Medicaid MCOs, banks, credit unions and nonprofits could pay to help families claim the seed; about $2.88B is at risk of going unclaimed. Who pays: philanthropy and grants, or institutions seeking customer acquisition. Low cost. Risk: Treasury app and auto-enrollment may make intermediaries unnecessary.
- **Employer benefit administration.** Employers can contribute (the $2,500 per-employee exclusion is from prior knowledge and was not confirmed in this search). This creates a payroll/benefits add-on for small employers: plan documents, contribution remittance and W-2 code TA. Incumbents: payroll providers (Gusto, ADP) will likely bundle it.
- **Philanthropic giving rails.** Donor-advised "seed a ZIP code" programs and stock-donation processing are now permitted under the Sep 2026 regs.
- **Rollover and investment products after age 18.** This is a long-dated play; Robinhood holds the incumbent position. Risk: the program is politically branded and could change after an election.

### Gaps
- The employer contribution limit ($2,500) and whether third-party trustees beyond Robinhood will be allowed later were not confirmed.

## 6. No tax on tips / overtime deductions (2025–2028)

### Takeaway
These are verified temporary deductions (tax years 2025–2028; the statute sunsets after 2028, per my prior knowledge). The forcing function for software vendors is new 2026 W-2 reporting: Box 12 codes TP and TT, and a Treasury occupation code in Box 14b. Tip/overtime calculation, occupation coding and Schedule 1-A prep are the opportunity, with a window of about 3 tax years.

### Cited Findings
- Draft 2026 Forms W-2/W-2c add Box 12 codes TP (qualified tips), TT (qualified overtime) and TA (Trump account), and split Box 14 into 14a/14b, with 14b holding the Treasury tipped-occupation code. Employees claim the deductions on Schedule 1-A — [NATP](https://www.natptax.com/news-insights/blog/draft-2026-form-w-2-adds-codes-for-tips-overtime-and-occupation-data/); [Thomson Reuters](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/news/payroll-professionals-weigh-overtime-reporting-changes-as-related-irs-drafts-forms-surface/)
- The 2025 W-2 was unchanged, and employers could use reasonable estimation for 2025 (IRS Notice 2025-62 transition relief) — [Oracle PeopleSoft blog](https://blogs.oracle.com/peoplesoft/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-no-tax-on-tips-and-no-tax-on-overtime-what-employers-need-to-do-in-2025-and-2026); [Bloomberg Tax](https://news.bloombergtax.com/tax-insights-and-commentary/employers-need-to-prepare-now-for-no-tax-on-tips-compliance)
- The overtime deduction is capped at $12,500 ($25,000 joint), phases out above $150k MAGI ($300k joint), and covers only the FLSA-required premium portion — [Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/insights/get-pdf/29677)
- Toast Payroll computes the qualified OT premium with its own formula and needs weekly hours; it is one vendor's interpretation — [Toast support](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Toast-Payroll-No-Tax-on-Tips-and-or-Overtime?lang=en_US)
- Treasury issued a preliminary tipped-occupations list. It remains unclear which "tip-like" payments qualify — [Cherry Bekaert](https://www.cbh.com/insights/articles/how-no-tax-on-overtime-tips-affects-employer-wage-reporting/)

### Inferences
- **Add-on compliance middleware.** Tip-pooling and occupation-code mapping for restaurants, salons and gig platforms; FLSA overtime-premium calculators for multi-state and fluctuating-workweek employers; and a "qualified tips" ledger for 1099 workers (rideshare, delivery), who need self-tracking. Who pays: SMB employers (per-employee per-month pricing), gig workers (consumer app, roughly $5–20/yr) and tax preparers. Startup cost is low (software).
- **Consumer tax-prep niche.** Schedule 1-A for tipped and OT workers could be a lead-gen funnel for preparers.
- **Window:** tax years 2025–2028, so about 3 more filing seasons. Risk: big payroll and POS vendors (Toast, Gusto, ADP, Square) already absorb this, so a standalone startup must target gaps such as 1099 tip workers or complex OT.

### Gaps
- The tips deduction cap ($25k) and phase-out were not re-verified in this pass (prior knowledge: $25k, phase-out above $150k/$300k MAGI).
- Final 2026 W-2 instructions and the final occupation list were not confirmed.

## 7. HSA changes and direct primary care

### Takeaway
From Jan 1, 2026, a qualifying DPC arrangement no longer disqualifies HSA eligibility, and DPC fees can be paid from an HSA. Bronze and catastrophic marketplace plans are treated as HSA-compatible, and the telehealth safe harbor is permanent. This lowers the barrier for DPC practices and for "HSA plus DPC" bundles sold to the self-employed and small employers. Sourcing in this pass is thin (one secondary blog).

### Cited Findings
- OBBBA §71308: from Jan 1, 2026, a qualified DPC arrangement is disregarded for HSA eligibility, and DPC fees can be paid tax-free from an HSA. IRS implementation guidance was Notice 2026-05 (Dec 2025). Typical fees are about $50–$150 per adult per month — [beancount.io (secondary)](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/06/04/direct-primary-care-hsa-integration-obbba-2026-self-employed-freelancers-solo-s-corp-owners-small-employers-150-monthly-fee-bronze-catastrophic-marketplace-telehealth-safe-harbor-irs-notice-2026-05-guide) (single secondary source; the URL also references bronze/catastrophic marketplace plans and the telehealth safe harbor)

### Inferences
- From prior knowledge, not verified here: the statutory DPC fee cap is $150/month individual and $300/month family, indexed. Bronze and catastrophic exchange plans are HSA-compatible from 2026, and pre-deductible telehealth coverage is permanently allowed.
- **DPC clinic.** A physician-owned DPC practice costs roughly $50k–$250k to start (an estimate) and pays out through membership revenue. The new HSA compatibility removes the main objection from HSA-holding employers.

## 8. 529 expansion, NFA $0 tax, and other notable provisions

### Takeaway
Verified: the NFA $200 tax on suppressors and SBRs/SBSs is $0 from Jan 1, 2026, which drove a large demand surge (150,000+ eForms on Jan 1; NFA checks up 177% y/y in June 2026). The 529 K-12 limit is $20,000 from 2026. A 1% remittance excise applies from 2026. The gambling-loss deduction is limited to 90% from 2026, with repeal bills pending. A tiered endowment tax tops out at 8%.

### Cited Findings
- **NFA:** ATF processed more than 150,000 eForms on Jan 1, 2026, versus about 2,500/day normally; the count included suppressors, SBRs and SBSs — [Pew Pew Tactical](https://pewpewtactical.com/atf-struggles-150000-eform-submissions-day-1/); [LSO News](https://www.lsonews.com/150000-in-one-day-suppressor-applications-surge/). NSSF reported NFA background checks of 166,677 in June 2026 versus 60,147 in June 2025, a 177% increase — [NSSF](https://www.nssf.org/articles/if-you-havent-heard-suppressor-sales-are-booming/). Approval times reported by TFB: eForm 4 at about 10 days for individuals and 26 days for trusts (date of snapshot unclear) — [The Firearm Blog](https://www.thefirearmblog.com/blog/silencer-saturday-427-2026-atf-nfa-data-44827597). An unverified claim of "1 million+ NFA forms in Q1" — [gunstuff.tv](https://gunstuff.tv/suppressor-sales-skyrocket-in-2026-over-1-million-nfa-forms-processed-in-q1-alone/) — is not corroborated. (Note: registration requirements and the $200 tax on machine guns and destructive devices remain, per prior knowledge.)
- **529:** K-12 distribution limit is $20,000 per beneficiary per year for distributions after Dec 31, 2025 — [beancount.io (secondary)](https://beancount.io/it/blog/2026/09/15/529-plans-2026-expansion-20000-k12-tutoring-apprenticeship-guide). 529 funds became usable for additional purposes effective July 4, 2025. ### Inferences
- **NFA:** the plays are FFL/SOT dealers and "suppressor-as-a-service" retail, NFA trust and eForm filing services, and suppressor manufacturing and machining. The initial January 2026 spike is a one-time pull-forward; steady-state demand is higher but the land-grab is over. Risks: ATF processing capacity, state bans (suppressors are illegal in about 8 states, per prior knowledge), litigation over NFA registration, and reputational and payment-processor de-risking. Licensing (FFL/SOT) takes months; startup cost is moderate.
- **529 K-12 expansion** makes tutoring, curriculum, testing and dual-enrollment vendors 529-payable, which pairs with the ESA plays in section 4. The opportunity is a payments and eligibility layer letting tutoring vendors accept 529 money (similar to ESA marketplaces).
- **Remittance tax:** the 1% applies only to cash-funded transfers, so it favors bank- or card-funded digital remitters over cash agents (my reading of the "cash or similar" limitation). There is a conversion opportunity for fintechs onboarding cash-dependent immigrant customers to bank accounts. Risk: compliance and KYC costs.
- **Gambling 90% rule:** creates tax-tracking and reporting tools for professional and high-volume gamblers and DFS/sports-betting players. Window risk: possible repeal.
- **Endowment tax:** advisory work for affected private universities is a big-firm market, with little for small operators.
