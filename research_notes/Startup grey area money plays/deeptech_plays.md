## Drones: who pays, for what, after the Covered List

### Takeaway
The Covered List plus new 2026 layers (a component-level FCC order effective Oct 13, 2026, and Section 232 drone tariffs from Sep 3, 2026) turn demand toward NDAA/Blue-compliant components and keep existing DJI fleets in service longer. The lowest-capital plays are services around installed fleets (repair, batteries, parts, compliance/transition consulting, training) and selling detection to newly authorized local agencies paid for by FEMA grants. The capital-heavy plays are whole airframes for Drone Dominance at fixed prices that fall every phase.

### Cited Findings
- DJI has been on the FCC Covered List since Dec 22, 2025. That restricts new authorizations, imports and marketing, not owning or flying existing units. Security/firmware updates for previously authorized aircraft are protected under FCC Public Notice DA 26-454 until at least Jan 1, 2029 — [DroneXL](https://dronexl.co/es/dji/)
- A separate FCC order (FCC 26-50) blocks new equipment authorizations for drones containing covered logic-bearing components: flight controllers, radio modules, camera boards and gimbal controllers. It reportedly takes effect Oct 13, 2026. Already-authorized equipment is unaffected and pending applications are grandfathered — [Camera Jabber](https://camerajabber.com/?p=1424571)
- A Section 232 proclamation signed Aug 13, 2026 imposes tariffs from Sep 3, 2026. The rate is 100% on drones over 55 lb, thermal-equipped aircraft, docks and listed components, and 25% on nearly everything else — [DroneXL](https://dronexl.co/es/dji/) (single secondary source; verify against Federal Register)
- DroneXL argues the real medium-term risk for DJI owners is parts, batteries and repair, not a remote kill switch. This is analysis; I found no measured shortage data — [DroneXL](https://dronexl.co/es/dji/)
- The FCC carve-outs for Blue UAS and Buy American "domestic end products" are described as temporary, to be reassessed before a Jan 1, 2027 deadline — [Inside Unmanned Systems via search snippet](https://insideunmannedsystems.com/?p=42767); [UC ANR](https://ucanr.edu/blog/salinas-valley-agriculture/article/updated-fcc-covered-list-means-ag-drone-users)
- Pentagon component rules cover 13 areas: flight controllers, GNSS, motors, batteries, propellers and others. Requirements tighten on a schedule through Aug 2027. NDAA compliance is necessary but not sufficient for the Blue List. DCMA took over the Blue List from DIU in Dec 2025 — [Inside Unmanned Systems (snippet; page blocked to fetch)](https://insideunmannedsystems.com/?p=42767)
- CATL's listing as a Chinese Military Company (Jan 2025) triggered a DoD contracting bar effective Jun 30, 2026. That creates demand for non-Chinese drone battery cells and packs — [Inside Unmanned Systems (snippet)](https://insideunmannedsystems.com/?p=42767)
- The Army's Tobyhanna depot began producing brushless drone motors, with a ribbon cutting on Jun 30, 2026. This is government in-sourcing of a component small vendors might otherwise sell — [DCMA](https://www.dcma.mil/News/Article/4560260/army-made-motors-power-historic-game-of-drones-flight/)
- Hoverfly launched NDAA-compliant, Blue-routed components including GPS modules (Jul 4, 2026). It cited a US drone component market of about $5.9B in 2025 (company claim) — [DroneXL](https://dronexl.co/2026/07/04/hoverfly-launches-ndaa-compliant-drone-components/)
- **Drone Dominance economics:**
  - Phase I: 25 vendors named Feb 3, 2026; up to 12 to produce 30,000 units by July at an average of about $5,000 per drone, about $150M in total — [Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/air/2026/02/03/pentagon-taps-25-firms-for-small-cheap-attack-drone-competition/)
  - Later gauntlets cut vendors from 12 to 5 as orders rise to 150,000 and unit price falls to about $2,300 — [Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/air/2026/02/03/pentagon-taps-25-firms-for-small-cheap-attack-drone-competition/)
  - Named invitees include Neros, Auterion, Firestorm, Dzyne, Vector, Perform, Halo Aeronautics and Kratos SRE — [Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/air/2026/02/03/pentagon-taps-25-firms-for-small-cheap-attack-drone-competition/); [Tectonic Defense](https://www.tectonicdefense.com/icymi-pentagon-announces-drone-dominance-vendors/)
- **Drone Dominance Phase II:**
  - Request for solutions posted Apr 21, 2026, revised May 5, 2026, via NSTXL (an OTA consortium) — [NSTXL](https://nstxl.org/opportunity/drone-dominance)
  - At least $300M in prototype delivery orders. About 30,000 drones at fixed prices: $4,500 long-range, $3,500 close-quarters, $3,250 per munition. The program totals about $1.1B over two years across four phases — [sUAS News](https://www.suasnews.com/2026/04/department-of-war-launches-phase-two-of-1-1bn-drone-dominance-programme/)
  - I found no Phase II winners announced as of Oct 2026.
- **Counter-UAS buyers and funding:**
  - FEMA's FY2026 C-UAS Grant Program funds SLTT purchases of detection and tracking technology. FY2026 eligibility is limited to the State Administrative Agencies of 11 states, including TX, FL, NY and CA. Missouri's period of performance runs Mar 2026–Sep 2027 — [Missouri DPS NOFO](https://dps.mo.gov/dir/programs/ohs/grantstraining/documents/fy-2026-c-uas-notice-of-funding-opportunity.pdf); [Mass.gov FAQ](https://www.mass.gov/doc/cuasgp-frequently-asked-questions/download)
  - FEMA has distributed half of a $500M OBBBA counter-drone appropriation to host jurisdictions — [CRS IF13279 (Jul 29, 2026)](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-07-29_IF13279_997b9410e5f148a36c703f89b6d55be3e29ac43c.pdf)
  - NYPD bought $6.5M of drone mitigation equipment and stood up a permanent counter-UAS unit under SAFER SKIES (2026) — [Robotics.press](https://robotics.press/news/nypd-6-5m-counter-drone-unit-safer-skies/); [Inside Unmanned Systems](https://insideunmannedsystems.com/?p=42408)
  - Texas DIR has DroneShield on a statewide law-enforcement purchasing vehicle valid to Feb 2027. Its offerings include eight-week threat-assessment packages and turnkey detect/track/defeat systems — [Unmanned Airspace](https://www.unmannedairspace.info/counter-uas-systems-and-policies/state-of-texas-signs-five-year-framework-agreement-with-drone-shield-for-counter-drone-technology/)
  - Federal side: the Army awarded AeroVironment a potential $500M commercial counter-drone contract on Jul 1, 2026 — [Soldier Systems (via search)](https://soldiersystems.net/?p=209875)
  - SAFER SKIES authority is conditioned on training and certification under the Jul 2026 DOJ/DHS IFR — [Federal Register](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-07-06/html/2026-13609.htm)
- **Drone-as-first-responder and drone-as-a-service:** Aerodome (DFR) raised a $21.5M Series A led by CRV with a16z; the date was not confirmed in the snippet. Measure (DaaS) raised a $15M Series B (undated) — [DroneLife tag pages](https://dronelife.com/tag/measure/page/6). I found no published DFR subscription pricing.

### Inferences
**Lowest-capital plays (roughly $50k–$500k, inference)**

- **DJI fleet sustainment.** Many fleets are in ag spraying, inspection and public safety. Services include:
  - repair depots
  - third-party and refurbished batteries
  - parts brokering of already-authorized stock
  - "fleet transition audits" that tell agencies what is legal to keep flying and what must be replaced

  Who pays: fleet operators, ag co-ops, police and fire. The window runs from now until roughly 2029, when the firmware-protection floor ends, but it erodes as fleets age out. The risk is a further FCC or Commerce action, including tariffs on spare parts under the Section 232 list.
- **Training and certification for counter-UAS operators.** SAFER SKIES makes training a precondition, so agencies must pay for it. Vendors can bundle training with sales. Watch for a federally designated curriculum (likely FBI/DHS), which could capture this market. See Gaps.
- **Grant-writing and procurement help for counter-UAS and DFR buyers.** FEMA grant cycles flow through state administrative agencies, and local agencies need help. One option is to work as an integrator or reseller on state vehicles (like Texas DIR) for detection systems.
- **Part 108 readiness consulting and BVLOS ops manuals.** Demand spikes once the final rule publishes. Until then, this is waiver work under Part 107.

**Mid-capital (roughly $1M–$10M, inference): NDAA/Blue-compliant sub-components**

- Candidates include flight controllers, radios, gimbals and camera boards (directly hit by FCC 26-50), GNSS, ESCs, motors and battery packs.
- Customers: Drone Dominance primes, whose $3,250–$4,500 fixed unit prices force cheap compliant bills of materials, plus commercial OEMs re-sourcing.
- Main risk: government in-sourcing (the Tobyhanna motors line), plus the price squeeze as unit costs head to about $2,300.

**Capital-heavy (roughly $10M+): full airframes and loitering munitions for Drone Dominance**

- This is a volume-tournament model: winners shrink from 12 to 5, prices fall, and margins are thin.
- Being a supplier to the winners is safer than competing as an airframe maker.

**Local counter-UAS detection is the most grant-funded civilian opening**

- Detection-only (RF/radar/acoustic) as a subscription or managed service suits small entrants.
- Mitigation is tightly gated by training and certification.

### Gaps
- Primary text of FCC 26-50 and the Section 232 drone proclamation was not fetched. Effective dates rest on secondary sources.
- No measured DJI parts or battery shortage data, and no repair-shop pricing data.
- Inside Unmanned Systems' sourcing-deadline article was blocked from fetch, so the per-component deadline table is unavailable.
- No published DFR or counter-UAS subscription price points.
- No confirmed SLTT counter-UAS training curriculum provider or cost; the IFR's training details were not extracted.
- Drone Dominance Phase I's final 12 winners and Phase II awards were not found.

## Defense procurement reform: how small firms actually get paid

### Takeaway
The path is: SBIR/STTR (back since Apr 2026, now with a $30M "strategic breakthrough" Phase II) or a CSO/OTA prototype through a consortium, then OTA follow-on production. The Nov 2025 Hegseth memos and the FY26 NDAA reward commercial and dual-use content and portfolio-level buyers.

### Cited Findings
- **SBIR/STTR lapse and return:**
  - The programs lapsed Sep 30/Oct 1, 2025. The Senate passed reauthorization Mar 3, 2026 and the House on Mar 17, 2026. The Small Business Innovation and Economic Security Act of 2026 (S. 3971) was signed Apr 13, 2026 and runs through Sep 30, 2031 — [Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/sbirsttr-programs-reauthorized-after-six-month-lapse)
  - New features: a Phase II "strategic breakthrough" vehicle with a ceiling up to $30M, more foreign-risk screening, and leftover FY26 SBIR funds may carry into FY27 — [Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/sbirsttr-programs-reauthorized-after-six-month-lapse); [OpenGrants](https://opengrants.io/sbir-grants-2026-what-changed-whats-next/)
  - DoD resumed SBIR solicitations after reauthorization, while most other agencies had not yet issued new NOFOs as of the report (undated) — [OpenGrants](https://opengrants.io/sbir-grants-2026-what-changed-whats-next/)
  - Some sources mention application caps — [GrantedAI](https://grantedai.com/blog/sbir-sttr-reauthorization-2026-signed-fy2031-strategic-breakthrough-award-30-million-application-caps-foreign-risk-screening-small-business-strategy) (vendor blog; unverified)
- **FY26 NDAA (P.L. 119-60, signed Dec 18, 2025):**
  - Statutorily establishes Portfolio Acquisition Executives (Sec. 1802) — [War on the Rocks](https://warontherocks.com/2025/12/an-industry-guide-to-the-fy2026-ndaa/)
  - Expands commercial-product preferences, though DoD may seek government-purpose data rights even on commercial items — [Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/the-fy-2026-national-defense-authorization-act)
  - Streamlines entry for new and commercial entrants and removes some compliance requirements for small firms — [GovConWire](https://www.govconwire.com/articles/trump-fy26-ndaa-acquisition-reforms)
  - The SASC version proposed removing CSO approval thresholds and allowing direct OTA follow-on production; final text not confirmed — [ExecutiveGov](https://www.executivegov.com/?p=276692)
  - A vendor blog claims the Major System RDT&E threshold rose from $115M to $275M and TINA thresholds rose to $10M — [iQuasar](https://community.iquasar.com/t/fy26-ndaa-just-flipped-the-script-on-defense-acquisition-otas-are-now-the-main-event/358) (unverified)
- **Nov 7, 2025 Hegseth memos:**
  - Rename the system the "Warfighting Acquisition System" and create PAEs with contracting officers reporting to them — [DefenseScoop](https://defensescoop.com/2025/11/07/hegseth-acquisition-transformation-speech-memo-guidance/); [Wiley](https://www.wiley.law/newsletter-Secretary-Hegseth-Issues-Directives-to-Transform-Acquisition-Process-and-Defense-Industrial-Base)
  - Direct maximum use of commercial products, direct-to-supplier relationships and alternative procurement methods — [Wiley](https://www.wiley.law/newsletter-Secretary-Hegseth-Issues-Directives-to-Transform-Acquisition-Process-and-Defense-Industrial-Base)
  - An official said the reforms give commercial and dual-use firms more opportunity than pure-play defense companies — [Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2025/11/hegseth-presses-defense-execs-to-move-faster-in-speech-laying-out-sweeping-acquisition-changes)
- **Live examples of the OTA consortium route:**
  - Drone Dominance Phase II runs through NSTXL — [NSTXL](https://nstxl.org/opportunity/drone-dominance)
  - Janus runs through DIU, up to $2.2B FY27–FY31, contractor-owned and operated — [DefenseScoop](https://defensescoop.com/2026/08/26/army-annouces-initiial-bases-to-host-nuclear-microreactors/)

### Inferences
- The realistic small-firm sequence:
  1. SBIR Phase I (roughly $150k–$300k typical; not sourced here) or a DIU CSO
  2. A prototype OTA through a consortium such as NSTXL
  3. OTA follow-on production, now easier under the NDAA
  4. Subcontracting to a winner as a hedge
- Low-capital "picks and shovels" in procurement:
  - SBIR/OTA proposal-writing and capture services
  - CMMC and foreign-risk-screening compliance help, since the new SBIR foreign-risk screening creates demand
  - NDAA/Blue/FEOC supply-chain provenance tooling
  - Consortium-membership facilitation
- Data rights are a key negotiation risk now that DoD may seek government-purpose rights even on commercial items.

### Gaps
- Enacted FY26 NDAA text on CSO/OTA thresholds and small-business compliance relief not verified.
- No data on 2026 SBIR award volumes or post-lapse funding backlog by agency.
- Implementation status of PAEs by Oct 2026 not confirmed.

## Nuclear supply chain: what suppliers and services are needed

### Takeaway
Reactor developers and the government's fuel programs are not the opening for small entrants. The opening is in what those programs must buy: TRISO and fuel fabrication, HALEU handling, NQA-1-qualified components and forgings, and qualification and licensing services. Certification cost (NQA-1) is the moat and the barrier.

### Cited Findings
- **Janus vendors and sites (Aug 26, 2026):**
  - Antares at Fort Bragg, BWXT at Fort Campbell, General Atomics EMS at Fort Hood, Radiant at Fort Benning, Westinghouse Government Services at Fort Drum — [World Nuclear News](https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/us-army-selects-five-microreactor-vendors-to-power-bases); [Army.mil](https://www.army.mil/article/294891)
  - The Army targets its first reactor by Sep 2028 and more than 20 microreactors once private funding is included; initial units stay contractor-owned and operated — [DefenseScoop](https://defensescoop.com/2026/08/26/army-annouces-initiial-bases-to-host-nuclear-microreactors/)
  - Business model: build-own-operate, with the Army as power buyer.
- **DOE fuel line pilot:**
  - Selected Oklo, Terrestrial Energy, TRISO-X and Valar Atomics, with Standard Nuclear conditionally selected first — [DOE](https://www.energy.gov/articles/energy-department-selects-four-companies-advanced-nuclear-fuel-line-pilot-projects)
  - Applicants bear all construction, operation, decommissioning and feedstock costs. They may get HALEU through DOE's HALEU Availability Program, and must be US-located and majority US-owned — [World Nuclear News](https://world-nuclear-news.org/articles/us-seeks-pilot-production-lines-for-advanced-reactor-fuel)
- **Fuel offtake deals:**
  - Standard Nuclear signed a binding multi-metric-ton TRISO supply agreement with Radiant (Aug 2026) supporting deployments through 2031. Its Oak Ridge facility produces about 0.5 MTU per year and was the first commercial firm to take custody of DOE-distributed HALEU feedstock — [Neutron Bytes / search](https://neutronbytes.com/2026/09/12/centrus-and-radiant-partner-for-haleu-fuel/)
  - Centrus–Radiant HALEU partnership (Sep 12, 2026) — [Neutron Bytes](https://neutronbytes.com/2026/09/12/centrus-and-radiant-partner-for-haleu-fuel/)
  - Kairos finalized a DOE HALEU contract for Hermes (Jan 2026), making TRISO pebbles with LANL — [ANS](https://www.ans.org/news/tag-haleu%20availability%20program/)
- **DOE enrichment and deconversion awards:**
  - Enrichment (Dec 2025): Centrus (ACO), General Matter, Orano Federal Services and Urenco USA (LES) — [ANS (search)](https://www.ans.org/news/tag-haleu%20availability%20program/)
  - Deconversion: six companies were contracted in Oct 2024; names not retrieved.
- **Component bottleneck:**
  - An Aug 2026 NEI report cites a shortage of NQA-1-qualified suppliers, caused by plant shutdowns and the cost of maintaining NQA-1 programs — [Neutron Bytes](https://neutronbytes.com/2026/08/24/the-reactor-isnt-the-bottleneck-the-forge-is/)
  - The large-forging supplier list is anchored by Japan Steel Works and Doosan; no US firm is on it — [Neutron Bytes](https://neutronbytes.com/2026/08/24/the-reactor-isnt-the-bottleneck-the-forge-is/)
  - EPRI: US/Canadian capacity for smaller forgings and piping will be strained — [EPRI](https://ant.epri.com/actions-supply-chain)
  - The Nuclear Company and Nucor are evaluating NQA-1-certified steel (Sep 2025) — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250926200497/en)

### Inferences
**Lower-capital plays (inference)**

- NQA-1 quality-program setup and audit consulting for machine shops entering nuclear
- Third-party inspection, NDE (non-destructive examination) and materials testing
- Licensing and regulatory-documentation services: DOE authorization packages, NRC Part 53 applications, Army/DIU site environmental work
- Radiation-tolerant instrumentation and sensors
- Specialty machining of small reactor internals (microreactors use smaller parts than gigawatt-scale plants)
- Workforce training: reactor operators, radiation protection technicians

Who pays: reactor developers flush with venture money and DIU/Army money. The window is about 2026–2031, tracking the Janus funding years.

**Mid-capital play: an existing precision shop earning NQA-1 certification.** The cost of maintaining a program is the stated barrier, which implies certification capital plus 12–24 months (inference, unsourced).

**Capital-heavy plays: fuel fabrication, deconversion and enrichment.** Customers are concentrated (Radiant and a handful of others), and developers bear the cost under the DOE pilots. Not a small-operator play unless the business is a licensed nuclear-material transport or packaging service.

**Risks**
- Customer concentration and developer failure; few reactors actually sell power before 2028.
- Pilot authority is not commercial licensing; NRC rules are slipping to Mar 2027 (per the earlier report).

## Data-center power: small-player angles in co-location, behind-the-meter gas, interconnection and SB 6

### Takeaway
Pricey, slow interconnection makes "time-to-power" the product. Under the Texas SB 6 draft rules, a 1 GW request would need about $50M of security, plus $100k–$300k study fees and a draft non-refundable $50k/MW fee. That cost of entry makes advisory, pre-screening, powered-land assembly and flexibility software the realistic small-player niches; owning generation is for the well-capitalized.

### Cited Findings
- **Texas SB 6 draft rule (16 TAC §25.194):**
  - PUCT voted Mar 12, 2026 to publish the draft; comments were due Apr 17, 2026. It applies to loads at or above 75 MW — [Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/03/public-utility-commission-of-texas-issues-proposed-rules-for-large-load-interconn/); [Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw.com/es/insights/2026/3/texas-senate-bill-6-update-what-data-centers-large-load-customers-should-know-about-proposed-interconnection-standards)
  - Study fees: $100k for 75–250 MW, $300k above 250 MW, plus actual costs beyond those amounts.
  - Security: $50k/MW, about 80% of it forfeited if the project is reduced or abandoned.
  - Draft non-refundable interconnection fee: $50k/MW — [Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/03/public-utility-commission-of-texas-issues-proposed-rules-for-large-load-interconn/)
  - Final adoption not confirmed.
- **ERCOT queue size:**
  - About 189 GW of large-load requests (PUCT, Oct 2025) — [NASEO/PUCT slides](https://mojo.naseo.org/Data/Sites/1/media/tknaseo/sb6-puct-17-oct-2025.pdf)
  - A blog claims Oncor alone tracked 273 GW and ERCOT's Apr 2026 preliminary forecast put filtered demand at 243 GW — [Compute Heat Rate (Substack)](https://computeheatrate.substack.com/p/the-queue-survived-even-thrived-with) (unverified)
- **Behind-the-meter (BTM) scale and deals:**
  - Announced BTM gas and fuel-cell capacity for data centers exceeds 12 GW globally by mid-2026, up from under 3 GW at the start of 2025 — [Datacentres.com (Aug 19, 2026)](https://www.datacentres.com/news/behind-the-meter-power-gas-turbines-and-fuel-cells-emerge-as-data-centres-escape-slot1-2026-08-19)
  - Enerflex won about 450 MW of BTM generation for a North American developer (announced Oct 1, 2026), delivering 2027–28 — [Blackridge Research](https://www.blackridgeresearch.com/news-releases/enerflex-lands-contract-for-450-mw-of-behind-the-meter-power-generation-for-north-american-data-center-developer)
- **Small "powered land" players:**
  - TerraVolt, a subsidiary of OTCQB-listed CalEthos, signed a firm gas supply deal (May 11, 2026) for a planned 200–240 MW Idaho campus. Its model is pre-permitted land plus on-site gas, sold to hyperscalers and developers — [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/05/11/3291806/0/en/CalEthos-Inc-and-its-subsidiary-TerraVolt-Infrastructure-Inc-sign-a-Natural-Gas-Supply-Agreement-for-TerraVolt-s-planned-AI-Data-Center-Infrastructure-Development.html); [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/terravolt-inks-natural-gas-supply-deal-for-onsite-power-plant-at-planned-data-center-campus-in-idaho/)
  - An entitled West Virginia site is listed on Crexi with third-party BTM gas arranged (Bedford Management Partners), with about 24 months to first power — [Crexi](https://www.crexi.com/properties/2142891/west-virginia-data-center)
- **Enverus caution:** a site "powered by gas" is not the same as buildable, permittable or connectable — [Enverus](https://www.enverus.com/blog/data-center-sites-powered-by-gas-isnt-the-same-as-ready-to-build/)
- **Flexibility:**
  - FERC's Jun 18, 2026 show-cause orders went to six RTOs on large-load rules — [Utility Dive](https://utilitydive.com/news/data-centers-flexibility-utilities-speed-to-power/822588/)
  - GridCARE with Portland General Electric unlocked more than 80 MW of incremental data-center capacity in 2026, out of more than 400 MW by 2029 — [APPA](https://www.publicpower.org/periodical/article/oregon-utility-gridcare-complete-project-accelerate-large-load-interconnections-using-flexibility)
  - Camus Energy's FlexConnect models 8,760-hour grid capacity for conditional-firm service — [Renewable Energy World](https://renewableenergyworld.com/power-grid/solving-interconnection-bottlenecks-with-data-center-load-flexibility)
  - EPRI published a large-load flexibility-class framework — [Power Engineering](https://www.power-eng.com/business/epri-launches-new-large-load-framework-to-reduce-time-to-power-for-data-centers/)
  - A Duke study found a 1–2% cut in data-center peak demand could lower rates by 0.5–2.8% — [Utility Dive](https://utilitydive.com/news/data-centers-flexibility-utilities-speed-to-power/822588/)

### Inferences
**Small-player models, lowest capital first (capital figures are inference)**

1. **Interconnection and large-load filing consulting**
   - Covers SB 6 studies, security structuring, and filings in the six RTO compliance proceedings.
   - Fee-for-service; customers are developers, landowners and crypto-to-AI converters. Startup capital is minimal for experienced engineers.
   - The draft forfeiture rule raises the value of pre-screening before posting $50k/MW.
2. **Site origination and powered-land brokering**
   - Assemble land plus a gas lateral, water and entitlements, then sell to developers.
   - Revenue is a success fee or option-and-flip. Capital is mostly option payments and engineering studies, roughly $0.5M–$5M (inference).
   - Examples: Bedford-type arrangers, TerraVolt.
   - Risk: Enverus's point that "gas-powered" does not mean buildable.
3. **Flexibility and curtailment software and services**
   - Utilities or data centers pay SaaS or per-MW fees. Examples: GridCARE, Camus.
   - The tailwind is FERC and PUCT curtailment authority (the Goodnight order says curtailment is uncapped).
4. **BTM generation services**
   - Gas compression, gensets, turbine rental, O&M, emissions permitting.
   - Equipment rental and O&M can start small. Owning generation (Enerflex-scale) is capital-heavy.
5. **Window**
   - Roughly 2026–2028, while queues are clogged and before RTO rules settle.
   - Risks: an AI capex pullback that strands land options, gas turbine lead times, forfeited security, and air permits.

- Final PUCT SB 6 rule status as of Oct 2026 not confirmed.
- No sourced fee benchmarks for interconnection consultants or site brokers.
- No 2026 funding data for GridCARE or Camus.

## Space and AV (brief)

### Takeaway
Space: all launch and reentry licensing moved to the streamlined Part 450 on Mar 17, 2026, and a further FAA rule on flight-safety analysis is pending. "Novel space activity" authorization still has no rule. The small-player angles are licensing and safety-analysis services and payload or ground segments, not launch vehicles. AV: NHTSA works by exemption (per the earlier report); I found no new small-player AV business-model evidence.

### Cited Findings
- **Part 450 consolidation:**
  - All FAA launch and reentry licensing moved under Part 450 on Mar 17, 2026: one license covers multiple operations, vehicle configurations and sites — [FAA](https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/faa-streamlines-commercial-space-license-approvals)
  - Legacy licenses moved by the Mar 9, 2026 deadline include Blue Origin, Firefly, SpaceX, Rocket Lab and ULA. FAA has issued 14 Part 450 licenses since 2021 — [AvWeb](https://avweb.com/aviation-news/faa-part-450-commercial-space-licenses/)
- **Follow-on rulemaking:** EO 14335 (Aug 13, 2025) ordered Part 450 reevaluated. A follow-on FAA proposed rule (Docket FAA-2026-9936) targets flight-safety-analysis requirements — [Federal Register public inspection](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-20388.pdf)
- **Criticism:** licensing delays persist and stakeholders raise safety-permissiveness concerns — [Leonard David](https://www.leonarddavid.com/streamlining-launch-and-reentry-licensing/)

### Inferences
- Low-capital space plays:
  - Part 450 flight-safety analysis and licensing consultancy, for which FAA review backlogs create demand
  - Range and ground services
  - Pre-filing advisory for "novel" in-space activities, pending Commerce rules
- Launch vehicles themselves are capital-heavy and dominated by incumbents.
