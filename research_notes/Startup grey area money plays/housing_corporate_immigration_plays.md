# Business Openings from 2025–2026 US Policy Changes: Housing/Zoning, Corporate/Capital Markets, Immigration, Insurance, Short-Term Rentals (as of Oct 9, 2026)

Method note: research done Oct 9, 2026 using web search (Firecrawl search, then WebSearch after rate-limiting). Many findings come from search-result excerpts and law-firm or industry summaries, not full primary documents. Where only a secondary source was available, this is flagged. "Inferences" covers business-model reasoning (who pays, startup cost, window), which is analysis and not sourced fact. ## Housing/zoning: what changed (CA SB 79, CEQA AB 130/SB 131, TX SB 840/SB 15, other state reforms, federal), and what plays does it open?

### Takeaway
The big 2025 state housing laws are now in force: CA SB 79 since Jul 1, 2026; CA CEQA infill exemption since Jun 30, 2025; TX SB 840/SB 15 since Sep 1, 2025. The federal ROAD to Housing Act became law Jul 11, 2026 and removes the manufactured-housing chassis requirement once HUD rewrites its rules. The money is in services that turn new legal entitlements into permitted projects: entitlement/feasibility analysis, permitting software, small-lot and commercial-to-multifamily development, and modular/factory-built supply. Local resistance (e.g., Los Angeles seeking delay to 2030) makes the timing of each opening differ city by city.

### Cited Findings
**California SB 79 (transit-oriented upzoning)**
- SB 79 took effect July 1, 2026 (CA HCD page) — [HCD](https://www.hcd.ca.gov/planning-and-research/sb79-tod)
- LA City Planning calls SB 79 the "Abundant and Affordable Homes Near Transit Act." LA's Low-Rise Ordinance and Phased Implementation Ordinance took effect June 30, 2026 — [LA City Planning](https://planning.lacity.gov/resources/senate-bill-sb-79)
- Allen Matkins (Jun 8, 2026): the City of Los Angeles is seeking to delay SB 79 implementation until 2030 — [Allen Matkins](https://www.allenmatkins.com/real-ideas/the-city-of-los-angeles-seeks-to-delay-implementation-of-senate-bill-79-sb-79-until-2030.html)
- Holland & Knight (Jun 2026): cities are responding with exclusion ordinances, TOD alternative plans and hybrid approaches. From July 1, 2026, cities must apply the heightened standards to new applications. HCD has sent at least one city a letter requiring a response within 60 days (by Jul 7, 2026) — [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/tracking-sb-79-implementation-before-the-train-leaves-the-station)
- California YIMBY published a one-month review (~Aug 2026); content seen only as a search excerpt — [CA YIMBY](https://cayimby.org/blog/sb-79-one-month-in-taking-stock-of-california-yimbys-signature-bill/)

**California CEQA reform (AB 130 / SB 131)**
- Signed June 30, 2025 and effective immediately. AB 130 creates a CEQA exemption for infill housing that is code-compliant, in an urban area, and on a site of 20 acres or less. Exclusions: historic-structure demolition, environmentally sensitive sites, listed hazardous-waste sites. Tribal consultation required — [Nixon Peabody, Jun 5 2026](https://www.nixonpeabody.com/insights/alerts/2026/06/05/california-ab-130-shapes-ceqa-exemptions-pathways-to-infill-housing); [Venable, Jul 2025](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2025/07/from-red-tape-to-green-light-ceqa-reforms-fast)
- Nixon Peabody (Jun 2026): developers "are already availing themselves" of AB 130, and risk is shifting to local processes and threshold-eligibility disputes (qualitative; no project counts) — [Nixon Peabody](https://www.nixonpeabody.com/insights/alerts/2026/06/05/california-ab-130-shapes-ceqa-exemptions-pathways-to-infill-housing)

**California small-lot / SB 9 / starter homes (2025 session)**
- 2025 legislation broadened small-lot subdivisions (SB 684, SB 1123) and strengthened SB 9 duplex and lot-split rights. Under the Starter Home Revitalization Act as amended, projects of 10 or fewer units on up to 10 parcels get ministerial approval. AB 1061 extends SB 9 to historic districts if no historic structure is altered or demolished (Nov 2025 alert) — [National Law Review / Mondaq](https://natlawreview.com/article/streamlining-middle-housing-latest-small-site-development-reforms)

**Texas SB 840 / SB 15 (effective Sep 1, 2025)**
- SB 840: certain Texas cities (Dallas included) cannot prohibit multifamily housing in zones that allow commercial uses such as retail and office. SB 15: cities cannot prohibit small-lot subdivisions on unplatted tracts over 5 acres zoned single-family — [Dallas City Hall](https://dallascityhall.com/departments/pnv/Pages/TXSBLanduse2025.aspx)
- SB 840 enrolled text: effective September 1, 2025; the population threshold language references cities above 300,000 (excerpt) — [LegiScan](https://legiscan.com/TX/text/SB840/id/3246238); [Foley, Jul 2025](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2025/07/texas-enacts-critical-real-estate-reforms-senate-bills-15-17-840/); [Texas Governor](https://gov.texas.gov/news/post/governor-abbott-signs-laws-to-combat-statewide-housing-crisis-in-austin)

**Other states (single-stair, ADU)**
- Pew (Sep 15, 2025): 14 states have passed some single-stair bill since 2023. Montana, Texas and Colorado allow single-stair buildings up to 5–6 stories. Oregon adopted an optional appendix allowing 4 stories and required rules for up to 6. Arkansas Act 313 and Iowa SF 592 allow ADUs on single-family lots. Maine LD 1829 requires at least one ADU per single-family lot — [Pew](https://www.pew.org/en/research-and-analysis/articles/2025/09/15/state-legislatures-make-bipartisan-breakthroughs-on-policies-that-promote-housing)
- A Mercatus count cited secondhand: 18 states have statewide ADU reforms. 412 housing-reform bills were introduced and 124 pro-housing laws enacted in 2025 — [The New Urban Order](https://thenewurbanorder.substack.com/p/state-housing-policy-is-having-a); [APA blog](https://planning.org/blog/9322801/what-we've-seen-on-the-housing-front-2025-state-policy-highlights-and-trends/)

**Federal**
- The 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act became law July 11, 2026. Sources report it took effect without the President's signature. The Senate vote was 89-10 on Mar 12 (date reported by a manufactured-housing retailer blog) — [News From The States](https://www.newsfromthestates.com/node/416877); [Braustin](https://www.braustin.com/?p=34084); [ACMA](https://acmanet.org/bipartisan-21st-century-road-to-housing-act-become-law/)
- The Act removes the federal permanent-chassis requirement for manufactured homes (MIT Golub calls this possibly its most important provision). HUD must revise regulations before chassis-free homes can be built, and state rules (e.g., Florida) also need changes. The law also expands manufactured/modular financing. It has 60 sections — [MIT Golub](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/node/52628); [Florida Realtors, Aug 2026](https://www.floridarealtors.org/news-media/news-articles/2026/08/new-federal-housing-law-expands-options-florida); [Rocket Mortgage](https://www.rocketmortgage.com/learn/21st-century-road-to-housing-act)
- A claim that chassis removal cuts prices by up to 9% comes only from a Texas realty blog. Low confidence — [LRG Realty](https://lrgrealty.com/lrg-blog/new-housing-law-manufactured-homes-texas/)

**Example companies**
- PermitFlow (AI construction-permitting software, NYC): $54M Series B led by Accel (Dec 2025, ~$90.5M total). Biggest footprints are CA, FL and TX. One job post gives Mar 2026 as the round date, which conflicts with Dec 2025 — [Signalbase](https://www.trysignalbase.com/news/funding/permitflow-secures-54m-series-b-funding); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2668036)

### Inferences
- **SB 79 parcel-feasibility / entitlement-as-a-service.** Owners and small developers near qualifying transit stops pay $2k–$15k per site report, or take a success fee or equity on entitlement. Startup cost is low (GIS data plus a land-use attorney or planner network, roughly <$250k). The window is about 2026–2030, while cities fight implementation and the rules stay unclear. That unclear, city-by-city compliance is itself the product (tracking exclusion ordinances and TOD alternative plans). Risks: LA delays, litigation, HCD enforcement pace, and high construction costs that hold back actual building even after upzoning.
- **Texas SB 840 commercial-to-multifamily and SB 15 small-lot plays.** Small developers and land brokers can find commercial-zoned parcels in large Texas cities (>300k population per bill text) and unplatted 5+ acre single-family tracts, then sell entitled or plat-ready land to builders. Revenue comes from land-assembly spread and brokerage fees. Capital needs are moderate to high (land options).
- **Permitting software** (PermitFlow is the scaled example) is now venture-crowded. Smaller openings: city-specific plan-check pre-review for SB 79 / AB 130 eligibility, and selling to cities (HCD compliance reporting).
- **Manufactured/modular.** HUD rulemaking on chassis-free homes likely takes 12–24+ months, so the window for dealers and land-lease communities is ~2027–2029. Today's play is positioning: land, dealer licenses, factory partnerships.
- **ADU builders and lot-split brokers.** The CA SB 9/SB 684/SB 1123 expansions plus 18-state ADU reforms support a local general-contractor or design-build model. Homeowners pay $150k–$400k per ADU (researcher estimate, unsourced). This is a mature, competitive category.

## Corporate law/capital markets: Delaware SB 21 and "DExit," Texas business courts, TXSE, SEC private-market moves, 401(k) access, Reg CF/Reg A — what plays?

### Takeaway
DExit is real but slow: about 49 Delaware public companies proposed reincorporation from Jan 2024 to Mar 2026, and Delaware's IPO share fell to ~62% in 2025. The Delaware Supreme Court upheld SB 21 on Feb 27, 2026. TXSE launched full trading Jul 31, 2026. The larger, near-term money is in retail and 401(k) access to private markets: DOL proposed a fiduciary safe harbor Mar 30, 2026, and the SEC proposed broader accredited-investor routes Sep 30, 2026. Incumbents are consolidating fast (Schwab–Forge $660M; Morgan Stanley–EquityZen), so for small operators the best openings are compliance, education/credentialing, and data tooling rather than marketplaces.

### Cited Findings
**Delaware SB 21 / DExit**
- The Delaware Supreme Court upheld SB 21's constitutionality on Feb 27, 2026 — [Foley, May 2026](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/05/dexit-one-year-later-an-assessment-of-sb21-the-continuing-pace-of-reincorporation-and-the-maturation-of-texas-as-a-corporate-domicile/)
- About 49 Delaware public corporations submitted redomestication proposals from Jan 2024 to Mar 2026. In 2025, 26 proposals went to stockholders: 21 approved by vote, plus 7 approved by written consent — [Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/05/dexit-one-year-later-an-assessment-of-sb21-the-continuing-pace-of-reincorporation-and-the-maturation-of-texas-as-a-corporate-domicile/)
- Delaware's share of US IPOs fell from ~80–93% historically to ~62% in 2025. Nevada had ~17% and Texas ~4% — same Foley source (figures via search summary)
- Movers include Dillard's, Coinbase, MSG Entertainment and ArcBest (Texas, 2026) — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/esg/texas-sees-growing-benefit-from-corporate-push-to-ditch-delaware)
- Counterpoint: Daily Journal reports only a "slight uptick," with many clients not feeling a strong need to reincorporate given the work involved — [Daily Journal](https://www.dailyjournal.com/articles/392868-the-race-for-corporate-america)

**Texas Business Court**
- About 367 cases from Sep 1, 2024 through Q1 2026, roughly half after HB 40 (effective Sep 1, 2025). HB 40 cut the qualified-transaction threshold from $10M to $5M and added IP jurisdiction — [V&E / Texas Lawbook, Jun 2026](https://www.velaw.com/insights/texas-business-court-caseload-expands-and-broadens-after-house-bill-40-expands-jurisdiction/)

**Texas Stock Exchange**
- TXSE began production trading July 6, 2026 with a phased symbol rollout. All NMS symbols were live by July 31, 2026, with 50+ member firms. Listings auctions are planned "later this year," and the first ETP listing was targeted for Sep 2026 — [TXSE press release, Jul 31 2026](https://www.txse.com/press/texas-stock-exchange-celebrates-successful-launch-of-trading); [TXSE launch guide](https://www.txse.com/trading/member-readiness-and-launch-guide); [Texas Governor](https://gov.texas.gov/news/post/governor-abbott-marks-successful-trading-launch-of-texas-stock-exchange)

**SEC accredited-investor proposal**
- The SEC voted Sep 30, 2026 to propose expanding the natural-person accredited-investor definition. Proposed routes include CPA, CFA, CFP and FINRA Series 79/86/87, a new accredited-investor exam, knowledgeable-employee status, and family offices with ≥$5M AUM. The wealth test stays. **401(k) / alternative assets**
- The Aug 2025 executive order on alternative assets in 401(k)s led DOL to propose a process-based fiduciary safe harbor on Mar 30, 2026 (91 Fed. Reg. 16088). It covers private equity, private credit, real estate, digital assets and more. It does not require plans to offer these assets and is not a blanket liability shield. Comments closed Jun 1, 2026. DOL reportedly aims to finalize by year-end 2026 — [DOL release](https://www.dol.gov/index.php/newsroom/releases/ebsa/ebsa20260330); [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260403-dol-proposed-rule-401-k-alternative-assets); [Sullivan & Cromwell](https://www.sullcrom.com/SullivanCromwell/_Assets/PDFs/Memos/DOL-Proposes-Rule-Facilitate-Alternative-Investments-401k-Plans.pdf)

**Reg CF / Reg A**
- Jan 2026 petition (Sherwood Neiss) asked the SEC to raise the Reg CF cap from $5M to $20M. The SEC Small Business Forum (Mar 9, 2026; report to Congress Jul 27, 2026) ranked the $20M cap as its top recommendation. The SEC agenda reportedly has an exempt-offering rulemaking slated for October 2026. No proposal confirmed. One source cites $10M instead — [Crowdfund Insider, Jul 2026](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/07/294431-sec-small-business-forum-recommends-raising-reg-cf-funding-cap-to-20-million-other-key-changes-to-improve-access-to-capital/); [Crowdfund Insider, Jan 2026](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/01/257193-petition-filed-with-securities-and-exchange-commission-to-raise-reg-cf-funding-cap-to-20-million/); [Angel Investors Network](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/capital-raising/sec-regulation-crowdfunding-changes-2026)

**Secondaries / retail private-market consolidation**
- Schwab agreed (Nov 2025) to buy Forge Global for ~$660M cash ($45/share), expected to close H1 2026 (closing not verified). Morgan Stanley agreed in Oct 2025 to acquire EquityZen. Schwab is also developing interval funds — [Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/schwab-forge-global-acquisition-drives-firm-further-into-private-markets); [FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2025/11/11/charles-schwab-acquires-forge-global-for-660m/)

### Inferences
- **Reincorporation services.** Customers are boards and GCs of controlled companies and small/mid caps. They pay for proxy/redomestication packages, Texas/Nevada governance benchmarking and registered-agent services. This is mostly a law-firm business. A small operator could do data and comparison SaaS or registered-agent/compliance in TX/NV. Startup cost is low. The window is open but narrowing, since SB 21 was upheld and many boards are holding steady.
- **Accredited-investor exam and credential prep.** If the Sep 30, 2026 proposal is adopted (likely 2027 at the earliest), an exam route creates a test-prep and testing business, much like the Series 65 prep market. Retail investors would pay roughly $50–$500. Startup cost is low (content plus a platform). Main risk: the rule could change or stall in final form. Building now is speculative.
- **401(k) alternatives plumbing.** Recordkeepers, plan sponsors and asset managers will pay for fiduciary-process documentation, valuation/liquidity reporting, and participant education tools so they can use the DOL safe harbor once final (target end-2026). B2B SaaS. Sales cycles are long and incumbents are strong. Litigation risk from the ERISA plaintiffs' bar is what creates demand for "process" tooling.
- **Secondary marketplaces** are consolidating into wirehouses (Schwab/Forge, MS/EquityZen). New entrants face high cost (broker-dealer/ATS licensing, roughly $1M+) and incumbent distribution. Niches remain: employee-liquidity tender administration and data/pricing feeds.
- **Reg CF funding portals.** If the cap rises to $20M, portal economics improve (fees typically a percentage of raise). Registration as a funding portal is a moderate cost. Window starts only after rulemaking.
- **TXSE.** Openings for small firms are limited: listing advisory, plus IR/compliance for TX-domiciled issuers if TXSE listings start (auctions planned late 2026).

## Immigration: H-1B $100k fee, weighted lottery, Gold Card, O-1 demand — what plays?

### Takeaway
The H-1B weighted (wage-level) lottery is final and was used for the FY2027 cap (effective Feb 27, 2026). The $100k H-1B fee proclamation (Sep 19, 2025) is in legal limbo. A Massachusetts court vacated it (Jun 2026), the First Circuit denied a stay (Jul 24, 2026), and a California court reportedly enjoined it (Sep 30, 2026). The D.C. Circuit is separately reviewing a ruling that upheld it, and DHS proposed a separate ~$103k fee rule in Aug 2026. The Gold Card launched with very few approvals. Uncertainty itself creates demand for immigration-strategy services (O-1, EB-2 NIW, E-2, wage-level optimization). Demand for O-1 is reported mainly by vendors.

### Cited Findings
- DHS weighted-selection final rule was published Dec 29, 2025, effective Feb 27, 2026, and used for the FY2027 cap. Registration fee is $215 — [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/12/29/2025-23853/weighted-selection-process-for-registrants-and-petitioners-seeking-to-file-cap-subject-h-1b); [USCIS](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/h-1b-specialty-occupations/h-1b-electronic-registration-process); [TRAC](https://tracreports.org/reports/768/)
- $100k fee proclamation of Sep 19, 2025: on Dec 23, 2025, D.D.C. (Judge Howell) upheld it in the Chamber of Commerce case. The Chamber appealed to the D.C. Circuit — [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/trump-can-order-employers-to-pay-extra-h-1b-fee-chamber-commerce/808772); [AILA](https://www.aila.org/library/district-court-rules-in-favor-of-dhs-in-lawsuit-over-100-000-h-1b-fee)
- D. Mass. (20-state suit) vacated the fee around Jun 8–9, 2026, finding it an unauthorized tax and an APA violation. Judge Sorokin administratively stayed his ruling Jun 12. The First Circuit denied the government's stay motion Jul 24, 2026, so the fee stays blocked; the merits are undecided — [SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/appeals-court-blocks-100k-h1b-fee); [Clark Hill](https://www.clarkhill.com/news-events/news/first-circuit-blocks-reinstatement-of-100k-h-1b-fee/); [Klasko](https://www.klaskolaw.com/august-2026-100000-h-1b-fee-blocked-again/)
- Reported, less verified: the proclamation was extended Sep 18, 2026, and N.D. Cal. issued a preliminary injunction Sep 30, 2026 — [Ellis](https://www.ellis.com/resources/h1b-100k-fee-blocked-california); [ILBSG, Oct 6 2026](https://bizlegalservices.com/2026/10/06/second-federal-court-blocks-100000-h-1b-fee-plus-new-extension/)
- DHS proposed rule (Aug 2026) would impose a $103,265 fee on new cap-subject H-1B workers. It is separate from the proclamation — per search summary of [SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/appeals-court-blocks-100k-h1b-fee) / [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/united-states/immigration/appeals-court-keeps-100000-h-1b-fee-blocked). Single-source detail; verify.
- Gold Card: applications opened Dec 2025 at a $1M price and a $15k processing fee. In April 2026 Lutnick told Congress only one had been approved, with "hundreds in the queue." Earlier he had cited ~$1.3B in "commitments" and ~70k waitlist registrations — [Fox/AP](https://www.fox13news.com/money/trump-gold-card-visa-one-approved); [The National, Apr 23 2026](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/us/2026/04/23/trump-gold-card/); [trumpcard.gov](https://www.trumpcard.gov/)
- Alma (an immigration vendor) says Gold Card applicants were fewer than expected as of spring/June 2026. Forbes (Jul 31, 2026) mentions litigation and alternatives — [Alma](https://www.tryalma.com/learn/trump-gold-card-visa); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/forbesbooksauthors/2026/07/31/is-the-trump-gold-card-visa-actually-working/)
- Vendor content calls O-1A the top founder alternative in 2026. It cites ~93.8% O-1 approval in FY2025 Q3 (vendor-sourced, possibly promotional). TechCrunch (Sep 25, 2025): founders called the fee a "talent tariff" that prices startups out — [Alma](https://tryalma.com/learn/h1b-alternatives-startup-founders); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/25/startup-founders-say-trumps-100k-h-1b-fee-is-a-talent-tariff-that-will-hurt-innovation)

### Inferences
- **O-1 / EB-2 NIW "evidence-building" services.** Founders, researchers and startups pay $5k–$25k per case plus legal fees. Example vendors: Alma, Boundless, Lighthouse, Passright. Startup cost is low to moderate, but you must work through licensed attorneys (unauthorized-practice-of-law risk). The window is likely multi-year because H-1B access stays uncertain.
- **Wage-level optimization and H-1B strategy SaaS for employers.** The weighted lottery rewards higher wage levels. Employers would pay for tools that model selection odds by SOC code and wage level and flag fee exposure. Customers are mid-size employers and staffing firms. The fee's legal status changes month to month, which supports a subscription "status tracker plus compliance" offering.
- **Gold Card concierge.** Demand is weak (one approval as of Apr 2026), so this is high-risk and a small market. Better treated as a referral line inside a wealth/immigration practice.
- **Main risk across all of these:** court decisions or new rules can erase demand quickly, e.g., if the $100k fee is struck permanently and the DHS fee rule fails.

- Gold Card approval counts after April 2026 not found.

## Insurance: California wildfire reforms, FAIR Plan, Florida reforms, parametric — what insurtech plays?

### Takeaway
California now lets insurers use forward-looking catastrophe models in rates. In return, insurers commit to writing in distressed areas: at least 85% of their statewide market share. The FAIR Plan keeps growing (696,562 policies, Jun 2026), and its rates are rising about 29–36% (dates conflict). Florida is going the other way: Citizens has fallen from ~1.4M policies (2023) to about 270k, following the 2022 litigation reforms. Openings: mitigation-linked brokerage, parametric "gap" products sold alongside FAIR Plan coverage, and Citizens takeout/MGA plays in Florida.

### Cited Findings
- California is allowing forward-looking catastrophe models in rate filings in exchange for commitments to write residential policies in at-risk areas equal to at least 85% of the insurer's market share. Travelers and the Auto Club exchange sought +7% and +11% — via search summary of [Insurance Journal, Sep 10 2026](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/west/2026/09/10/884417.htm); [CDI release 055-2025](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/0400-news/0100-press-releases/2025/release055-2025.cfm); [InsuranceNewsNet](https://insurancenewsnet.com/oarticle/two-california-home-insurers-to-raise-rates-expand-coverage-by-late-2026)
- FAIR Plan had 696,562 dwelling and commercial policies as of Jun 2026, up 8% from Sep 2025. A consumer group counts 12,189 policy "commitments" rather than written policies — same Insurance Journal summary (secondary)
- FAIR Plan rate filing: 35.8% requested. One blog says 29.1% was approved, effective Oct 15, 2026. Another analysis says it went live Apr 1, 2026 on ~555k policies. Conflicting — [CoverageCat](https://www.coveragecat.com/blog/california-fair-plan-rate-hikes-2026); [actuary.info](https://actuary.info/insights/california-fair-plan-35-percent-wildfire-april-2026)
- Florida Citizens: about 270,000 policies (peak ~1.4M in Sep 2023). Multiple carriers were approved for 2026 takeouts (Slide up to 100k; eight others for Feb–Apr). Officials credit the 2022 litigation reforms — [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/property/citizens-cuts-policy-count-to-270000-amid-nonrenewal-push-588731.aspx); [Florida Trend, Nov 2025](https://www.floridatrend.com/articles/2025/11/26/citizens-policies-now-lowest-level-since-2019/)
- Parametric and mitigation examples: Arbol launched a parametric wildfire product (Sep 2025, limits up to $10M, Lloyd's Somers Syndicate capacity). ## Short-term rentals: NYC Local Law 18 and other city restrictions — what are operators pivoting to?

### Takeaway
NYC Local Law 18 (passed Jan 2022, enforced Sep 2023) effectively ended whole-unit stays under 30 days. Hosted stays have a two-guest cap, and platforms can't book unregistered units. Listings in the outer boroughs fell more than 90%. Operators have pivoted to 30+ night mid-term rentals (traveling nurses, corporate, relocations) and to licensed hotel/aparthotel formats. Bills to loosen the rules (Intro 879 / 1107) are pending. Other cities in 2026 mostly use partial restrictions: caps, zones, primary-residence rules.

### Cited Findings
- LL18: hosts must register with the Office of Special Enforcement. Platforms cannot process bookings for unregistered units. Stays under 30 nights require the permanent resident present, and guests are capped at 2. Passed Jan 2022, effective Sep 2023 — [Lodgify encyclopedia](https://encyclopedia.lodgify.com/new-york-local-law-18); [Rakidzich 2026](https://www.rakidzich.com/articles/are-airbnbs-legal-in-nyc-2026)
- Airbnb listings fell >90% in Queens, Brooklyn, the Bronx and Staten Island. An Airbnb-commissioned report says hosts lost >$320M citywide over three years (Sep 2026; advocacy source) — [QNS, Sep 2026](https://qns.com/2026/09/local-law-18-queens-lose-100-million-income/)
- Intro 879 would raise the hosted-stay guest limit to 4. Intro 1107 also would loosen the rules. Status not verified — same QNS / search summary
- Furnished Finder CEO Jeff Hurst (May 2026 podcast) says investors are shifting to mid-term rentals (30+ days). Tenants are traveling healthcare workers, corporate/skilled-trade workers, and relocating families — [Afford Anything](https://affordanything.com/?p=14345)
- 2026 bans are mostly partial (caps, zones, primary-residence, density). Commentary lists NYC, Dallas, Memphis and parts of LA County. Dallas enforcement was on hold due to litigation as of Oct 2024 (stale) — [Rakidzich](https://www.rakidzich.com/articles/airbnb-regulation-aftershock-short-term-rental-ban-2026); [Furnished Finder Dallas](https://www.furnishedfinder.com/blog/dallas-airbnb-and-str-bans)

### Inferences
- **Mid-term rental (MTR) operator or arbitrage.** Operators master-lease or own furnished units and rent them for 30–90 days to nurses and corporate tenants. Revenue is the spread over long-term rent. Startup cost is roughly $10k–$30k furnishing per unit plus deposits (researcher estimate). Risks: tenancy law (in NYC, 30+ day occupants may gain tenant protections), lower yields than STR, and lease prohibitions on subletting.
- **B2B services for MTR.** Tenant screening, corporate-housing placement, furnishing-as-a-service and compliance registration tools. Example platforms: Furnished Finder, Blueground, Landing (not verified in this research).
- **Compliance-tech for hosts in partially restricted cities** (registration, primary-residence evidence, tax remittance). This is defensible as rules spread, but platforms may build it in-house.
- **Window:** NYC rules are stable unless Intro 879/1107 pass. Loosening would reopen hosted-STR plays.
