# Federal Retreat Opens Startup Grey Zones Nationwide

Between January 2025 and October 2026, the US federal government mostly loosened rules through executive orders, staff statements, enforcement discretion, proposed rules and rescinded guidance. It rarely did so by statute. That pattern produced the largest set of startup grey zones in a decade. Congress was more aggressive than the agencies were. The states then filled the gaps unevenly, some quickly and some not at all. The most valuable grey zones as of October 2026 are these:

- **sports and event contracts** listed on CFTC exchanges, where three federal circuits have split and Supreme Court petitions are pending;
- **stablecoins and token launches**, operating in the gap between the enacted GENIUS Act and its unfinished rules, and between a failed market-structure bill and SEC interpretive relief;
- **AI products framed to fall outside state definitions**, such as "wellness coach" instead of therapist, or "assistant" instead of companion;
- **FDA enforcement discretion** for wellness wearables and single-recommendation clinical decision support;
- **federal "pilot" fast lanes in deep tech**: DOE reactor authorization, drone procurement "gauntlets," the eVTOL pilot program, and a domestic drone market protected by an FCC ban on new foreign models.

Most of these openings are not durable. They rest on reversible instruments, a hostile state AG or a private right of action can close them, and several have hard expiry dates within the next 90 days:

- **intoxicating hemp-THC products become illegal on Dec 11, 2026**;
- **DEA telehealth prescribing flexibilities end Dec 31, 2026**;
- the GENIUS Act takes effect **no later than Jan 18, 2027**.

The common strategy among winners is to get some form of federal sanction (a charter, a registered exchange, a pilot slot, an exemption) and then use it to defend against state enforcement. The common failure mode is assuming that federal deregulation means no regulation at all. *This report is informational research synthesized from secondary sources. It is not legal advice.*

A note on sourcing: the underlying research relied heavily on search-result summaries of law-firm alerts and trade press, because many primary sources could not be fetched in full. Claims that rest on a single or low-quality source, or where sources conflict, are flagged inline as **[single-source]**, **[unverified]** or **[conflicting]**. Anything time-sensitive should be checked against the Federal Register, the court docket or the statute before anyone relies on it.

## AI: state laws stay binding while Washington attacks them

**What changed.**

At the federal level:

- President Trump revoked Biden's AI executive order in January 2025.
- The July 2025 AI Action Plan directed the FTC to set aside orders that "unduly burden AI innovation." The FTC used that in December 2025 to vacate its 2024 Rytr consent order over AI-generated reviews ([Wiggin and Dana](https://www.wiggin.com/publication/ftc-sets-aside-rytr-llc-final-order-citing-the-trump-administrations-ai-action-plan/)).
- Congress twice refused to preempt state AI laws. A 10-year moratorium was stripped from the reconciliation bill by a **99–1 Senate vote**, and an NDAA attempt also failed ([StateScoop](https://statescoop.com/state-ai-law-moratorium-omitted-2026-defense-bill-trump-eo/)).
- The administration then turned to **EO 14365 (Dec 11, 2025)**. It created a DOJ AI Litigation Task Force to challenge state laws, directed Commerce to list "onerous" state laws, and tied BEAD broadband funds to state compliance. It explicitly carves out child safety ([White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/12/eliminating-state-law-obstruction-of-national-artificial-intelligence-policy/); [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/ai-executive-order-targets-state-laws-and-seeks-uniform-federal-standards)).
- The first visible Task Force action was DOJ joining **xAI's April 2026 suit against the Colorado AI Act** ([Jenner](https://www.jenner.com/en/news-insights/client-alerts/doj-joins-xai-in-lawsuit-challenging-colorado-ai-act)).
- In July 2026 the FTC proposed a policy statement treating "suppression of accuracy" in AI outputs as potentially deceptive. It also argues that state laws requiring "alterations to the accurate outputs" of models can conflict with the FTC Act ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/07/2026-13628/policy-statement-concerning-the-suppression-of-accuracy-in-artificial-intelligence-systems)).

The states kept legislating:

- **California SB 53** (frontier transparency) took effect Jan 1, 2026.
- **California SB 243** (companion chatbots) also took effect Jan 1, 2026, with a **private right of action for $1,000 per violation** ([Jones Walker](https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/ai-regulatory-update-californias-sb-243-mandates-companion-ai-safety-and-accoun.html?id=102lq7c)).
- **New York's AI companion law** took effect Nov 5, 2025.
- **New York's RAISE Act**, as amended in March 2026, takes effect **Jan 1, 2027** with a 72-hour incident-reporting window ([Wiley](https://www.wiley.law/alert-New-York-Finalizes-RAISE-Act-for-Frontier-AI-Models-Law-Takes-Effect-January-1-2027)).
- **Illinois** banned AI therapy from Aug 1, 2025 ([AEI](https://www.aei.org/technology-and-innovation/illinois-bans-ai-therapy-questions-about-enforcement-remain/)).
- **Colorado** delayed its AI Act to June 30, 2026 ([Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/enforcement-of-colorado-ai-act-delayed-until-june-2026)). The legislature then passed SB 26-189 to "repeal and replace" it ([Troutman](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/05/colorado-legislature-passes-bill-to-repeal-and-replace-colorado-ai-act/)). A secondary guide says it was signed May 14, 2026, reframes the law around "automated decision-making technology," and takes effect Jan 1, 2027 **[single-source, unverified]**.

In Europe, GPAI obligations under the EU AI Act began Aug 2, 2025, and the AI Office's **fining powers began Aug 2, 2026** ([CSA Labs](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-gpai-enforcement-20260803-csa-st/)). The AI Digital Omnibus was adopted in June 2026. It moves Annex III high-risk obligations to **Dec 2, 2027** and product-embedded systems to **Aug 2, 2028**, while generative-output marking under Art. 50(2) applies from **Dec 2, 2026** ([Hogan Lovells](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/eu-legislators-agree-to-delay-for-highrisk-ai-rules)). Its reported Official Journal citation (Reg. 2026/1744) is **[single-source]**. The UK still has no AI bill. In March 2026 it dropped a text-and-data-mining opt-out exception as its "preferred option" without choosing a replacement ([UK government report](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69ba692226909a14239612e4/CP2602959_-_Report_on_Copyright_and_Artificial_Intelligence_web.pdf)).

**Why it is ambiguous.** An executive order cannot preempt state law on its own. White & Case concludes EO 14365 "would likely not independently displace state AI laws" ([White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/state-ai-laws-under-federal-scrutiny-key-takeaways-executive-order-establishing)). State laws therefore stay enforceable until a court enjoins them, even while the federal government attacks them. Under that headline conflict, the real grey zone is definitional. Whether a product is regulated at all depends on these boundaries:

- "companion chatbot" versus general assistant;
- "therapy" versus "wellness";
- "high-risk AI" versus "ADMT."

The frontier laws (SB 53, RAISE) apply only above 10^26 FLOPs and $500M in revenue. In practice that exempts nearly every startup as a model developer, but it leaves their use-case exposure untouched ([WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20251001-transparency-in-frontier-artificial-intelligence-act-sb-53-california-requires-new-standardized-ai-safety-disclosures)).

Copyright adds a second layer of uncertainty:

- In *Bartz v. Anthropic*, Judge Alsup held training on books "exceedingly transformative" but left pirated acquisition for trial.
- In *Kadrey v. Meta*, Judge Chhabria made market dilution the likely deciding factor ([Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/client-alert/making-sense-of-the-first-two-often-contradictory-sometimes-confounding-gen-ai-fair-use-rulings)).
- Anthropic's **$1.5B settlement**, covering about 482,000 works, received final approval in July 2026. It does not release output claims or future conduct ([Polsinelli](https://www.polsinelli.com/publications/ai-vs-authors-update-court-approves-historic-anthropic-settlement-while-meta-litigation-continues)).
- The only appellate ruling is *Thomson Reuters v. Ross* (3d Cir., Sept 29, 2026). It rejected fair use and recognized harm to an emerging market for licensing training data. Its reasoning is sealed, and it involved a non-generative tool ([IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2026/09/30/third-circuit-affirms-revised-fair-use-ruling-against-ross-ai-legal-research-platform-in-sealed-opinion/); [Ballard Spahr](https://www.ballardspahr.com/insights/alerts-and-articles/2026/10/third-circuit-addresses-fair-use-in-ai-training-but-leaves-generative-ai-questions-unresolved)).

**What startups are doing.** Startups are positioning on the unregulated side of definitions: "coach" rather than therapist, "productivity assistant" rather than companion, "information" rather than legal advice. Marketing an "AI therapist" is itself unlawful in Illinois ([Lysinski](https://lysinski.com/practices/ai-law/ai-therapy-chatbot-law-illinois/)). They are also shipping during not-yet-effective windows, such as the EU high-risk gap that runs to late 2027. They choose jurisdictions, geoblocking therapy-ban states and training outside the UK. And they are moving toward licensed, synthetic and provenance-documented data, since Bartz works out to roughly $3,000 per pirated work. That per-work figure is an inference from the settlement totals, not a ruling.

**Risks and what could close the gap.** The largest near-term litigation exposure for consumer chatbot startups is SB 243's private right of action. Most other state AI laws are enforced only by the AG. A reported California follow-on, **SB 1119**, would add age assurance and pre-launch child-safety assessments from Jan 1, 2027 **[single-source, unverified]** ([getlimina](https://www.getlimina.ai/en/blog/california-sb-243-companion-chatbot-law)).

AI legal tools face a live unauthorized-practice-of-law theory in *Nippon Life v. OpenAI* ([Barnes & Thornburg](https://btlaw.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/lawsuit-alleges-ai-chatbot-engages-in-unauthorized-practice-of-law)). California has also barred defendants from arguing that "the AI autonomously caused the harm" ([Baker McKenzie](https://connectontech.bakermckenzie.com/us-legal-accountability-for-ai-agents-when-ai-agents-act-who-is-responsible-under-us-laws/)).

Federal preemption is still unresolved as of Oct 7, 2026 ([Regulatory Review](https://www.theregreview.org/2026/10/07/knapp-the-hidden-costs-of-ai-preemption/)). The most recent drafts are narrowing toward frontier-developer duties only, through a June 2026 bipartisan three-year preemption draft ([Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/06/04/bipartisan-ai-draft-proposes-three-year-preemption-of-state-laws/)) and a reported "FRONTIER Act" rework **[single-source tracker]**. Preemption, if it comes, will therefore probably not rescue chatbot, employment or child-safety products.

The **NO FAKES Act**, which would preempt state digital-replica laws, cleared Senate Judiciary unanimously. It then stalled when Sen. Cruz objected to unanimous consent ([Daily Journal](https://www.dailyjournal.com/article/395055-no-fakes-act-dead-for-now-as-cruz-objects-to-unanimous-consent-request)). In the EU, startups that substantially fine-tune open models may become GPAI "providers" exposed to fines. Commission guidance on modification thresholds is still the deciding variable.

## Crypto: enacted statute, unfinished rules and a stalled market bill

**What changed.**

Stablecoins:

- The **GENIUS Act** (July 2025) created a federal stablecoin regime. It takes effect on the earlier of **Jan 18, 2027** or 120 days after final rules ([GovernanceDocs](https://governancedocs.com/genius-act-effective-date/)).
- Regulators missed the **July 18, 2026** rulemaking deadline. The OCC proposed its rule in February 2026 and now targets a **November 2026** final rule ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/legal/2026/occ-promises-final-rule-for-genius-act-by-november/); [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/03/02/2026-04089/implementing-the-guiding-and-establishing-national-innovation-for-us-stablecoins-act-for-the)).
- The FDIC proposed two-business-day redemption and no pass-through deposit insurance for reserves ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/10/2026-06974/genius-act-requirements-and-standards-for-fdic-supervised-permitted-payment-stablecoin-issuers-and)).
- Treasury proposed "substantially similar" principles for state regimes ([Treasury NPRM](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/136/NPRM-GENIUS4c-Principles.pdf)).
- Three further reports are unconfirmed: a Treasury interim final rule on Sept 30, 2026 **[single-source]**; a joint August 18 rule package **[single-source; conflicts with the Mar/Apr dates]**; and a $5M issuer capital floor **[single-source, unverified]**.

Market structure:

- The House passed **CLARITY** 294–134 in July 2025.
- **Senate cloture failed 49–50 on Sept 15, 2026**. The reported sticking point was ethics language on officials' crypto income, not the SEC/CFTC split ([FinTech Weekly](https://www.fintechweekly.com/news/clarity-act-cloture-vote-fails-49-50-september-2026); [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/10/The-CLARITY-Act-Stalls-in-the-Senate-Whats-Next-for-Digital-Asset-Regulation)). Accounts of which Republicans defected **[conflicting]** differ.

SEC and CFTC, which moved from enforcement to rulemaking:

- The SEC dismissed Coinbase and Binance and issued staff statements on meme coins and protocol staking ([Cozen O'Connor](https://www.cozen.com/news-resources/news/2025/sec-crypto-2-0-roadmap-of-sec-developments-on-cryptocurrency-and-digital-assets-in-2025); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2025/06/sec-staff-concludes-protocol-staking-activities)).
- A **joint SEC–CFTC interpretive release (Mar 17, 2026)** set a five-category token taxonomy. It holds that investment contracts can end ([Ballard Spahr](https://www.ballardspahr.com/insights/alerts-and-articles/2026/03/sec-and-cftc-clarify-when-digital-assets-are-and-are-not-securities)).
- The SEC proposed **Regulation Crypto Assets** on Aug 18, 2026 ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/atkins-statement-regulation-crypto-assets-081826)). Its reported $5M startup exemption, $20M/$75M tiers and blue-sky preemption are **[secondary-source, unverified against rule text]**.
- A five-year **innovation exemption for tokenized NMS stock venues** followed on Sept 17, 2026. It gives no Investment Company Act relief ([Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/sec-s-innovation-exemption-opens-path-tokenized-stock-trading)).
- On Oct 5, 2026 the CFTC issued an ANPRM on leveraged retail crypto. Chair Selig conceded the agency cannot force spot crypto onto CFTC platforms without Congress ([Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/cftc-previews-regulation-ctx-and-cam-federal-charter-crypto-exchanges)).

Banking:

- Federal bank regulators withdrew their crypto prior-approval regimes in March–April 2025 ([Simpson Thacher](https://www.stblaw.com/about-us/publications/view/2025/04/28/federal-banking-agencies-take-steps-to-accommodate-banks-involvement-in-crypto-related-activities)).
- An August 2025 executive order targeted "debanking" ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2025/08/executive-order-on-fair-banking)).
- The OCC conditionally chartered national trust banks for Circle, Ripple, BitGo, Paxos, Fidelity Digital Assets, Bridge, Crypto.com, Protego and Coinbase ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/occ-national-trust-bank-charter-approve-circle-paxos-ripple-bitgo-gould-crypto/807799/)).

Tax and the EU:

- Congress used the CRA to repeal the IRS DeFi broker rule (Apr 10, 2025) ([RSM](https://rsmus.com/insights/tax-alerts/2025/congress-nullifies-irs-crypto-reporting-regulations-for-defi-platforms)).
- MiCA's transitional period ended EU-wide on **July 1, 2026** ([ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-04/ESMA75-113276571-1679_Statement_on_the_end_of_transitional_periods_under_MiCA.pdf)).

**Why it is ambiguous.** Federal crypto law now sits in three layers that do not line up. GENIUS is enacted but not operative. The token taxonomy is interpretive guidance rather than statute. The safe harbor is only proposed. Interpretive releases and exemptive orders can be withdrawn by a future Commission far more easily than a statute can be repealed. Spot "digital commodity" exchanges still have no federal registration path, so they remain state money-transmitter or BitLicense businesses.

Stablecoin "rewards" paid by third parties such as exchanges, as distinct from issuers, are not covered by GENIUS's issuer-level interest ban. The Senate's Tillis–Alsobrooks compromise would have banned rewards "economically or functionally equivalent" to deposit interest while allowing activity-based rewards ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/05/01/clarity-act-text-lets-crypto-firms-offer-stablecoin-rewards-while-shielding-bank-yield)). It died with the cloture vote, so the question is unresolved by statute. Liquid staking and restaking, tokenized funds, and tokens still inside an investment contract also remain open.

**What startups are doing.** Eleven companies applied for federal crypto banking charters within 83 days ([FinTech Weekly](https://www.fintechweekly.com/news/occ-national-trust-bank-charter-crypto-fintech-2026)). Firms preserved rewards programs while the Senate deal was live ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/cryptocurrency/2026/crypto-firms-keep-stablecoin-rewards-under-senate-deal/)). Others are launching state-chartered or non-issuer stablecoin products ahead of the federal rules, building tokenized-stock venues under the September exemption, and planning token launches around the proposed safe harbor.

**Risks and what could close the gap.** The states are the binding constraint. State AGs expanded crypto enforcement as the SEC pulled back. The NY AG pursued unregistered platforms, and California's DFPI brought its first Digital Financial Assets Law action in June 2025 ([Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/01/where-states-jumped-in-when-sec-stepped-back-in-2025-law360)). Reversal risk is high because almost all relief is administrative, and conduct undertaken now can be examined by a future, hostile Commission.

Possible closers, roughly in order of timing:

- final OCC and FDIC GENIUS rules (November 2026 target);
- a lame-duck CLARITY revival after the **Nov 3, 2026** midterms, or reintroduction in January 2027;
- a final Regulation Crypto Assets;
- CFTC final rules for leveraged crypto venues (Regulation CTX/CAM);
- replacement interagency banking guidance.

Whether the OCC charters convert from conditional to final is unclear. Circle's reported final approval on July 10, 2026 is **[single-source, unverified]**.

## Fintech and prediction markets: federal permission collides with state prohibition

**What changed.**

The CFPB shrank but survived:

- The One Big Beautiful Bill Act cut its funding cap from 12% to 6.5% of Fed operating expenses ([GAO](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-108448)).
- Headcount fell from about 1,700 to about 1,300.
- It withdrew **67 guidance documents** ([Covington](https://www.cov.com/-/media/files/corporate/publications/2026/01/funding-haze-and-deregulatory-pursuits-the-cfpb-in-2026.pdf)).
- Courts twice ordered it funded, most recently in a late-September 2026 ruling for 22 state AGs ([Minnesota AG](https://www.ag.state.mn.us/Office/Communications/2026/09/28_CFPB.asp)).

Rules and guidance withdrawn:

- Congress repealed the **digital-payments larger-participant rule** via the CRA ([CRS](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12935)).
- The CFPB withdrew its BNPL interpretive rule ([Kilpatrick Townsend](https://ktslaw.com/en/Blog/Consumer-Financial-Services/2025/4/CFPB-Shifts-Course-on-Buy-Now)).
- A December 2025 advisory opinion declared that **"Covered EWA" is not credit** and that tips and expedite fees are not finance charges ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/12/23/2025-23735/truth-in-lending-regulation-z-non-application-to-earned-wage-access-products)).

Open banking:

- The 2024 **Section 1033 open-banking rule was preliminarily enjoined** on Oct 29, 2025, and the CFPB now calls it unlawful and is rewriting it ([CRS](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF13117.html)).
- JPMorgan began charging aggregators for data in July 2025.
- A revised proposal reportedly went to OIRA on Aug 6, 2026 and reverses the fee ban **[single-source tracker, unverified]** ([Open Banking Tracker](https://openbankingtracker.com/guides/section-1033-status)).

Prediction markets:

- The CFTC withdrew the 2024 proposed ban on political and sports contracts and issued an ANPRM ([CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9194-26)).
- On **June 10, 2026** it proposed replacing categorical bans with a discretionary "public interest determination" framework ([Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/06/rewriting-the-rulebook-cftc-proposes-rule-changes-for-prediction-market-contracts)).

**Why it is ambiguous.** Prediction markets are the clearest example of federal–state collision. The courts of appeals have now split:

- The **Third Circuit** (Apr 6, 2026, New Jersey) found Kalshi likely to win on CEA preemption.
- The **Ninth Circuit** (Aug 28, 2026, Nevada) disagreed.
- The **Sixth Circuit** (late September 2026, Ohio and Tennessee) went further. It held sports event contracts are **not "swaps"** at all, and it vacated a pro-Kalshi injunction in Tennessee. The exact date is reported as Sept 25 or Sept 28 **[conflicting]** ([Sportico](https://www.sportico.com/law/analysis/2026/kalshi-circuit-split-scotus-1234945840/)).
- New Jersey's cert petition (No. 26-299) has backing from **39 states and regulators**, and a Fourth Circuit decision is pending ([RG.org](https://rg.org/news/prediction-markets/39-states-regulators-urge-supreme-court-nj-kalshi-case)).

State enforcement includes **Arizona's 20-count criminal case against Kalshi** (Mar 17, 2026), which includes election-wagering counts ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/03/17/politics/arizona-charges-prediction-market-kalshi)), plus actions in Massachusetts, Kentucky and elsewhere. The Ho-Chunk Nation has filed a tribal-gaming suit ([CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/prediction/news/prediction-market-legal-states/)). A contract can therefore be federally self-certified and a state crime at the same moment, depending on where the user sits.

Other fintech grey zones follow the same federal-off, state-on pattern. Federal EWA relief covers only employer-integrated "Covered EWA." Several states now regulate EWA directly:

- **Maryland bans tips from Oct 1, 2026** ([HR Morning](https://www.hrmorning.com/news/earned-wage-access-maryland-law/)).
- **Indiana caps fees** ([Faegre Drinker](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2025/9/indianas-new-earned-wage-access-ewa-law-what-employers-and-providers-need-to-know)).
- **New York** proposed BNPL licensing rules in July 2026 that largely re-impose the withdrawn federal protections, effective 180 days after adoption ([NY DFS](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2026/07/rp-bank-3nycrr-423-text.pdf); [McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/new-york-proceeds-with-expansive-regulation-of-buy-now-pay-later-products/)).

Bank-fintech models face no settled rule either. The FDIC withdrew its brokered-deposit tightening ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/03/14/2025-04088/unsafe-and-unsound-banking-practices-brokered-deposits-restrictions-guidelines-establishing-standards-for-corporate-governance-and-risk-management-for-covered-institutions-with-total-consolidated-assets-of-10-billion-or-more)). It left its post-Synapse custodial-account recordkeeping proposal pending, at least as of 2025 ([Steptoe](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/fdic-board-of-directors-rolls-back-several-biden-era-rulemaking-actions.html)). FBO-account expectations are therefore set by consent orders rather than a rule.

**What startups are doing.**

- Buying federal status. Polymarket acquired the CFTC-registered QCEX for $112M and then self-certified parlay-style "combinatorial" sports contracts in May 2026 ([Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/264161/polymarket-adds-multi-leg-contracts-as-it-chases-kalshi-sportsbooks/)).
- Suing states pre-emptively, as Kalshi did against Arizona and Polymarket did against Massachusetts.
- Seeking OCC trust charters (Bridge, Crypto.com).
- Signing bilateral paid data deals instead of waiting for 1033, as in JPMorgan–Plaid ([Open Banking Tracker](https://www.openbankingtracker.com/guides/open-banking-data-access-fees)).

**Risks and what could close the gap.** For event contracts, the likely closers are a Supreme Court grant, possible in the October 2026 term, a CFTC final rule under Rule 40.11, or congressional action. The Sixth Circuit's "not swaps" reasoning, if adopted, would remove the CFTC shield entirely. Parlays push the products closer to sportsbooks, which strengthens the states' case.

The sweepstakes-casino story is a warning about how quickly states close a model. About nine states have enacted statutory bans, including **California AB 831 (Jan 1, 2026)**, and **Oklahoma's takes effect Nov 1, 2026**. The ban counts are **[conflicting]** across aggregators ([Gambling.com](https://www.gambling.com/us/sweepstakes-casinos/legal); [Frankfurt Kurnit](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102lrox/california-bans-online-sweepstakes-casinos)).

The CFPB's future depends on three things: the pending en banc D.C. Circuit decision in *NTEU v. Vought*; the House Financial Services Committee's **H.R. 10184**, which would move the bureau onto appropriations ([Consumer Finance Monitor](https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2026/09/22/house-financial-services-committee-approves-legislation-to-place-cfpb-under-congressional-appropriations-process/)); and the pending confirmation of Brian Johnson as director.

## Health and telehealth: the compounding window narrows as a year-end prescribing deadline approaches

**What changed.**

Compounded GLP-1s:

- Once the GLP-1 shortages ended, compounding an "essentially a copy" became unlawful without a documented patient-specific clinical difference.
- FDA sent repeated waves of warning letters: about 100 cease-and-desist letters in the September 2025 DTC-advertising crackdown ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-launches-crackdown-deceptive-drug-advertising)), then **30 letters to telehealth GLP-1 marketers in March 2026** ([Foley](https://www.foley.com/p/102mmr0/glp1-compliance-fda-targets-telehealth-marketing-in-30-new-warning-letters/)) and **25 more in June 2026** ([Sheppard Mullin](https://www.sheppard.com/insights/blogs/fdas-focus-returns-to-compounding-and-telehealth-another-wave-of-warning-letters)).
- On Apr 30, 2026 FDA proposed excluding semaglutide, tirzepatide and liraglutide from the 503B bulks list. No final action has been found ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-proposes-exclude-semaglutide-tirzepatide-and-liraglutide-503b-bulks-list)).
- Novo sued Hims & Hers over patents, then dismissed without prejudice after Hims agreed to stop promoting compounded GLP-1s and to sell branded drugs at cash prices ([Hims 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001773751/000177375126000076/hims-20260331.htm)).
- TrumpRx launched in February 2026, with cash GLP-1 prices from about $350 a month falling toward $250 ([Conference Board](https://www.conference-board.org/research/CED-Newsletters-Alerts/administration-strikes-deal-on-glp1-drug-pricing-and-coverage); [KFF](https://www.kff.org/patient-consumer-protections/trumprx-whats-the-value-for-customers/)).

Telehealth prescribing and Medicare:

- DEA's fourth temporary extension allows tele-prescribing of controlled substances without an in-person visit only **through Dec 31, 2026** ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/12/31/2025-24123/fourth-temporary-extension-of-covid-19-telemedicine-flexibilities-for-prescription-of-controlled)).
- The final Special Registration rule went to OIRA on Aug 25, 2026, and its text is not public ([McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/dea-appears-close-to-finalizing-telemedicine-special-registration/)).
- Medicare telehealth waivers lapsed briefly and were then extended retroactively **through Dec 31, 2027** ([ABA](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/health_law/news/2026/medicare-telehealth-flexibilities-extended-2027/)).

FDA loosened oversight by guidance rather than by rule:

- **January 2026 General Wellness guidance** puts non-invasive wearables estimating blood pressure, SpO2 or glucose under enforcement discretion if their outputs are for wellness only ([Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-20-fda-opens-aperture-for-wearables-in-latest-general-wellness-guidance)). FDA closed its WHOOP warning letter in June 2026.
- **Revised CDS guidance** extends discretion to single-recommendation tools, including some AI ([Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/01/5-key-takeaways-from-fdas-revised-clinical-decision-support-cds-software-guidance)).
- The **LDT rule was vacated** in March 2025, and FDA did not appeal ([Baker Donelson](https://www.bakerdonelson.com/federal-court-vacates-ldt-final-rule)).
- Twelve peptides, including BPC-157, came off "Category 2" in April 2026 ([DJ Holt Law](https://djholtlaw.com/regulatory-alert-the-legal-status-of-bpc-157-in-compounding-and-clinical-practice/)).
- An April 2026 executive order prioritized psychedelics. FDA granted priority vouchers to psilocybin and methylone developers while those drugs remain Schedule I ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/04/24/health/fda-psychedelic-drugs-priority-vouchers)).

**Why it is ambiguous.** The 503A "clinical difference" pathway is still formally open, and FDA's own guidance still permits individualized compounding. Enforcement so far has targeted marketing: implied sameness with approved drugs, and telehealth-branded labels that hide the compounder. FDA has not tested "personalized" compounding at scale. A reported Aug 27, 2026 Fifth Circuit ruling upholding FDA's shortage-end decisions is **[single-source, unverified]** ([Remevi Health](https://remevihealth.com/is-compounded-glp1-legal/)). Warning letters are advisory, not final actions.

The wellness and CDS carve-outs turn on marketing claims and on an undefined "clinically appropriate" standard. Researchers say the guidance leaves validation standards unaddressed ([Recatools summary](https://recatools.com/news/researchers-push-back-on-the-fdas-2026-wellness-device-guidance-what-it-means-for-wearable-owners/)). The peptides are in limbo: no longer prohibited, but not approved, not lawful supplements, and not cleared for 503A compounding. AI mental-health tools face a split among states:

- **outright bans** in Illinois and Nevada (Nevada penalties up to $15,000);
- **disclosure rules** in Utah;
- a 2026 wave in Colorado, Maine, Rhode Island, Tennessee and Vermont **[conflicting tracker counts]** ([Quartz](https://qz.com/state-laws-restricting-ai-mental-health-care-guide-072826); [Becker's](https://www.beckersbehavioralhealth.com/ai-2/5-states-restrict-ai-therapy-chatbots-in-2026/)).

FDA has authorized no generative-AI mental-health device ([Hogan Lovells](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/fdas-digital-health-advisory-committee-weighs-guardrails-for-generative-ai-in-mental-health-devices)). There is therefore no federal clearance path, and no preemption, that a national product could rely on.

**What startups are doing.**

- The scale players are pivoting from compounded GLP-1s to "platform plus branded cash-pay," the Hims model.
- Smaller telehealth brands are rewriting marketing to disclose who compounds the drug.
- Wearables and longevity companies are making wellness-only claims.
- AI mental-health products are rebranding as "coaching," putting a clinician in the loop and geoblocking ban states.
- Peptide clinics are operating in limbo.

**Risks and what could close the gap.** The **Dec 31, 2026 DEA cliff** is the most urgent dated risk in this report. It hits tele-psychiatry, ADHD (Schedule II), testosterone and weight-loss startups, whose compliance depends on rule text no one has seen. The FDA notes recommend DEA registration in every patient state and in-person referral networks as hedges.

For compounding, the closers are a final 503B exclusion, FDA action defining "clinical difference," state pharmacy boards, possible DOJ civil cases, and the falling price of branded drugs, which may do more to close the gap than enforcement does. Lilly is also suing "personalized" marketers ([Health Law Alliance](https://www.healthlawalliance.com/blog/tirzepatide-compounding-enforcement)). The wearable and CDS relief is enforcement discretion and can be withdrawn without notice-and-comment.

## Drones, nuclear, energy and space: pilot status works as de facto licensing

**What changed.** A cluster of 2025 executive orders on drones, supersonic flight, nuclear and space created federal fast lanes. The enabling general rules mostly lag behind.

Drones:

- **Part 108 BVLOS** was proposed in August 2025 and missed its executive-order deadline. FAA sent the final rule to OIRA only on July 10, 2026, and it had not been published as of about Sept 30, 2026 ([PickDrones](https://pickdrones.com/faa-part-108-bvlos-rule-delayed-oira-2026/); [NextMSC](https://www.nextmsc.com/news/faa-part-108-bvlos-rule-nears-finalization-as-utm-market-gains-momentum)).
- In December 2025 the FCC **added all foreign-made drones and critical components to its Covered List**, barring new authorizations. Blue UAS-listed and domestic end products are exempt ([Wiley](https://www.wiley.law/alert-FCC-Issues-Exemptions-Clarifications-for-Sweeping-Prohibition-of-Foreign-Made-Drones)). DJI's challenges remain pending ([DroneLife](https://dronelife.com/2026/02/24/dji-lawsuit-fcc-covered-list/)).
- The FY2026 NDAA extended counter-UAS authority to state and local agencies, and a **July 6, 2026 interim final rule** set up certification ([Federal Register](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-07-06/html/2026-13609.htm)).
- The Pentagon's Drone Dominance program named 25 Phase I vendors in February 2026 ([DefenseScoop](https://defensescoop.com/2026/02/03/drone-dominance-program-hegseth-military-uas-vendors/)). Its volume targets are **[conflicting]**.

Nuclear:

- **Four advanced reactors reached criticality under DOE authorization, not NRC licensing**, by about July 4, 2026 ([DOE](https://www.energy.gov/articles/department-energy-celebrates-fourth-criticality-ahead-july-4th-goal)).
- NRC finalized Part 53 in March 2026 ([ANS](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-03-13/article-7842/nrc-provides-timeline-update-on-rules-meeting-eo-deadline/)).
- On Aug 26, 2026 the Army selected five vendors for its **Janus** microreactor program, worth up to about $2.2B over FY2027 to FY2031 ([POWER](https://powermag.com/u-s-army-mobilizes-2-2b-janus-program-to-deploy-more-than-20-commercial-microreactors)).

Aviation:

- DOT selected eight eVTOL pilot projects across 26 states ([FAA](https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/future-aviation-here-trumps-transportation-secretary-sean-p-duffy-and-faa-unveil-eight)).
- FAA proposed ending the 1973 overland supersonic ban for en route flight meeting a near-"boomless" noise standard ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2026/07/proposed-faa-rule-would-allow-civil-supersonic-flight)).

Energy:

- CEQ's NEPA regulations were rescinded, finally on Jan 8, 2026 ([Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/01/CEQ-Issues-Final-Rule-Eliminating-NEPA-Regulations-Path-to-NEPA-Reform)).
- On data-center power, FERC chose **show-cause orders to all six RTOs** (June 18, 2026) instead of a national large-load rule ([Bracewell](https://www.bracewell.com/resources/ferc-show-cause-orders-data-center-interconnection/)).
- OBBBA requires wind and solar projects that begin construction after July 4, 2026 to be in service by end-2027. It also imposed foreign-entity-of-concern ("FEOC") material-assistance tests ([The Tax Adviser](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2026/feb/navigating-safe-harbor-rules-for-solar-and-wind-sec-48e-facilities.html)).

Autonomous vehicles and space:

- NHTSA is advancing AVs by exemption: a Zoox exemption, and a July 31, 2026 interim final rule letting vehicles built before an exemption is granted qualify ([Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2026/06/the-on-ramp-an-autonomous-connected-and-electric)).
- EO 14335 ordered Commerce to create a "novel space activity" authorization process. No rule was found ([Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2025/10/executive-order-aims-to-accelerate-commercial-space-development-through-deregulation)).

**Why it is ambiguous.** In each sector, operating authority exists for a chosen few before general rules exist for everyone:

- **Drones.** BVLOS still runs on Part 107 waivers. The final Part 108 is disputed, chiefly over drone right-of-way over crewed aircraft that lack ADS-B. Fleets built to the draft could need retrofits.
- **Nuclear.** DOE-authorized criticality is not a commercial license, and no binding NRC rule says how pilot data will count. The Nuclear Innovation Alliance tracker shows several EO 14300 final rules slipping to **Mar 31, 2027**, past the Nov 23, 2026 deadline ([NIA tracker](https://www.nuclearinnovationalliance.org/sites/default/files/2026-09/Public%20Rule%20timeline%209-1.pdf)).
- **Space.** "Novel" in-space activities have no clear federal authorizer, the classic Outer Space Treaty Article VI mission-authorization gap.
- **Energy.** NEPA review now depends on which agency leads. Co-location rules will come out of six separate RTO proceedings. Treasury still has not defined "effective control" for prohibited-foreign-entity status, which Latitude Media called "underwhelming" guidance ([Latitude Media](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/underwhelming-feoc-guidance-punts-on-industrys-key-question/)).
- **Texas.** Texas is ahead under SB 6. The **PUCT's July 24, 2026 Goodnight/Crusoe order** approved a 260 MW co-located data center but held that its emergency curtailment is not capped ([White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/puct-affirms-curtailment-authority-over-co-located-data-centers-first-net-metering)). ERCOT's data on whether this was the "first" such approval **[conflicting]** differs.
- **Notice 2025-42.** A reported court vacatur of the wind/solar beginning-of-construction notice is **[unverified]**.

**What startups are doing.** The pattern is consistent. Companies win pilot or sanctioned status and use it as de facto authority:

- Aalo, Valar, Antares and Deployable Energy reached criticality on the DOE path. Antares and Radiant also won Janus sites.
- Joby, Archer, BETA and others joined eVTOL pilot projects.
- Boom's XB-1 flight data underpins the proposed supersonic noise standard.
- Blue UAS vendors capture demand that the Covered List protects.
- Crusoe pairs data-center load with existing generation in Texas.

Openings also exist for counter-UAS sales to newly authorized local agencies, FEOC supply-chain tracing tools, load-flexibility software, and domestic component makers.

**Risks and what could close the gap.**

- **Final rules that differ from proposals**: Part 108, supersonic, and the remaining NRC rules.
- **Litigation**: DJI v. FCC, the Notice 2025-42 suits, and challenges to agency NEPA procedures.
- **Reliance on reversible instruments.** Many openings rest on executive orders and interim final rules: the counter-UAS IFR, the NHTSA IFR, the CEQ rescission. These can be undone after 2028. Statutory anchors are sturdier: the NDAA counter-UAS provisions, OBBBA, and Part 53 under NEIMA.
- **Sourcing scrutiny**: the Joby–Archer dispute over Chinese parts suggests FEOC-style sourcing scrutiny is spreading into advanced air mobility ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3100766)).
- **Small first orders.** Defense "gauntlet" buys of about $150M spread across many vendors mean follow-on production funding is the real risk.

## Privacy, labor, hemp and the rest: tailwinds, patchworks and one hard cliff

**What changed.**

Privacy and kids' safety:

- Indiana, Kentucky and Rhode Island privacy laws took effect Jan 1, 2026. **Rhode Island has no cure period** ([Koley Jessen](https://www.koleyjessen.com/insights/publications/new-state-privacy-laws-effective-january-1-2026-indiana-kentucky-and-rhode-island)).
- California's risk-assessment, audit and ADMT regulations began phasing in. The exact ADMT compliance date is **[conflicting]** ([O'Melveny](https://omm.com/insights/alerts-publications/2026-data-security-and-privacy-compliance-checklist-key-us-state-law-updates-ai-rules-coppa-changes-and-global-data-protection-risks)).
- The amended COPPA Rule required full compliance by Apr 22, 2026 and now covers biometrics ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule)).
- The Fifth Circuit **stayed the injunction against Texas's App Store Accountability Act**, so the law is in effect while litigation continues ([FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/comparing-enacted-app-store-accountability-acts/)).
- The GOP **SECURE Data Act** would preempt all state privacy laws but lacks bipartisan support ([IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/secure-data-act-analysis-of-the-new-federal-privacy-bill)).
- The EU's GDPR Digital Omnibus remains a proposal. It would allow AI training under legitimate interest and set a 96-hour breach notice, and EU data protection authorities oppose its narrower definition of personal data ([Bird & Bird](https://cm.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/introduction-to-the-the-european-commission’s-digital-omnibus-package)).

Labor:

- DOL proposed returning to a 2021-style independent-contractor test built on control and opportunity for profit or loss. It is not final ([Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/03/dol-proposes-to-rescind-2024-independent-contractor-rule-and-reinstate-2021-framework)).
- The FTC dropped its non-compete ban appeals and will proceed case by case ([Paul Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/ftc-shifts-approach-to-non-compete-enforcement)).
- The NLRB regained a quorum ([Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/new-year-new-board-nlrb-nominations-confirmed-board-regains-quorum)). *Trump v. Slaughter* overruled *Humphrey's Executor*, weakening the Board's independence ([Barnes & Thornburg](https://laborandemployment.btlaw.com/post/102n7k9/trump-v-slaughter-what-does-it-mean-for-the-nlrb)).
- Portable-benefits safe-harbor laws spread to Utah, Tennessee, Alabama and Georgia ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/daily-labor-report/doordash-backed-policy-on-driver-benefits-spreads-across-states)).

Hemp and cannabis:

- The FY2026 appropriations law redefined hemp. It bans most intoxicating hemp-THC products, capping finished goods at **0.4 mg total THC per container** ([MJBizDaily](https://mjbizdaily.com/ohio-will-allow-hemp-thc-drinks-amid-federal-ban-with-conditions/)).
- A September stopgap pushed the effective date to **Dec 11, 2026**, except for synthetics. The administration says there will be no further extensions ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/ajherrington/2026/09/03/trump-signs-bill-delaying-hemp-thc-ban-until-december/); [Cannabis Law Blog](https://www.cannabis-law-blog.com/federal-intoxicating-hemp-ban-changes-pushed-back-until-december-11-2026/)).
- DEA moved **only medical and FDA-approved marijuana to Schedule III** (Apr 22, 2026). Broader rescheduling awaits an ALJ recommendation ([Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/09/high-time-for-a-change-post-dea-hearing-broader-marijuana-rescheduling-questions-remain)).

Cross-cutting tailwinds:

- The **QSBS expansion**: a $15M cap, a $75M asset ceiling, and partial exclusion at 3 and 4 years ([Kutak Rock](https://www.kutakrock.com/newspublications/publications/2025/july/changes-to-irc-section-1202-under-obbba)).
- **CTA reporting** was removed for domestic companies ([Greenberg Traurig](https://gtlaw.com/en/insights/2025/3/cta-update-fincen-issues-interim-final-rule-exempting-domestic-companies-and-us-beneficial-owners-from-reporting-requirements)).
- The **FTC click-to-cancel rule** was vacated ([Alston & Bird](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2025/07/ftc-click-to-cancel-rule-vacated)).
- The SEC proposed credential-based paths to accredited-investor status around Sept 30, 2026 ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/ja/insights/publications/2026/10/proposed-expansions-to-the-accredited-investor-definition-for-natural-persons)).

Cross-cutting headwinds:

- **De minimis duty-free treatment ended** in August 2025 and was not restored after the Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs in February 2026. OBBBA codifies its end for July 1, 2027 ([Thomson Reuters](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/posts/corporates/sunset-of-de-minimis/)).
- The **$100,000 H-1B fee** was upheld in district court and is on expedited appeal ([Virginia Lawyers Weekly](https://valawyersweekly.com/2026/02/03/federal-court-upholds-100000-h-1b-visa-fee-pending-appeal/)).

**Why it is ambiguous.**

- **Worker classification.** A contractor who passes the proposed federal test can still be an employee under California's ABC test. Portable-benefits contributions are a safe harbor only in states with such a statute and could be read as evidence of employment elsewhere.
- **Vacated federal rules.** A vacated rule does not end the obligation. State auto-renewal laws and FTC case-by-case enforcement on non-competes and subscriptions continue.
- **Age verification.** Courts disagree on the level of scrutiny. The district court applied strict scrutiny and the Fifth Circuit intermediate, so age-gating laws are more likely to survive in some circuits than others.
- **Hemp.** Hemp has no ambiguity left on timing, only on whether Congress will rescue it. As of Sept 30, no bill to regulate rather than ban had received a hearing ([Hytiva](https://www.hytiva.com/news/congress-delays-federal-hemp-thc-ban-to-december-11-as-industry-presses-for-rules)).
- **Partial rescheduling.** Partial Schedule III leaves dual-license (medical plus adult-use) operators unsure whether they get 280E tax relief.

**What startups are doing.**

- Building privacy programs to the strictest common denominator (CA plus CO/CT plus RI) and consuming the app-store age signals that Texas now requires.
- Shifting 1099-heavy models to state-by-state compliance and lobbying for portable-benefits laws.
- In hemp: reformulating to microdose or CBD-dominant products, moving into licensed state cannabis channels or state-run hemp-THC markets, or running down inventory.
- In e-commerce: moving to bonded US warehousing and bulk import to replace de minimis.
- In hiring: using O-1 visas and offshore hiring or employer-of-record services instead of H-1B.

**Risks and what could close the gap.**

- **Hemp** is the clearest cliff. The only closers are a regulatory framework attached to the **Dec 11** stopgap or FY2027 appropriations. Absent one, intoxicating hemp-THC products lose federal legality, and payment and ad platforms are likely to pull back before the deadline.
- **Federal privacy preemption** looks unlikely before 2027.
- **A final DOL contractor rule** would be litigated.
- **The H-1B fee** depends on the D.C. Circuit.

The most attractive combination of high opportunity, real durability and low closing risk sits in **sanctioned physical-economy niches**: domestic drones, nuclear pilots, energy co-location and tokenized securities. In each, a statute, a long-dated order or a national-security consensus props up the gap. The highest-upside consumer gaps (event contracts, stablecoin rewards, AI companions, compounded drugs) all carry high closing risk, because states, private plaintiffs or incumbents with standing are actively litigating them.

## Conclusion

The core finding is that in 2025–26, "deregulation" did not create permission. It moved the regulator. Each time a federal agency stepped back, through a withdrawn guidance document, a dropped case, an executive order against state law, or a proposal left unfinalized, some other actor stepped in. Sometimes that was a state AG, sometimes a private plaintiff, sometimes a federal court of appeals or an incumbent with a patent. The startups best placed are not the ones exploiting the absence of rules. They are the ones that secured an affirmative federal status (a charter, a registered exchange, a pilot slot, a Blue UAS listing, an exemption order) and can argue it preempts or outranks state action. Even that shield is being tested, most visibly in the Sixth Circuit's holding that sports contracts are not swaps.

Two implications follow. First, the calendar matters more than the politics. A cluster of hard dates between November 2026 and January 2027 will convert several grey zones into black-and-white rules within one quarter: the hemp ban, the DEA cliff, the GENIUS effective date, the RAISE Act and the reported Colorado rewrite, and the EU watermarking duty. Second, nearly all of the relief rests on instruments that a future administration can reverse without Congress, while enforcement limitation periods outlast the current political window. Founders should plan as if today's conduct will be judged by tomorrow's regulators. The founders in the strongest position are pricing in that reversal risk now rather than assuming that federal pressure for preemption will hold.
