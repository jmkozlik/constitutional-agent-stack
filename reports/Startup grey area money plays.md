# Sell Compliance Shovels, Not Regulatory Bets

For a new startup or solo operator, most of the money in the 2025 to October 2026 US policy shifts sits beside the grey zones, not inside them. The best-funded grey zones are already crowded or about to close: prediction-market venues, stablecoin issuance, compounded GLP-1s and intoxicating hemp. A small entrant does better selling services and software to the businesses, households and agencies that now have to comply with a new rule, claim a new benefit, or buy under a new program. The openings fall into three groups:

- **Permanent tax provisions in the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA)** that create recurring paid work: cost segregation built on permanent 100% bonus depreciation, QSBS structuring, Opportunity Zone 2.0 fund formation, HSA-compatible direct primary care, and a federal scholarship credit starting Jan 1, 2027.
- **Dated compliance cliffs** that force buyers to spend within the next three to fifteen months: hemp on Dec 11, the EU AI-marking deadline on Dec 2, DEA telehealth on Dec 31, California's automated decision-making (ADMT) rules on Jan 1, 2027, and the GENIUS Act by Jan 18, 2027.
- **Government-funded procurement** in drones, counter-drone systems, nuclear and data-center power. Here a newcomer's realistic role is supplier, consultant or grant writer, not prime contractor.

**Capital needed:**

- **Under $10k:** expertise-led consulting (grid interconnection, SBIR and grant writing, DEA compliance, NQA-1 nuclear quality) and thin software tools.
- **$10k to $250k:** cost segregation, physician-owned direct primary care, Texas ESA-funded microschools, wildfire-mitigation inspections and licensed insurance agencies.
- **$250k and up:** powered land for data centers, NDAA-compliant drone components and NQA-1-certified machine shops.

Read every capital estimate, window length and "money potential" rating as my inference unless a citation sits next to it. The underlying research leaned on law-firm alerts, trade press and some vendor blogs, and a few figures rest on one source. *This report is informational research, not legal, tax or financial advice. Confirm any rule, deadline or figure against the statute, the Federal Register or a licensed professional before acting.*

## Three filters separate durable openings from traps

**Filter 1: what kind of rule creates the opening.** The research points to one rule for judging any opening: the legal instrument decides how long the money lasts.

- **Statutes last longest.** OBBBA's permanent provisions (bonus depreciation, QSBS, Opportunity Zones, DPC/HSA), the FY26 NDAA and the SBIR reauthorization through 2031 create multi-year service markets that a future administration cannot easily undo.
- **Agency guidance and enforcement discretion are fragile.** FDA's wellness and clinical-decision-support (CDS) carve-outs, the SEC's tokenization exemption and the CFPB's earned-wage-access (EWA) opinion can be withdrawn, and some without notice and comment.
- **Hard cliffs are short sales windows.** Hemp, the DEA telehealth deadline and EU marking are worth selling into right now and worthless soon after.

**Filter 2: whether regulation is your moat or your competitor's.** It is a moat for you only if you can cheaply obtain the credential, such as a licensed insurance agency, an NQA-1 quality program or an FFL/SOT firearms license. It is a moat against you when it costs seven figures. A 50-state money-transmitter footprint costs about **$1.1M to $1.5M and one to two years** ([Faisal Khan, vendor](https://faisalkhan.com/solutions/licensing/money-transmitter-license-mtl/money-transmitter-license-cost/)), and broker-dealers, DCM exchanges and OCC charters cost similar sums.

**Filter 3: whether a platform will absorb the opening.** Payroll vendors are already folding in tips-and-overtime reporting. Apple and Google are becoming the source of age signals. Polymarket bought the API startup Dome and Kalshi absorbed Oddpool ([The Block](https://theblock.co/post/390546/polymarket-buys-fresh-prediction-market-api-startup-dome-marking-second-official-acquisition); [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/149005-oddpool-raises-3m-seed-then-joins-kalshi)). Where a platform will absorb the opening, plan for an acquisition or a niche the platform will not bother to serve, not for an independent company.

**Flags used below.** **[inferred]** marks my own estimates, mainly capital, windows and money potential. **[single-source]** marks figures resting on one source. **[vendor]** marks figures from a company selling the service. **[conflicting]** marks points where sources disagree.

## OBBBA turns tax provisions into recurring service markets

The tax openings suit most profiles because they are **permanent or multi-year**, the buyers already have money, and the product is often a document: a study, a filing or an attestation.

**Cost segregation is the most accessible durable play.** OBBBA permanently restored **100% first-year bonus depreciation** for property acquired after Jan 19, 2025 ([Citizens Private Banking](https://www.citizensbank.com/private-banking/insights/obbba-cre-tax-incentives.aspx)). It also covers real-estate improvements with recovery periods of 20 years or less ([CBIZ](https://www.cbiz.com/insights/article/questions-and-answers-breaking-down-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-act)). Trade press reported cost-segregation demand "surging" in 2026 ([BreakingAC](https://breakingac.com/news/2026/apr/17/with-100-bonus-depreciation-now-permanent-cost-segregation-demand-surges-in-2026/)).

- **Who pays:** short-term-rental investors and small landlords. Studies cost **$3,000 to $15,000**, with most STRs at $4,000 to $8,000 **[vendor]** ([bnbcalc](https://www.bnbcalc.com/blog/real-estate-tax/rental-income-tax/cost-segregation-to-maximize-bonus-depreciation)).
- **Capital:** about $10k to $50k for software, an engineering or estimating partner, and CPA referral channels **[inferred]**.
- **Your edge:** cheap "desk-based" studies for properties under $1M, sold through STR communities.
- **Risks:** IRS scrutiny of STR material-participation claims, and liability for poorly supported allocations.

**The Section 174A R&D rush is over, but the annual work remains.** Small businesses with gross receipts of $31M or less could apply domestic R&E expensing retroactively to 2022 ([Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.com/insights/alerts/tax/2025/flash/irs-issues-procedural-guidance)). That election window closed in **early July 2026** (July 3 under [Rev. Proc. 2025-28](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-28.pdf), July 6 per [CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/05/05/the-rd-tax-credit-window-is-closing-what-cpas-must-do-before-july-6-and-what-comes-next/182683/)) **[conflicting by days; both passed]**. The ongoing market is annual §41 credit studies and modeling the interactions among 174A, §59(e), CAMT and §163(j). The Tax Adviser notes that immediate expensing "might not be the optimal treatment" for some taxpayers ([The Tax Adviser](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2026/may/practical-sec-174a-tax-planning-in-the-post-obbba-landscape/)). That complexity is what makes it billable. It is a crowded category, so a newcomer should sell to CPA firms as white-label software or a back-office service.

**QSBS changes reward documentation and structuring.** For stock issued after July 4, 2025:

- The exclusion cap rises to **$15M**, or 10x basis ([Dickinson Wright](https://www.dickinson-wright.com/news-alerts/client-alert-federici-obbb-expands-tax-incentives)).
- The gross-asset test rises to **$75M** ([Spencer Fane](https://www.spencerfane.com/insight/the-one-big-beautiful-bill-act-significantly-expands-qualified-small-business-stock-benefits/)).
- The exclusion is tiered: 50% after three years, 75% after four, 100% after five ([Lowenstein](https://www.lowenstein.com/news-insights/publications/client-alerts/one-big-beautiful-bill-and-opportunities-to-avoid-or-defer-tax-on-gains-te)).

Founders and funds now need a contemporaneous record of the asset test at each issuance and of holding periods across three tiers. That supports compliance and attestation SaaS. Carta's cap-table tracking is the obvious incumbent **[inferred]**. Structuring work such as trust "stacking" belongs to licensed attorneys.

**Opportunity Zone 2.0 makes Q4 2026 a build window for fund formation.** The new 10-year zone maps take effect **Jan 1, 2027** ([CA GO-Biz](https://business.ca.gov/?p=80605)). Existing zones accept new investment only through Dec 31, 2026 ([Duane Morris](https://blogs.duanemorris.com/opportunityzones/)). The eligibility threshold falls from 80% to 70% of area median income, which shrinks the eligible tract pool by about 25% ([NADO](https://www.nado.org/opportunity-zones-2-0-resource-guide/)).

- **Plays:** single-asset qualified opportunity funds for 2027 deals, fund-admin and compliance SaaS, and map data comparing old and new zones.
- **Capital:** fund legal costs of roughly $25k to $100k+ per fund **[inferred]**.
- **Uncertainty:** the governor-designation deadline (July 1 vs. Sept 29/30, 2026) is **[conflicting]**, and rural-fund specifics were not confirmed.

**The federal scholarship credit and the Texas ESA create a 2026–27 education rush.** From **Jan 1, 2027**, donors get a dollar-for-dollar federal credit of up to **$1,700** for gifts to scholarship-granting organizations (SGOs) ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/government-entities/federal-state-local-governments/federal-scholarship-tax-credit-fstc)). As of Oct 8, **31 states had opted in and 6 had opted out** ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/State_participation_in_the_Federal_Scholarship_Tax_Credit)). Texas's $1B education savings account program drew **101,000+ applications** for about 90k–100k funded slots, paying **$10,474 per private-school student** and up to $30,000 for students with disabilities ([Community Impact](https://communityimpact.com/south-central-austin/texas-legislature/applications-for-texas-education-savings-accounts-set-to-exceed-available-funding/)).

The for-profit plays are:

- **SGO back-office software:** donor receipts, IRS reporting and eligibility checks. SGOs must be listed before 2027 donations start.
- **Microschools and tutoring as ESA vendors:** roughly $20k to $150k to open **[inferred]**.
- **529-payment layers for tutors:** the K-12 529 limit is now **$20,000 per year** **[single-source, secondary]** ([beancount.io](https://beancount.io/it/blog/2026/09/15/529-plans-2026-expansion-20000-k12-tutoring-apprenticeship-guide)).

**Risks:** opt-in is yearly and political, I did not read the SGO fee rules, and Texas pays ESA funds on a delay.

**Trump Accounts, tips/overtime and other provisions open narrower lanes.**

- **Trump Accounts.** These launched July 4, 2026 with a **$1,000 seed** for children born 2025–2028, and **60M+ children** were auto-enrolled ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/01/treasury-trump-accounts-auto-enroll-more-than-60-million-children.html)). BNY and Robinhood hold the core rails ([Robinhood](https://robinhood.com/us/en/newsroom/robinhood-tapped-as-brokerage-for-trump-accounts-together-with-bny)). Commonwealth estimates about **$2.88B of seed money could go unclaimed** among lower-income families (via CNBC). That makes claim-assistance outreach funded by philanthropy, hospitals or credit unions a low-capital, mission-adjacent business. The risk is that the Treasury app makes intermediaries unnecessary.
- **No tax on tips and overtime.** These deductions run through tax year 2028. Draft 2026 W-2s add **Box 12 codes TP and TT** and an occupation code ([NATP](https://www.natptax.com/news-insights/blog/draft-2026-form-w-2-adds-codes-for-tips-overtime-and-occupation-data/)). The overtime deduction is capped at $12,500 ($25,000 joint) ([Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/insights/get-pdf/29677)). Toast and the payroll giants will absorb employer reporting ([Toast](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Toast-Payroll-No-Tax-on-Tips-and-or-Overtime?lang=en_US)). The gap they leave is 1099 rideshare and delivery workers who must track their own qualified tips, which suits a cheap consumer app or a tax-preparer lead funnel. The window is about three filing seasons **[inferred]**.
- **Suppressors and short-barreled rifles.** The NFA tax on these fell to **$0** on Jan 1, 2026. ATF took **150,000+ eForms** on day one ([Pew Pew Tactical](https://pewpewtactical.com/atf-struggles-150000-eform-submissions-day-1/)), and June NFA background checks rose **177%** year over year ([NSSF](https://www.nssf.org/articles/if-you-havent-heard-suppressor-sales-are-booming/)). FFL/SOT dealers, NFA trust and eForm filing services, and suppressor machining all benefit. The January land-grab is over, and payment-processor de-risking is a real risk.
- **Remittances.** A **1% excise on cash-funded transfers** from 2026 ([Accounting Today](https://www.accountingtoday.com/list/obbba-changes-to-keep-an-eye-on-for-2026)) favors digital remitters that move cash-dependent customers onto bank accounts.
- **Gambling losses.** The deduction cap falls to **90% of losses** ([Barnes Dennig](https://www.barnesdennig.com/obbba-major-changes-to-gambling-loss-deductions/)), which creates a niche for tax-tracking tools for heavy bettors. Repeal bills are pending.

## Deep tech pays suppliers and consultants more than primes

**Drone protectionism creates paid work at both ends of the fleet lifecycle.** Several rules now shape the market:

- DJI has been on the FCC Covered List since Dec 22, 2025, but existing units can still fly. Firmware updates for previously authorized aircraft are protected until at least **Jan 1, 2029** ([DroneXL](https://dronexl.co/es/dji/)).
- FCC 26-50 reportedly blocks new authorizations for drones containing covered flight controllers, radios, camera boards and gimbals from **Oct 13, 2026** ([Camera Jabber](https://camerajabber.com/?p=1424571)) **[single-source]**.
- Section 232 tariffs of 25% to 100% on drones and components began Sept 3 **[single-source]** ([DroneXL](https://dronexl.co/es/dji/)).

Together these keep installed DJI fleets flying longer while squeezing their spare parts. The cheapest play is **fleet sustainment**: repair depots, refurbished batteries, brokering already-authorized parts, and "fleet transition audits" for ag co-ops, inspectors and police. It needs roughly $50k to $500k **[inferred]** and lasts to about 2029. DroneXL argues parts and repair, not a kill switch, are the real medium-term risk for owners, but no shortage data exists.

At the other end, Drone Dominance Phase II buys about **30,000 drones at fixed prices of $3,250 to $4,500**, within a program of about **$1.1B** over two years ([sUAS News](https://www.suasnews.com/2026/04/department-of-war-launches-phase-two-of-1-1bn-drone-dominance-programme/)). Vendors are cut from 12 to 5 as unit prices fall toward about **$2,300** ([Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/air/2026/02/03/pentagon-taps-25-firms-for-small-cheap-attack-drone-competition/)). That is a margin tournament for well-funded airframe makers. The safer seat is supplying NDAA-compliant subcomponents to the finalists, at roughly $1M to $10M of capital **[inferred]**. One competitor is the Army's own Tobyhanna motor line ([DCMA](https://www.dcma.mil/News/Article/4560260/army-made-motors-power-historic-game-of-drones-flight/)). Another is Hoverfly, which cites a **~$5.9B** US component market **[vendor]** ([DroneXL](https://dronexl.co/2026/07/04/hoverfly-launches-ndaa-compliant-drone-components/)).

**Counter-drone money is grant-funded and gated by training.** Several sources of public money are flowing:

- FEMA's FY2026 counter-UAS grant runs through state administrative agencies in 11 states, including TX, FL, NY and CA ([Missouri DPS NOFO](https://dps.mo.gov/dir/programs/ohs/grantstraining/documents/fy-2026-c-uas-notice-of-funding-opportunity.pdf)).
- Half of a **$500M** OBBBA counter-drone appropriation has gone to host jurisdictions ([CRS](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-07-29_IF13279_997b9410e5f148a36c703f89b6d55be3e29ac43c.pdf)).
- NYPD alone spent **$6.5M** ([Robotics.press](https://robotics.press/news/nypd-6-5m-counter-drone-unit-safer-skies/)).
- The July 2026 DOJ/DHS interim final rule makes training and certification a precondition for local mitigation authority ([Federal Register](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-07-06/html/2026-13609.htm)).

For a small entrant that points to three plays: grant-writing and procurement help for local agencies, operator training, and reselling detection-only systems on state contract vehicles. DroneShield, for example, sits on a Texas statewide vehicle ([Unmanned Airspace](https://www.unmannedairspace.info/counter-uas-systems-and-policies/state-of-texas-signs-five-year-framework-agreement-with-drone-shield-for-counter-drone-technology/)). The risk is that a federally designated curriculum captures the training market.

**Defense procurement reform rewards proposal writers.** SBIR/STTR lapsed for six months and was reauthorized **through Sept 30, 2031** on Apr 13, 2026. The reauthorization added a "strategic breakthrough" Phase II of **up to $30M** and new foreign-risk screening ([Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/sbirsttr-programs-reauthorized-after-six-month-lapse)). The FY26 NDAA and the November 2025 Hegseth memos push commercial and dual-use buying through portfolio acquisition executives ([Wiley](https://www.wiley.law/newsletter-Secretary-Hegseth-Issues-Directives-to-Transform-Acquisition-Process-and-Defense-Industrial-Base)). For someone without a product, the money lies in "picks and shovels":

- SBIR and OTA proposal and capture writing;
- CMMC and foreign-risk compliance help;
- NDAA, Blue UAS and FEOC supply-chain provenance tooling.

FEOC, the "foreign entity of concern" rules, now gates energy tax credits through "material assistance" tests, and Treasury has still not defined "effective control" ([Latitude Media](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/underwhelming-feoc-guidance-punts-on-industrys-key-question/)). That uncertainty is why provenance tracing sells. These businesses need almost no capital beyond expertise **[inferred]**.

**Nuclear's bottleneck is certified suppliers, not reactors.** The Army's **Janus** program picked five microreactor vendors on Aug 26, 2026 and targets a first reactor by Sept 2028 ([DefenseScoop](https://defensescoop.com/2026/08/26/army-annouces-initiial-bases-to-host-nuclear-microreactors/)). An August 2026 NEI report cites a shortage of **NQA-1-qualified suppliers**, the nuclear quality-assurance standard, because maintaining NQA-1 programs is expensive ([Neutron Bytes](https://neutronbytes.com/2026/08/24/the-reactor-isnt-the-bottleneck-the-forge-is/)). The large-forging list has no US firm on it. That shortage supports several businesses:

- **Under $10k for an experienced quality engineer [inferred]:** NQA-1 program setup and audit consulting, nondestructive examination (NDE) and materials testing, licensing documentation, and reactor-operator training.
- **Mid-capital [inferred]:** an existing precision machine shop earning NQA-1 certification over 12 to 24 months.

Fuel, enrichment and deconversion are for well-funded companies only. The main risk is customer concentration: few reactors will sell power before 2028.

**Data-center power makes "time-to-power" the product.** Texas SB 6 draft rules for loads of 75 MW or more set study fees of **$100k to $300k** and security of **$50k/MW**, about 80% of it forfeited if a project shrinks or dies ([Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/03/public-utility-commission-of-texas-issues-proposed-rules-for-large-load-interconn/)). ERCOT's large-load queue stood at about **189 GW** ([PUCT slides](https://mojo.naseo.org/Data/Sites/1/media/tknaseo/sb6-puct-17-oct-2025.pdf)). Announced behind-the-meter gas and fuel-cell capacity for data centers passed **12 GW** by mid-2026, up from under 3 GW ([Datacentres.com](https://www.datacentres.com/news/behind-the-meter-power-gas-turbines-and-fuel-cells-emerge-as-data-centres-escape-slot1-2026-08-19)). Forfeitable security makes pre-screening valuable, which gives small players four ways in:

- **Interconnection and filing consulting:** fee-for-service, minimal capital for experienced power engineers.
- **Powered-land origination:** land plus a gas lateral, water and entitlements, flipped to developers. Capital runs about $0.5M to $5M **[inferred]**. TerraVolt in Idaho is an example ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/terravolt-inks-natural-gas-supply-deal-for-onsite-power-plant-at-planned-data-center-campus-in-idaho/)). Enverus warns that a site "powered by gas" is not the same as buildable ([Enverus](https://www.enverus.com/blog/data-center-sites-powered-by-gas-isnt-the-same-as-ready-to-build/)).
- **Load-flexibility software:** GridCARE unlocked **80+ MW** at Portland General Electric ([APPA](https://www.publicpower.org/periodical/article/oregon-utility-gridcare-complete-project-accelerate-large-load-interconnections-using-flexibility)).
- **Generator rental and O&M:** can start small.

The window runs roughly 2026 to 2028, while queues stay clogged **[inferred]**. The main risk is an AI capex pullback that strands land options.

**Space and advanced air mobility offer licensing work.**

- **Space.** All FAA launch licensing moved to the streamlined Part 450 on Mar 17, 2026 ([FAA](https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/faa-streamlines-commercial-space-license-approvals)), and a further flight-safety-analysis rule is pending. That supports safety-analysis and licensing consultancies.
- **eVTOL.** The eight eVTOL pilot projects span 26 states ([FAA](https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/future-aviation-here-trumps-transportation-secretary-sean-p-duffy-and-faa-unveil-eight)), but vertiport zoning and noise stay local. That is a planning-consultancy and software niche for the 2026 to 2028 test phase **[inferred]**.

## Health and consumer openings reward claim discipline and cash pay

**Direct primary care is the strongest health play for a licensed clinician.** From Jan 1, 2026, a DPC arrangement no longer disqualifies HSA contributions if fees stay at or below **$150 a month for an individual or $300 for a family**, and the fees can be paid from the HSA ([HUB International](https://www.hubinternational.com/hub-resources/employee-benefits-compliance-bulletins/2025/08/direct-primary-care-rules-for-hsa-plans-under-the-obbba); [GoodRx](https://goodrx.com/insurance/fsa-hsa/hsa-one-big-beautiful-bill)). The economics, from the 2026 State of DPC survey and other sources:

- The average fee is **$98.60 a month** ([Hint](https://blog.hint.com/state-of-dpc-2026-key-takeaways-from-the-dpc-alliances-physician-survey)).
- Mature panels run 400 to 700 patients, and **68%** of practices are not yet full ([Medical Economics](https://www.medicaleconomics.com/view/five-surprising-findings-about-the-state-of-direct-primary-care)).
- Employers fund about **60%** of memberships at $55 to $65 per member per month **[secondhand]** ([DPC News](https://dpcnews.com/direct-primary-care-statistics/direct-primary-care-statistic-of-the-week-2/)).
- Startup cost runs from about **$5,000 to $100,000+**, though that figure is from 2020 ([AAFP](https://www.aafp.org/pubs/fpm/blogs/inpractice/entry/dpc-faqs.html)).

At 500 patients and about $80 a month, a solo physician grosses roughly **$480k a year** **[inferred]**. Non-physicians can sell DPC enablement instead:

- membership billing (Hint and Elation are incumbents);
- HSA-compliant "DPC-in-a-box" kits;
- employer DPC networks;
- bundles pairing DPC with bronze HSA plans for the self-employed.

The fee cap rules out premium concierge pricing.

**Cash-pay GLP-1 now earns money from care, not drug margin.** TrumpRx launched Feb 5, 2026 as a referral hub. It lists prices such as **$149** for the Wegovy pill and **$299+** for Zepbound, and GoodRx and Cost Plus handle fulfillment ([pharmaphorum](https://pharmaphorum.com/news/white-houses-trumprx-platform-has-arrived)). Hims & Hers proves the model at scale:

- **2.9M subscribers** and **$753M** of quarterly revenue, up 38% ([Hims 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001773751/000177375126000163/hims-20260630.htm)).
- Gross margin fell to about **64%** from 82% in 2023 **[secondary]** ([Kavout](https://www.kavout.com/market-lens/hims-hers-s-glp-1-revenue-surge-erodes-core-profitability)).

That margin squeeze is the warning. Value now sits in clinical memberships, labs and adherence services built around cheap branded drugs. FDA warning-letter waves against telehealth marketers in March and June 2026 ([Foley](https://www.foley.com/p/102mmr0/glp1-compliance-fda-targets-telehealth-marketing-in-30-new-warning-letters/)) make compounded-drug marketing a declining bet.

**Wellness wearables and clinician AI live on claim discipline.** FDA's Jan 6, 2026 wellness guidance lets non-invasive wearables estimate blood pressure, glucose or SpO2 if they make no diagnostic claims ([Covington](https://www.cov.com/news-and-insights/insights/2026/01/fda-issues-revised-guidance-on-general-wellness-products)). There are limits:

- A glucose estimate qualifies only if it is **contraindicated for people with diabetes or prediabetes** ([King & Spalding](https://www.kslaw.com/insights/articles/fda-updates-general-wellness-and-clinical-decision-support-guidance-documents)), which removes the obvious market.
- FDA closed its WHOOP warning letter in June 2026 after the company modified its product, a sequence lawyers call a "wellness playbook" ([ArentFox Schiff](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/alerts/not-fdas-whoop-ing-boy-how-warning-letter-became-wellness-playbook)).

New hardware costs millions **[inferred]**. The cheap entry is software: wellness analytics on Apple, Oura or Garmin data, or white-label algorithms. On the clinician side, OpenEvidence shows that ad-funded clinical AI can reach about **$150M** annualized revenue **[secondary estimate]** ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/openevidence)). The top of that market is taken, so newcomers should build narrow single-recommendation tools for one specialty, sold per seat. Both carve-outs rest on enforcement discretion and can be withdrawn without notice.

**AI mental-health products must be built for a state-by-state map.** At least four states ban AI-delivered therapy: Illinois, Nevada, Rhode Island and Maine ([Psychology.com](https://psychology.com/ai-therapy/state-bans)). Becker's counts five **[conflicting]**. Illinois also bars licensed clinicians from letting AI interact therapeutically with clients or detect emotions ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/08/new-illinois-law-restricts-use-of-ai-in-mental-health-therapy)). Three models fit:

- a consumer "coaching" subscription that never says "therapy" and geofences the ban states;
- clinician-side tools for documentation and measurement, priced per seat;
- employer and employee-assistance contracts.

The Path raised a **$14.3M** seed for a consumer AI mental-health product ([HLTH](https://hlth.com/insights/news/ai-mental-health-startup-the-path-raises-14-3m)). The window is shrinking as more states copy Illinois. California's SB 243 adds a private right of action for companion bots.

**Psychedelics: training and services pay; retail centers struggle.** The April 18, 2026 executive order and the FDA priority vouchers are real ([Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/04/psychedelics-and-the-executive-order-from-schedule-i-to-treatment-priority/)), but drug approvals are years away. Oregon shows the hard retail economics:

- About **a third of service centers had closed** by January 2026 ([OPB](https://opb.org/article/2026/01/22/think-out-loud-oregon-licensed-psilocybin)).
- The state proposed **doubling the $10k license fee** ([OPB](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/07/16/licensing-fees-could-double-for-oregon-psilocybin-service-centers-and-facilitators/)).
- InnerTrek closed its center and pivoted to training ([Willamette Week](https://www.wweek.com/news/health/2026/06/30/in-change-of-tack-major-portland-psilocybin-service-center-closes/)).

Meanwhile, New Mexico plans to launch its medical program by the end of 2026 and is writing training standards ([Filter](https://filtermag.org/new-mexico-accelerate-psilocybin-access/)). That makes multistate facilitator training the clearest cash generator, at $10k to $250k to set up an approved program **[inferred]**.

**The DEA telehealth deadline and longevity clinics favor compliance sellers.** DEA's telehealth prescribing flexibilities expire **Dec 31, 2026**. The final special-registration rule has sat at OIRA since Aug 25, and its proposed version required several things ([McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/dea-telemedicine-flexibilities-for-controlled-substances-2026/)):

- prescription drug monitoring program (PDMP) checks;
- identity verification;
- Schedule II limits;
- data reporting;
- a separate registration for each patient's state.

Telehealth platforms and multistate practices will pay for registration management, PDMP integration and in-person referral networks. Capital is low, but the urgency fades if DEA issues a fifth extension **[inferred]**. In longevity, Function Health's **$365-a-year** lab membership reached **500k+ members** ([Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/function-450-million-growth-financing-general-catalyst/)), so the subscription model is proven and crowded. BPC-157 and similar peptides remain in "regulatory limbo" despite marketing claims ([DJ Holt Law](https://djholtlaw.com/regulatory-alert-fdas-april-2026-update-on-bpc-157-and-ghk-cu/)), which makes peptide add-ons a legal tail risk.

**Hemp is a liquidation trade with about 60 days left.** From **Dec 11, 2026**, finished hemp products are capped at **0.4 mg total THC per container**, with THCA counted ([KCUR](https://www.kcur.org/environment-agriculture/2026-09-17/hemp-federal-ban-thc-growers)). Advisors recommend "tapered liquidation," reformulation, or a move into state licenses ([HBK CPA](https://hbkcpa.com/insights/federal-hemp-ban-business-guide/)). The buyer-side plays are:

- buying distressed extraction equipment and brands;
- inventory clearing and destruction-compliance services;
- licensing hemp-beverage brands to licensed cannabis operators;
- pivoting to non-intoxicating functional drinks.

All of these are short (to about Q1 2027) and low-capital if brokered rather than bought **[inferred]**. Washington's precedent, where licensed hemp growers fell from 220 to 42 ([Barchart/AP](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/36351501/here-s-what-to-know-about-the-federal-ban-threatening-the-market-for-thc-infused-drinks-and-snacks)), shows consolidation follows.

## Housing, immigration, insurance and rentals turn new rights into paid paperwork

**Upzoning creates a feasibility-report business before it creates buildings.**

- **California SB 79**, which upzones land near transit, took effect **July 1, 2026** ([HCD](https://www.hcd.ca.gov/planning-and-research/sb79-tod)). Los Angeles wants to delay it to 2030 ([Allen Matkins](https://www.allenmatkins.com/real-ideas/the-city-of-los-angeles-seeks-to-delay-implementation-of-senate-bill-79-sb-79-until-2030.html)), and cities are responding with exclusion ordinances and alternative plans ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/tracking-sb-79-implementation-before-the-train-leaves-the-station)).
- **California AB 130**'s CEQA infill exemption is already in use ([Nixon Peabody](https://www.nixonpeabody.com/insights/alerts/2026/06/05/california-ab-130-shapes-ceqa-exemptions-pathways-to-infill-housing)).
- **Texas SB 840** bars large cities from prohibiting multifamily housing in commercial zones, and **SB 15** opens small-lot subdivisions ([Dallas City Hall](https://dallascityhall.com/departments/pnv/Pages/TXSBLanduse2025.aspx)).

The city-by-city confusion is itself the product. Site-feasibility reports for owners near qualifying transit could sell for about $2k to $15k each, on capital under $250k for GIS data and a planner or attorney network **[inferred]**. The window runs about 2026 to 2030. PermitFlow's **$54M** Series B ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2668036)) shows permitting software is venture-crowded. The federal ROAD to Housing Act (law July 11, 2026) removes the manufactured-home chassis requirement once HUD rewrites its rules ([MIT Golub](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/node/52628)). That points to a 2027–2029 window for land-lease communities and dealers **[inferred]**, so today the play is positioning.

**Immigration uncertainty is itself a revenue source.** The weighted H-1B lottery is final and was used for the FY2027 cap ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/12/29/2025-23853/weighted-selection-process-for-registrants-and-petitioners-seeking-to-file-cap-subject-h-1b)). The **$100k fee** is blocked after the First Circuit denied a stay ([SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/appeals-court-blocks-100k-h1b-fee)), while a separate DHS fee rule of about $103k was proposed **[single-source]**. The Gold Card had **one approval** as of April 2026 ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/us/2026/04/23/trump-gold-card/)). Two businesses come out of this:

- **O-1 and EB-2 NIW evidence-building services.** Alma and Boundless are incumbents. Pricing is about $5k to $25k per case **[inferred]**, and the work must run through licensed attorneys to avoid unauthorized practice of law.
- **H-1B wage-level and odds-modeling SaaS for employers.**

Gold Card concierge is a weak market.

**Insurance gaps favor licensed agencies and inspectors, not new carriers.** California now lets insurers use forward-looking catastrophe models if they write at least **85%** of their statewide share in distressed areas ([Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/west/2026/09/10/884417.htm)). The FAIR Plan held **696,562** policies in June 2026 (same source), and its rate increase is **[conflicting]** at 29% to 36%. Three small-operator plays follow:

- **Mitigation inspection and certification** for defensible space, vents and roofs, which priced risk now rewards **[inferred]**.
- **Parametric "gap" wraps sold alongside FAIR Plan policies through a licensed agency.** Arbol and Kettle supply capacity ([Arbol](https://www.arbol.io/post/arbol-launches-new-wildfire-parametric-insurance-product-to-fill-growing-coverage-gap-in-the-western-u-s)), and RockRose raised **$12.5M** for mitigation-linked brokerage ([FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/08/20/rockrose-risk-lands-12-5m-series-a-for-wildfire-cover/)).
- **Independent agencies in Florida** capturing policyholders leaving Citizens, which has shrunk to about **270k policies** from 1.4M ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/property/citizens-cuts-policy-count-to-270000-amid-nonrenewal-push-588731.aspx)).

**Short-term-rental bans pushed operators into mid-term rentals.** NYC's Local Law 18 cut outer-borough listings by **90%+** ([QNS](https://qns.com/2026/09/local-law-18-queens-lose-100-million-income/)). Furnished Finder's CEO says investors are moving to 30+ night stays for traveling nurses and corporate tenants ([Afford Anything](https://affordanything.com/?p=14345)).

- **Model:** mid-term rental arbitrage at about $10k to $30k of furnishing per unit **[inferred]**.
- **Risks:** tenant-protection exposure for occupants staying 30+ days, and lease bans on subletting.
- **Adjacent plays:** host-compliance and registration tools in partially restricted cities.

**Corporate and capital-markets shifts mostly favor incumbents.**

- **DExit.** About **49** Delaware public companies proposed leaving between Jan 2024 and Mar 2026 ([Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/05/dexit-one-year-later-an-assessment-of-sb21-the-continuing-pace-of-reincorporation-and-the-maturation-of-texas-as-a-corporate-domicile/)), mostly work for law firms.
- **Private assets in 401(k)s.** DOL's proposed fiduciary safe harbor ([DOL](https://www.dol.gov/index.php/newsroom/releases/ebsa/ebsa20260330)) creates B2B demand for "process" documentation tooling, but sales cycles are long.
- **Accredited investors.** The SEC's Sept 30, 2026 proposal would add credential and exam routes ([American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/sec-rule-would-let-investors-take-exam-to-qualify-as-accredited)). That could create a Series 65-style test-prep business, but only if adopted, likely 2027 at the earliest **[inferred]**.
- **Secondaries.** Schwab–Forge (~$660M) and Morgan Stanley–EquityZen ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/schwab-forge-global-acquisition-drives-firm-further-into-private-markets)) show secondary marketplaces consolidating into wirehouses.

## Compliance and fintech tooling beat licensed venues for newcomers

**AI compliance has dated deadlines but a funded field.** Comp AI raised **$34M** ([MSSP Alert](https://www.msspalert.com/brief/comp-ai-raises-34-million-to-advance-ai-powered-compliance-software)) and Geordie AI **$30M** ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/05/28/geordie-security-governance-ai-agents/)). Credo AI and Holistic AI sell only on custom quotes ([Decryption Digest](https://www.decryptiondigest.com/blog/eu-ai-act-compliance-platforms-credo-ai-vs-holistic-ai-vs-fairly-ai)). EU Article 50 requires machine-readable marking of generative outputs by **Dec 2, 2026** for systems already on the market, using at least two techniques ([InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/08/eu-ai-content-watermark/)). Frontier providers are already marking their own outputs, though, so a standalone watermark vendor gets squeezed. The open space is in two places:

- **Statute-specific kits.** No vendor surfaced selling an SB 243 crisis-protocol and reporting kit for companion-chatbot startups, which deployers must run themselves ([National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/ai-regulatory-update-californias-sb-243-mandates-companion-ai-safety-and)).
- **Provenance verification on the receiving side.**

Revenue tied to a single US state statute is fragile while federal preemption pressure continues.

**Privacy and age-assurance demand is dated, but identity verification is taken.** California's ADMT opt-out duties begin **Jan 1, 2027**, and the first risk-assessment summaries are due **Apr 1, 2028** ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/01/updates-to-the-ccpa-regulations-what-businesses-need-to-know-now-about-automated-decision-making-cybersecurity-audits-and-risk-assessments)). App-store age laws are live in Texas and Utah, with California's arriving Jan 1, 2027 ([Wiley](https://www.wiley.law/alert-State-App-Store-Accountability-Acts-Introduce-New-Obligations-for-App-Developers)). k-ID, Persona, Incode and Veratad have formed a reusable-age-check network ([Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202601/persona-incode-veratad-join-k-ids-openage-initiative-for-reusable-age-checks)). The gap a newcomer can fill is **developer-side middleware**: consuming operating-system age signals, gating features, capturing parental consent and logging evidence for each state. It is cheap to build, but the window is narrow before platforms bundle it.

**Stablecoins: rent licenses, sell compliance.** Mastercard's purchase of BVNK for up to **$1.8B** ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/03/17/mastercard-agrees-to-purchase-bvnk-for-up-to-usd1-8-billion)) and Bridge's conditional OCC charter ([Payments Dive](https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/stripe-bridge-occ-conditional-approval-national-trust-bank-charter/812425/)) set the exit ceiling. Issuance-as-a-service is crowded: Paxos, Brale, Agora and M0 with Anchorage all offer it. Two plays remain realistic at seed scale **[inferred]**:

- **Regional payment orchestration on licensed partners.** VelaFi raised $20M for Latin America ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/01/12/velafi-raises-usd20-million-in-series-b-to-expand-stablecoin-payments-infrastructure)), and Yellow Card $40M for Africa.
- **GENIUS reserve-attestation and AML software** for small and state-regulated issuers, timed to the **Jan 18, 2027** effective date.

**Tokenized stocks: no venue has launched yet, so build the tooling.** The SEC's Sept 17, 2026 innovation exemption runs five years. It covers only permissioned AMM pools, gives issuers a **30-day objection** period, and offers no Investment Company Act relief ([Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/09/sec-innovation-exemption-establishes)). Superstate (**$82.5M** Series B) and Dinari lead on transfer-agent and broker-dealer infrastructure ([Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/News/2026/02/Superstate-Raises-82-5-Million-Series-B-to-Advance-Tokenized-Investment-Products); [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/08/04/dinari-brings-tokenized-u-s-stocks-to-american-investors-as-equity-race-heats-up)). The tooling niches are seed-scale:

- issuer-objection workflows;
- whitelisting and know-your-customer (KYC) layers;
- corporate-action and dividend data feeds (oracles);
- surveillance for automated market makers (AMMs).

**Prediction markets: data and surveillance, built to be acquired.** A **$35M** fund backed by both the Kalshi and Polymarket CEOs targets data, liquidity and compliance startups ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/03/23/kalshi-polymarket-5cc-capital-prediction-market-fund-raise/)). Dome raised a $4.7M seed and was bought ([The Block](https://theblock.co/post/390546/polymarket-buys-fresh-prediction-market-api-startup-dome-marking-second-official-acquisition)). Polysights raised $1.5M for insider-trading monitoring **[single-source snippet]**. A solo developer can build a cross-venue odds feed or an integrity monitor for very little. The realistic exit is acquisition within 12 to 24 months **[inferred]**. The hazards are changes to platform API terms and state litigation. The courts of appeals are split over whether sports contracts are even swaps ([Sportico](https://www.sportico.com/law/analysis/2026/kalshi-circuit-split-scotus-1234945840/)).

**Open banking is shifting from free data to metered data.** The CFPB's §1033 rewrite went to OIRA Aug 6, 2026 and would allow banks to charge data-access fees ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/legal/bank-regulation/2026/cfpb-open-banking-rewrite-could-put-data-access-fees-at-center-of-section-1033-fight/)). That creates demand for three products:

- routing layers that send each call to the cheapest of Plaid, MX, Finicity and Akoya ([Open Banking Tracker](https://www.openbankingtracker.com/blog/best-open-banking-api-providers-developers-2026));
- monetization platforms for community banks;
- consent ledgers.

**Older non-crypto openings from the earlier research still pay.**

- **Earned wage access.** The CFPB says employer-integrated "Covered EWA" is not credit ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/12/23/2025-23735/truth-in-lending-regulation-z-non-application-to-earned-wage-access-products)). States are going the other way: Maryland bans tips from Oct 1, 2026 ([HR Morning](https://www.hrmorning.com/news/earned-wage-access-maryland-law/)), and Indiana caps fees ([Faegre Drinker](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2025/9/indianas-new-earned-wage-access-ewa-law-what-employers-and-providers-need-to-know)). The opening is state-licensing and fee-compliance tooling, not a new tip-funded consumer app.
- **Portable benefits.** Safe-harbor laws in Utah, Tennessee, Alabama and Georgia ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/daily-labor-report/doordash-backed-policy-on-driver-benefits-spreads-across-states)) create a benefits-account administration niche for gig platforms. In other states the same contributions could be evidence of employment.
- **End of de minimis.** Duty-free low-value imports ended in August 2025, and OBBBA codifies the end for July 1, 2027 ([Thomson Reuters](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/posts/corporates/sunset-of-de-minimis/)). That supports US bonded-warehouse, 3PL and customs-brokerage services for small importers.
- **Subscription rules.** The FTC click-to-cancel rule was vacated ([Alston & Bird](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2025/07/ftc-click-to-cancel-rule-vacated)), but state auto-renewal laws still bind. Subscription-compliance tooling built to California's standard stays relevant.
- **Sweepstakes casinos are the warning.** About nine states have banned them, including Oklahoma from Nov 1, 2026 **[conflicting counts]** ([Gambling.com](https://www.gambling.com/us/sweepstakes-casinos/legal)). States can shut down a grey-zone business model within one or two legislative sessions.

## Best bets by profile

**Software builder.** Start where a deadline forces a purchase and no named vendor yet serves the buyer:

- DEA special-registration workflow software, sold before Dec 31;
- a developer-side age-signal and consent middleware for the California Jan 1, 2027 regime;
- a prediction-market data or integrity tool built with acquisition by Kalshi or Polymarket in mind.

For durability, pick **compliance SaaS on permanent statutes**: QSBS attestation, OZ 2.0 fund administration, SGO back-office, or DPC billing with HSA-compliance logic. These buyers do not disappear when a policy reverses. Avoid standalone watermarking and identity verification, which platforms and funded incumbents are taking.

**Services or sales person.** Two kinds of business fit:

- **Pure expertise, almost no capital:** SBIR/OTA and FEMA counter-UAS grant writing, data-center interconnection pre-screening (if you have power-sector credibility), and O-1 evidence building with partner attorneys.
- **A short licensing step, then a commission base:** a property-insurance agency selling FAIR Plan wraps in California or Citizens takeouts in Florida, or wildfire-mitigation inspection.

Cost segregation is the most repeatable sales-led service. It has a clear $3k to $15k ticket, a permanent tax driver and CPAs as a referral channel. A clinician with a sales instinct should look first at a DPC practice, because the HSA change removes the employer objection.

**Capital allocator.** The best risk-adjusted uses of capital tie to statutes and physical scarcity:

- OZ 2.0 single-asset funds for 2027 deals;
- Texas SB 840 and SB 15 land assembly;
- powered-land options for data centers, with heavy diligence on what is actually buildable;
- acquiring a precision machine shop and taking it through NQA-1 certification;
- mid-term rental portfolios in markets where short-term rentals were banned.

Distressed hemp equipment and brands are a short, opportunistic trade before Dec 11. Treat stablecoin, tokenized-venue and market-making bets as venture-style lottery tickets. Their upside is real (BVNK sold for about $1.8B), but license costs and incumbent bundling make them poor fits for an individual's capital.

## Conclusion

Across every sector studied, the margin has moved from exploiting an ambiguity to resolving it for someone else. Founders who sell certainty earned more of it in 2026 than founders who sold access: Hims's margin fell as it moved to branded drugs, Oregon's psilocybin centers closed while training programs grew, and the API startups were absorbed by the prediction-market platforms. Feasibility reports, attestation software, grant packages, NQA-1 audits and inspection certificates collect fees whether the rule tightens or loosens. They also tend to need the least capital, which suits an individual with unknown resources. An operator who must pick one starting point should choose the cheapest opening that sits on a **statute** and has a **named, paying buyer**. Cost segregation, DPC, SBIR writing and OZ 2.0 work all qualify. Dated cliffs such as DEA and hemp are worth layering on as short campaigns, not as a company.

The second takeaway is about timing. The densest cluster of forcing deadlines in this research falls between **Nov 1, 2026 and Jan 18, 2027**. Someone starting in October 2026 has about one quarter of peak urgency to sell into. After that quarter, the market for these services shifts from emergency work to routine annual compliance: lower prices, but recurring. *Informational research only, not legal, tax or financial advice. Capital estimates, windows and money-potential ratings are inferences, and several cited figures are single-source or vendor-reported as flagged. Verify against primary sources and licensed professionals before committing money.*
